## Translator's flags

- **Parity 3/3.** Psalm-catena — BCP/Coverdale is the target wording where it agrees with Andrewes' Vulgate: Ps 65:2, 55:17, 5:3, 141:2 ("as the incense in thy sight"), 63:7 ("because thou hast been my helper"), 123:2, 91:11, 25:4 ("Shew me thy ways… teach me thy paths"), 119:133 ("no wickedness have dominion over me"), 17:5 ("my footsteps slip not"), 119:132 ("as thou usest to do unto those that love thy Name"), 42:7 ("One deep calleth another"). Kept these verbatim.
- **§9 divergence noted:** `narrabo et annunciabo` (Ps 55:17, Vulg. lit. "I will tell and declare") — the BCP paraphrases it "will I pray, and that instantly"; since Andrewes' Latin tracks the Vulgate verb, I followed the Latin ("will I declare and tell it forth") rather than import the BCP paraphrase, per §9's second clause.
- **Inline Greek glosses kept** (§11): `φιλάνθρωπε` (Tit. 3:4), `πολυεύσπλαγχνος` (Jas. 5:11) — apposed to their Latin phrases, not dropped.
- `Adjuva Tu impœnitentiam meam` (271) deliberately rewrites Mark 9:24 (Vulg. *adjuva incredulitatem meam*, "help thou mine unbelief") → **impenitence** for unbelief; kept the Markan cadence ("Help Thou mine impenitence") and flagged the swap — Andrewes' variation is the point (§9).
- `Abyssus miseriæ nostræ abyssum misericordiæ tuæ` (272): Andrewes coins an antithetical echo of Ps 42:7 (the verb *invocat* carries over) — "the deep of our misery [calleth] the deep of Thy mercy." Rendered elliptically to match the Latin's own ellipsis.
- `peccatorum { primo / miserrimo }` (272): brace holds the 1 Tim 1:15 tag (*chief of sinners*) against Andrewes' superlative *miserrimo* — "the chief / the most wretched."
- `Superexaltet misericordia Tua justitiam Tuam` (272) = Jas 2:13 (*superexultat misericordia judicio*, "mercy rejoiceth against judgment"); rendered "triumph over" to keep the agonistic sense.
- `appello a solio… ad thronum` (272): a legal *appeal* from one seat to another (bench of justice → throne of grace, Heb. 4:16); kept the forensic verb "appeal."
