## Translator's flags

1. **The articles keep their printed rows.** Eighteen nouns under the head-word *Faith*, in six rows of three — except **row 2, which has only two** (Epiphany, Baptism), kept short as printed rather than padded. Set as rows they are a creed compressed to nouns; run together as prose they become a list, and the compression is the point.

2. **Received creedal English for the Greek nouns**, not fresh translation: Ἐπιφάνεια = **the Epiphany**; Κατάβασις = **the Descent**; Ἀνάβασις = **the Ascension**; **Καθέδρα = the Session** (the sitting at the right hand — "seat" would be literal and wrong); **Ἐπάνοδος = the Return** (literally "the way back," the second coming). The heading's *Ἀνακεφαλαίωσις* is Paul's **recapitulation** (Eph. 1:10) — the heading-up of all things under one Head — which is why "summary" will not do.

3. **"Partaker" twice, deliberately.** *Fac me horum participem* closes the creed; *Obsecro fac participem* stands in the pleading. The same English word must carry both, because the second piece exists to obtain what the first asks.

4. **⚠ Every verb of the Pleading is doing separate work, and one of them is startling.** *I call to mind · I give thanks · **I put thee in mind** · I remember · I commemorate · I offer · **or I ask that thou offer** · make me partaker · **apply them unto me**.* Memorial, thanksgiving, offering, application — a whole eucharistic theology in four lines. **`admoneo` = "I put thee in mind"** — the anamnesis as reminding *God* of the sacrifice; Andrewes already has three verbs for the worshipper's remembering (*recolo, recordor, commemoro*), so this fourth one is not another of them and must not be translated as though it were. And *vel peto ut offeras* — "**or I ask that thou offer**" — hands the offering back to Christ rather than claiming it. Neither line is softened here.

5. **`Interpellatio` = a Pleading.** The legal word for interposing a plea before a judge, and the verb of Rom. 8:34 (*qui etiam interpellat pro nobis*, "who also maketh intercession for us"). "Eucharistic Intercession" would be vaguer and would lose the courtroom.

6. **The 364 layout is two parallel columns, and the English keeps them.** Two braced Latin/Greek lists on the left run **beside** an unbraced column of Greek accusatives on the right; the braces therefore open and close across rows here instead of collapsing to one line as everywhere else in this edition. Reformatting them into tidy single-line braces would silently destroy a page-layout that is itself meaningful.

7. **`the firstfruits of thy blood`** — *ἀπαρχὰς αἵματος*, the Circumcision as the first shedding, set beside *circumcision* on the same line. One of the finest phrases in Part II and it is left exactly that dense.

8. **The left column descends while the right ascends, and neither is reordered.** *Emptying-out → humbling → enfleshing → conception in the womb* traces the descent from heaven down into the womb (ἐκκένωσις is the *kenosis* of Phil. 2:7); the right column begins at the nativity and moves forward. The two columns cross over the same mystery in opposite directions. **Do not put either into chronological order.**

9. **`having not where to lay thy head`** — Matt. 8:20, quoted with its verb gapped in the Greek (*οὐκ ἔχων ποῦ τὴν κεφαλήν*). The AV's words supply what the printing leaves out; the gap is noted in the transcript rather than mended there.

10. **Gethsemane, Gabbatha, Golgotha are §11's triad**, compressed here into a single brace — the three place-names that structure the whole Passion meditation at printed 309–315. **Same English forms in both places**, so a reader hears the compression for what it is.

11. **`Prayer: { Yea — Let it ascend. / Nay — Turn thou not away. }` quotes §24.** *Ascendat* and *Ne declines* are lifted verbatim from the *Divisio*, the outline of prayer at printed 347, and are here re-labelled as prayer's **Yea** and its **Nay** — its positive petition and its negative. Two Latin words standing inside a Greek line is the signal. Rendered with the same English as at §24 so the quotation is visible.

12. **`Of Faith. Of Hope. Of Love.`** — three bare genitives (1 Cor. 13:13), hanging on *Confession* in the line above. Left bare; repeating "confession" three times would destroy a cadence that is doing the work of a full stop for the whole section.

13. **`ὕβριν` rendered "thy shame"** — the outrage done to him, not his own; in the hunger/weariness/thirst/watching series it is the fifth bodily indignity, so "insult" would be too external and "pride" (the classical sense) simply wrong.
