# Part II — Preces Quotidianæ — §40 Ἀφορμαὶ *Meditationum* (three catenæ: ante Preces Pœnitentiales · ante Intercessionum · ante Εὐχαριστίαν ἢ Εὐλογίαν) — raw transcript

Source: 1853 Parker ed., printed pp. 381 (mid) – 384 (upper) (PDF 408, 413, 414, 415), images `raw/end/p-NNN.jpg`. Offset **+27 at 381, then +31 from 382** — see the offset note below; **every leaf eye-verified**, and 385 carries the signature `AND. PRECES. — 3 D` as an independent check.
**FOUR SCRIPTS on this stretch: Latin · Greek · Hebrew** (Ps lxv. 2 at printed 383) — the stretch the volume map has been warning about since Phase 0. The Hebrew is **main text**, not apparatus.
**Genre note: this is NOT a prayer.** It is three **catenæ of authorities** — one set of ἀφορμαί ("occasions", starting-points, handles for meditation) before each of three offices: the Penitential Prayers, the Intercessions, and the Eucharist-or-Blessing. Each entry is a *citation tag* (book + chapter + verse, or a father's name) followed by its saying, in whichever language the 1853 gives it, Greek and Latin alternating freely and without translation. The tag-and-saying typography is the same as §§33–34's exemplar-rolls.
Section boundary: begins mid-381 with the heading Ἀφορμαὶ *Meditationum ante Preces Pœnitentiales*, directly after §39's close (*…Dominum ac Servatorem nostrum.*); ends on 384 at the cue-line **Ἀλλὰ τίς εἰμι ἐγώ, Κύριε—&c.** Confirmed by read-ahead: *Oratio præparatoria ante* Εὐλογίαν opens mid-384 (that is §41), and the *Monita et Meditationes præparatoriæ* follow at the foot of 384 and run onto 385+.
Discipline: verbatim incl. accentuation/punctuation; `[?]`=unreadable; printer's turned-line hyphens kept as printed; broken/wrong sorts flagged, never mended.

## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ OFFSET BREAK — the duplicate tail has resumed, and arithmetic would have wrecked this section.** Printed 381 = PDF 408 (**+27**, as recorded for 370–381). But **the spread 380/381 is scanned THREE times** — PDF 407/408, again at 409/410, again at 411/412 — so printed 382 is **PDF 413, offset +31**, not the +27 the resume note projected. All four leaves of this section were verified by eye against the printed page number in the running head, and printed 385 (PDF 416) carries the gathering signature `AND. PRECES. — 3 D`, which confirms +31 independently of the head. **The 370–381 stretch had no duplicates at all; the pattern remains unreliable and every leaf from here to the end must be eye-verified.**

2. **⚠ The Hebrew at 383 is MAIN TEXT and is the second Hebrew in Part II** (after §3's Hosanna at printed 274). Verified on a 600-dpi crop, `raw/end/crop-hebrew-383-414.jpg`: **לך דומיה תהלה** — unpointed, no vowel points and no accents in the plate. It is **Ps. lxv. 2 in the Hebrew numbering (lxv. 1 in English Bibles)**, and the whole point of quoting it here is that the Hebrew says something the Latin and English Psalters do not: *to thee silence is praise*. The 1853 sets its own gloss immediately after in italic — *tacet tibi laus vel Psalmus* — which is a **literal rendering of the Hebrew, deliberately against the Vulgate's *Te decet hymnus*** (the Vulgate reads the same consonants as *dumiyah* → "is fitting" rather than "silence"). Andrewes then glosses his own gloss on 384: *Opera Tua non attingit, plus habet silentii*. **Neither layer may quietly substitute the Prayer-Book's "Thou, O God, art praised in Sion"** — the argument of the whole entry is that praise falls silent, and the received English text destroys it. Keep the Hebrew; add the English after it per CONVENTIONS §11; do not drop a script.

3. **Three headings, three different word-orders — kept exactly as printed.** 381 reads Ἀφορμαὶ *Meditationum ante Preces Pœnitentiales* (Greek word first); 382 and 383 both read *Meditationum* ἀφορμαὶ *ante …* (Latin first, Greek second, lower-case). The macaronic heading is itself the tell that these three belong together as one architecture, and the varying order is the compositor's or Andrewes' own — **not regularised**.

4. **`ante Intercessionum` (382) is ungrammatical as printed** — *ante* takes the accusative, so *ante Intercessiones* is wanted; the genitive plural is presumably attracted to *Meditationum* standing next to it. **Kept as printed**, joining the standing list of things flagged and not mended (`[Jer. l. 24.]` §23, `[Eph. i. 8.]` §27, `[Dan. vi. 4.]` §28, `[Psal. cxix. 114.]` §34). The English renders the sense (*before the Intercessions*) without comment on the page.

5. **`*Ecclus,*` at 383 (xliii. 27) is printed with a COMMA where every other tag on the page has a period** — a wrong or fallen sort, kept as printed per the broken-sorts rule.

6. **`Ὅτι χρηστὸν ἐναντίων τῶν ὁσίων` (383, Psal. lii. 10) departs from the LXX and is kept.** LXX Ps li. 11 reads *ἐναντίον τῶν ὁσίων σου* (adverbially, "before thy saints"); the plate has the **genitive plural ἐναντίων** and no *σου*. Verified at 600 dpi (`raw/end/crop-gk-383-414.jpg`) — the omega is unambiguous. Latin unrepaired; the English follows the received sense ("before thy saints") and says so here rather than emending the Greek.

7. **`Μακάριος ὁ γινώσκων ἀλαλάγματα` (383, Psal. lxxxix. 16) is compressed and pluralised.** LXX Ps lxxxviii. 16 reads *μακάριος ὁ λαὸς ὁ γινώσκων ἀλαλαγμόν* — Andrewes drops *ὁ λαός* (so the beatitude falls on the individual worshipper, not the people) and gives the plural *ἀλαλάγματα*. Both changes are the point; **neither is restored.** *ἀλαλαγμός* is the **festal shout**, the BCP's "joyful sound."

8. **`*Jo.* iv. 23. ———— ἐν πνεύματι καὶ ἀληθείᾳ.` — the long rule is the 1853's ELISION MARK, not a lacuna.** It stands for the omitted head of the verse (*τοὺς προσκυνητὰς προσκυνήσουσι τῷ Πατρὶ*), exactly as a modern editor would use an ellipsis; the entry is quoting only the tail, *in spirit and in truth*. The transcript keeps the rule; the English must **not** silently supply the missing words, and must not print an empty gap either — a bracketed lead-in is the right device.

9. **`Ἀλλὰ τίς εἰμι ἐγώ, Κύριε—&c.` is a CUE, not a fragment**, and closes the section. It is 2 Sam. vii. 18 (David: "who am I, O Lord") — **the same verse already set out in §25** (printed 348foot–349, in its cento with 2 Sam. ix. 8, *canem mortuum similem mei*). The `—&c.` is the 1853 telling the reader to go on from memory. The English keeps the cue-form and the dash: finishing the quotation would turn a pointer into a text, and would also make §40 end on a sentence when it ends on a gesture. **Cross-referenced in §25's files; do not import §25's fuller wording back into §40.**

10. **`Perge igitur ἀγωνίζεσθαι ἐν προσευχαῖς` (382) is a macaronic line and stays one.** Latin imperative governing a Greek infinitive lifted from Col. iv. 12 (*ἀγωνιζόμενος ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν ἐν ταῖς προσευχαῖς*) — Andrewes turns Epaphras' participle into the reader's own instruction. The English keeps *striving* / *wrestling* for ἀγωνίζεσθαι (the athletic word, and Paul's) and keeps the whole line unbroken; splitting it into a Latin part and a Greek part loses the fact that Andrewes read the two languages as one page.

11. **`Non passibus, sed precibus, itur ad Deum` (382, Ambrose) turns on a sound-pun** *passibus/precibus* — "not by paces but by prayers is God approached." The jingle cannot be reproduced exactly; the English keeps the two ablatives parallel and near-rhyming if it can, and does not paraphrase the impersonal *itur* into a first person ("we go") — the point is that this is how the going is done, by anyone.

12. **`Oratio est breviarium fidei, interpres spei` (382) — *breviarium* is the ABRIDGEMENT**, the short summary (the service-book sense is later and would be an anachronism here): prayer is faith in little, and hope's interpreter. The three unattributed lines on 382 (*Oratio est… / Non passibus… / Fides fundit…*) are set **indented and without a tag** — they continue Ambrose by implication but the plate does not say so, so the English leaves them untagged too.

13. **`Aug. contra Max.` = Augustine, *Contra Maximinum*.** Its argument depends on *inenarrabilis* meaning **unutterable** in Rom. viii. 26 and then being turned into a question about the reader's own spirit (*qui sæpe nullus, sæpe frigidus est* — often no spirit at all, often a cold one). The *unam Columbam* is the Church as the one Dove; the groanings are unutterable **because they are everyone's at once**, not because any one of them is deep. The English must keep the chain — *unutterable groanings → is your spirit unutterable? → no, but the Church's is* — or the paragraph collapses into piety.

14. **`nec Job, nec Daniel, nec Noah` (Jerome, 383) is Ezek. xiv. 14 used against post-mortem intercession** — the three righteous men who "shall deliver but their own souls." **The 1853 spells `Noah` (not *Noe*) in a Latin sentence**; kept as printed. The clause *sed unusquisque portat onus suum* is Gal. vi. 5.

15. **The three closing epigrams on 383 are untagged and rhythmically matched** — *Qui pro aliis orat, pro se laborat* (rhyme *orat/laborat*) · *Si pro te solo rogas, rogabis pro te solus* (the chiasmus of *solo/solus*) · *Si oras pro omnibus, orabunt pro te.* The English should carry the **turn of the second one especially**: pray for thyself alone and thou shalt be alone in praying for thyself. These are the hinge of the whole Intercession catena and are the most quotable lines in the section.

16. **`Bar. iv. 28` (384) — *διπλασιάσατε ἐπιστραφέντες ζητῆσαι αὐτόν*, "being turned, seek him TENFOLD" — is glossed by Andrewes' own Latin line immediately below** (*Ut antea in peccatis, sic jam in bonis operibus… abundemus*). The Greek verb is literally *double it*; the LXX of Baruch has *δεκαπλασιάσατε*. **The plate reads διπλασιάσατε** (verified at 600 dpi, `raw/end/crop-gk-384-415.jpg`) — kept as printed, and the English says *double* rather than the LXX's *tenfold*.

17. **No `[?]` in this section.** All four leaves are clean; the Greek was checked at 600 dpi on four crops (`crop-gk-381-408`, `crop-gk-382-413`, `crop-sam-382-413`, `crop-gk-383-414`, `crop-gk-384-415`) and the Hebrew on `crop-hebrew-383-414`. No capital upsilon occurs in this stretch, so the §34 fount trap does not bite here.
