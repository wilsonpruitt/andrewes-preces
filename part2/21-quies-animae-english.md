# Part II — Preces Quotidianæ — §21 *Quies animæ* (The Soul's Rest) — English

Line-keyed English of `21-quies-animae-transcript.md` (printed 331 mid–333). **LATIN ONLY** source (no Greek) — translated from the Latin (§6). A three-movement scripture-catena of consolation: *Quies animæ* (the soul's rest), *Recordare* (Remember), *Misericordia superexaltans* (mercy that triumphs). Register thou-form AV/BCP (§8–10); psalm-lines Coverdale/BCP (§9), the Isaiah/Ezekiel/Pentateuch bulk echoing AV where Andrewes' Latin agrees. Parity target 3/3.

## Translator's flags

- **Parity 3/3** against the three page-markers (331 mid, 332, 333). *Quies animæ* — the soul's rest: after the *Miserere* plea (§20), a catena of God's own words of promise spoken back to the penitent soul. Three movements, all Latin, all Scripture. Boundary confirmed: §22 CONFESSIO PECCATI opens printed 334.
- **The whole section is Scripture (§6, §9).** Since the bulk is Isaiah / Ezekiel / Pentateuch (not Psalms), the AV is the working model; psalm-lines (42, 103, 78, 116, 119) checked against Coverdale/BCP first. Andrewes' Latin sits close to the Vulgate/AV throughout — I have followed AV cadence wherever the Latin permits and flagged the few Vulgate-flavoured departures below.
- **Quies animæ (331–332).** *Adhuc peccatum excubat præ foribus* (Gen 4:7) — the one *warning* note amid consolation; Andrewes' *excubat* ("keeps watch, lies in wait") follows the Vulgate/LXX sense rather than the AV "lieth" — rendered "lieth in wait." The **Exod 34:6–7 mercy-formula** rendered in AV cadence ("merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth… forgiving iniquity"); Andrewes' *Dominator, Dominus Deus* = the LXX/Vulgate doubling, kept "The Ruler, the Lord God." Deut 30:1–6 (the circumcised heart) and the Ps 42/116 close (*Return, O my soul, unto thy rest* — the verse that names the section) rendered from Coverdale/BCP.
- **Recordare (332).** "Remember" — Ps 119:49/116 (BCP "O think upon thy servant… wherein thou hast caused me to put my trust"; I have kept closer to the Latin *Memor esto verborum* / *spem dedisti* = "Remember thy word… wherein thou hast given me hope"), then Ps 103:9–10 and Ps 78:38–39 (*Recordatus est quia caro sumus* — "he remembered that we are but flesh") on God's own remembering. Ps-lines Coverdale/BCP (§9).
- **Misericordia superexaltans (332–333).** "Mercy that triumpheth" (cf. Jas 2:13, "mercy rejoiceth against judgement," Vulg. *superexultat misericordia judicio*) — the great return-invitation: Is 1:18 (scarlet→snow), Is 30:15, the **bruised reed** Is 42:3 (spelled out in full here — *Calamum quassatum non confringet* — where §20's citation had the abbreviated/odd Greek κάλαμον κατεάγης), Is 43:25 & 44:22 (blotting out), Is 46:4 (God carrying to old age), the **Suffering Servant** Is 53:4–6 ("surely he hath borne our griefs… by his stripes we are healed… the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all"), Is 65:24 ("before they call, I will answer"), the Ezekiel refrains (18:23/30/32; 33:11 — *Vivo Ego; nolo mortem impii*, "as I live, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked"), and the close Is 55:7 ("he is great to pardon" — *quia multus est ad ignoscendum*, AV "he will abundantly pardon"). Rendered in AV cadence throughout.
- **Line-keying.** Printed hanging-indent line-breaks mirrored between layers so a finger runs across in step; the long prose verses (Exod 34, Deut 30, the Ezekiel/Isaiah wraps) keep the same number of lines in both. No Greek anywhere in §21.
