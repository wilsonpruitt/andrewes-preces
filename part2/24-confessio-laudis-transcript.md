## Translator's flags

1. **Third *Confessio Laudis* in Part II.** The heading has now been used at printed 299 (§8, the PROPTER attributes litany), in the variant *Confessio Laudum* at 305 (§10) and 316 (§13), and here. They are distinct compositions, not repetitions; the numbering in this repo is ours, not the 1853 edition's, so the slug carries a `24-` prefix to keep them apart.

2. **The estate-catalogue (345 foot – 346) reads column-wise as antitheses.** Each left member is what God made him; each right member, prefixed *non* / *neque*, is what he was spared. Kept as printed ` | ` rows. Two rows are two-line on the left against a single right member (*Ex honesta / parentela* against the crow-brace; *Erutus e periculis / ex infamia* against *non absorptus ab { illis / illa }*) — the brace and the singular/plural pairing (*illis* answering *periculis*, *illa* answering *infamia*) is the printing's own, preserved.

3. **`non { mali corvi / malum ovum, }` is a proverb, printed as a brace.** *Mali corvi malum ovum* — "of a bad crow, a bad egg." The 1853 brace splits the proverb across two lines against the two-line *Ex honesta parentela*; the English keeps the proverb legible rather than reproducing the split literally.

4. **`Verna`** = a slave born in the master's house, not a captive — hence "not house-born a bondman" rather than simply "not a slave." Paired with *Liber*.

5. **`Literatus, non mechanicus`** — the antithesis is the liberal arts against the mechanical (manual) trades, not "literate/illiterate." Rendered "lettered, not a mechanic."

6. **`Educatus, non expositus`** — *expositus* is the technical word for a child put out to die. "Brought up, not cast out."

7. **`1 Pet. i. 3. [4.]`** — the 1853 reference cites only i. 3, but the quotation runs into i. 4 (*in hæreditatem incorruptibilem …*); the bracketed `[4.]` is the 1853 editor's own supplement, kept as printed.

8. **The *Divisio* (347) is an outline, and its two columns read consecutively, not as pairs.** The left column runs *Compellatio … Deprecatio*; the right column continues it — *Deprecatio* takes its objects from the head of the right column (*Peccati, Pœnæ*), then *Comprecatio Gratiæ, Præmii*, then *Intercessio pro Creatura …*, closing *Benedictio, Commendatio*. Transcribed in the printed rows (page-faithful, per the mirror-layout rule) with the reading order flagged here; the English restores the running order so the outline is intelligible, and says so on the page.

9. **The 348 columns are genuinely parallel**, unlike 347: the left is a second scheme of the parts of a prayer (confession → profession of penitence → petition for pardon; then the connective formulae *Quia / Propter vel Per / Secundum / Et annunciabo*; then the alternative — *Vel sic* — of vow, confession of infirmity, petition for grace), and the right is a single list headed *Per.* — the acts of God *through* which the prayer is made (Creation … Redemption … Inspiration). Transcribed as printed rows including the two right-column overflow lines that have no left member.

10. **Ἄσκησις (348)** — the only Greek in the section, printed with the breathing-and-accent set before the capital in the 1853 manner. Not "asceticism" in the modern sense: the *exercise* proper to a man's age, calling, and estate. The brace *si { senex; / in Clero; / Episcopus. }* is Andrewes writing his own case — he was all three when the manuscript was compiling; the English keeps the conditional rather than resolving it.

11. **No `[?]` in this section.** Every leaf 345–348 is clean in the Google scan; the signature `AND. PRECES. — Y Y` on 345 confirms the gathering and the +23 offset independently of the page number.
