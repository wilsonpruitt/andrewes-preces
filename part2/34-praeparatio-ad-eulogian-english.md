## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ The section is an approach to communion, and its last line is the door.** *Præparatio ad **Εὐλογίαν*** — Andrewes' Greek for the Eucharist is *εὐλογία*, the cup of blessing (1 Cor. x. 16), so the heading is **Preparation for the Blessing** and not "for a benediction." Four acts follow — thanksgiving, hope, faith, doxology — and the last words of the last act are **"Blessed are they which are called unto the supper of the Lamb."** The English is set so that line stands alone at the foot with nothing after it, as the plate has it; it is the point the whole preparation has been walking toward, and it is the sentence a priest says at the administration.

2. **⚠ §§33 and 34 are one architecture, and the English must keep it.** §33 rolled the fathers **who were delivered** and then turned the roll on the worshipper (*so deliver us also*). §34 rolls first the **confessors** and then the **heavenly quire**, each entry tagged with whose word it is. Three lists, one move: what was said by them is now said by me. The tag-and-saying shape here is therefore typographically identical to §33's name-and-deliverance shape — same indentation, same terse apposition, no connectives added.

3. **The tags are possessives because in the Greek they are genitives hanging on the heading.** ΔΑΒΙΔ · ΠΑΥΛΟΥ · ἸΩΑΝΝΟΥ · … each depends on **Πιστεύω**: *I believe — David's word: to see the goodness of the Lord …* English "DAVID'S." keeps that dependence in one apostrophe. The same in the Doxology: **THE ANGELS' · ALL CREATED THINGS' · THE MARTYRS' · THE FOUR AND TWENTY ELDERS'**. Rendering them as headings ("The Angels") would turn a grammar into a caption and lose the fact that the worshipper is speaking every one of these lines himself.

4. **⚠ The four-cell table at the foot of 367 is left unfilled, deliberately.** *I would, if more. | I fear, lest. / I grieve, that not. | I should rejoice, if.* Andrewes prints four verbs of hope, each with its bare conjunction and nothing after it — the grammar of hope as a frame for the man praying to fill. **Every completion is the translator's sentence, not his**, so none is supplied; the English is as unfinished as the Latin, in the same four cells. The chiasm is worth seeing: the left column holds the wish and the grief, the right the fear and the joy.

5. **⚠ Justification: Gal. ii. 16 and Jas. ii. 22 stand side by side and are not reconciled.** *Justified by the faith of Christ, and not by the works of the law* — then, four lines later, *faith wrought with works, and by works is faith made perfect*. Andrewes closes his creed by setting the two texts down together and saying nothing. **No connective, no ordering, no gloss** is added in English; whatever is to be said about a Jacobean bishop doing that belongs in an introduction.

6. **`Confiteantur … benedicant` (Ps. 145:10) is rendered jussive** — *Let thy works confess thee … let thy saints bless thee* — against both the AV ("shall praise … shall bless") and Coverdale ("praise thee … give thanks"), because Andrewes' Latin is subjunctive. §9 gives Coverdale the first crib but not against the grammar on the page.

7. **`Ps. 31:1` is given in its Te Deum wording** — *O Lord, in thee have I trusted; let me never be confounded* — rather than Coverdale's "let me never be put to confusion." Same verse, but it is the last line of the Te Deum that an English reader carries in the ear, and this is a preparation for service.

8. **`do` (366) is kept abrupt: "I give them."** The Latin gives before it names the gift; the object (*laudes*) arrives in the next line. Smoothing it to "I offer thee praise" would repair a deliberate stumble in a passage that is about being unfit to speak at all.

9. **`[Ps. 119:114]` is wrong in the 1853 and is left wrong.** The line is verbatim Ps. 119:**49** (*Remember the word unto thy servant, upon which thou hast caused me to hope*), the verse §21 also used. Kept as printed per the standing rule; the English translates the line Andrewes actually wrote, not the verse the reference points at.

10. **`Dominus spei` is translated "the Lord of hope"** though both Vulgate and AV read *the God of hope* at Rom. xv. 13. The substitution is his; it is not normalised.

11. **`In tegmine alarum, in umbra, sub pennis` takes Coverdale's vocabulary** — *feathers*, not *pinions* (BCP Ps. 91:4, "thou shalt be safe under his feathers"). The three-fold phrase is compressed out of Ps. 57:1 and 91:4 together, and the English keeps it as three bare phrases without supplying a verb.

12. **`ΤΩΝ ΜΑΡΤΥΡΩΝ` rendered "THE MARTYRS'."** The word can be *witnesses*, and Rev. vii's multitude is not named martyrs in the text; but they are those that came out of great tribulation, and *martyr* is the word Andrewes chose. Flagged because the alternative is defensible.

13. **`ἘΠΙΘΑΛΑΜΙΟΝ` is his own label and is kept as one: THE MARRIAGE-SONG.** It is the only tag in the Doxology naming a kind of song rather than a singer, and it is there to prepare the last line about the marriage of the Lamb.

14. **`ΣΥΝΟΔΟΥ ΤΩΝ ΑΠΟΣΤΟΛΩΝ ΚΑΙ ΠΡΕΣΒΥΤΕΡΩΝ` is "THE SYNOD'S, OF THE APOSTLES AND ELDERS."** Acts xv, the council at Jerusalem — but rendered with the conciliar noun, because a **synod** confessing alongside David and Peter and Martha is the striking thing, and the awkwardness of the English possessive is worth the accuracy.

15. **Rev. 19:9 is given as "the supper of the Lamb," without "marriage."** The AV has "the marriage supper"; Andrewes' Greek is `εἰς τὸ δεῖπνον τοῦ Ἀρνίου`, bare — and the marriage has already been named two lines above in the *Epithalamion*. Keeping the last line spare is what lets it fall as an invitation.

16. **Scan/plate defects underlying this English are listed in the transcript's flag 15** (unaccented `Κυριου`, breathing-less `εις`, the damaged stop after `ΚΤΙΣΜΑΤΩΝ ΠΑΝΤΩΝ`, and the θρόνου/Θρόνου inconsistency). None of them affects the sense, and none is repaired in either layer.
