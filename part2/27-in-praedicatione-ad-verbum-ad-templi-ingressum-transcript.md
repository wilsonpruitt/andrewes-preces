## Translator's flags

1. **This is the priest's own vestry-and-pulpit office** — the three headings run in the order of the act: the figure of what preaching *is* (*In Prædicatione*), the prayer before opening the book and before speaking (*Ad Verbum*), the prayer at the church door (*Ad Templi ingressum*). Andrewes is praying as the man who will preach, which is why the *Ad Verbum* prayers are all for the mouth and the lips.

2. **The draught-of-fishes table (354) is a five-fold allegory, not a list.** *Mundus | Mare · Homines | Pisces · Ecclesia | Lembus · Prædicator | Piscator · Verbum | Rete* — Luke v. 5, Peter's night of fruitless toil. Each row is *thing signified | figure*, in that order; the English must keep the direction (the World **is** the Sea, not the Sea the World). *Lembus* is a small boat/skiff, the fishing smack of the Gospel scene, not a ship.

3. **`pruna geminæ naturæ` (355) is the section's jewel, and a patristic figure.** The live coal of Isaiah vi. 6, taken with the tongs from the altar and laid on the prophet's lips — read as **the coal of the twofold nature**, the incarnate Word in whom fire (Godhead) and coal-substance (manhood) are one. The figure is standing in the Greek Fathers and in the Liturgy (the coal of the Eucharist laid on the communicant's lips); Andrewes addresses it directly — *O Tu, pruna* — as a vocative to Christ. **Do not flatten to "live coal"**; the *geminæ naturæ* is the whole point.

4. **`ter sancti Spiritus` (355)** = the **thrice-holy** Spirit (the Trisagion), not "three times holy" nor "most holy." *Potentia sanctificante ter sancti Spiritus Tui* — by the sanctifying power of thy thrice-holy Spirit.

5. **`[Eph. i. 8.]` for *Illumina sensus cordis* is almost certainly a slip for Eph. i. 18** (Vulg. *illuminatos oculos cordis vestri*, "the eyes of your understanding being enlightened"); i. 8 is "wherein he hath abounded toward us in all wisdom." **Kept as printed**, flagged, not corrected — same discipline as the `Jer. l.` case at §23.

6. **`siquod verbum ad ædificationem necessitatis` (355)** is Eph. iv. 29 in Vulgate words (*sed si quis bonus ad aedificationem opportunitatis*) — "if any word be to the edifying of the need," i.e. a word fitted to the hearers' present want. Yoked to Is. l. 4, "the tongue of the learned … a word in season to him that is weary." The two references are printed together because they are one petition.

7. **The commemoration on 356 is liturgical, not private.** *Memor esto … fratrum mihi circumstantium, et comprecantium mihi hac hora* / *Memor etiam esto eorum, qui propter rationabiles causas absunt* — the diptych of the Greek anaphora, remembering those present and those absent "for reasonable cause." *Comprecantium mihi* = praying **together with** me, not "praying for me." Keep the pair; it is why the *Ad Templi ingressum* is not a solitary devotion.

8. **The Greek couplet at the foot of 356 is a doublet, not a new text.** Κύριε, ἠγάπησα εὐπρέπειαν οἴκου σου / καὶ τόπον σκηνώματος τῆς δόξης σου is LXX Ps. xxv. 8 — the same verse already given in Latin twelve lines above (*Domine, dilexi decorem domus Tuæ, et locum habitationis gloriæ Tuæ*, cited there as Ps. xxvi. 8). Andrewes closes the devotion by saying it again in Greek. **The English must repeat its own rendering of the line**, not vary it, or the seal is lost. Note the 1853 prints **τῆς δόξης** where the LXX has bare δόξης; kept as printed.

9. **`[Vers. 7.]` and `[Vers. 8.]` (356)** = verses 7 and 8 of Ps. xxvi, the psalm cited just above — not a book. Note they run **backwards** (v. 8 cited first, then v. 7): the 1853 is faithful to Andrewes' order, which follows his sense, not the psalm's. Kept as printed.

10. **`Aperite mihi portas justitiæ` (356)** — Ps. cxviii. 19, the gates-of-righteousness verse sung at the church door; note the plural imperative (*Aperite*, "Open ye"), addressed to the gatekeepers, not to God. Preserve the plural.
