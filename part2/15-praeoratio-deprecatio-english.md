## Translator's flags

- **Parity 2/2** against the two page-markers (321, 322). Two caps-titled parts of one oration movement — the plea *for* hearing (Præoratio) and *against* wrath (Deprecatio). No Greek.
- **PRÆORATIO (321):** "The Fore-Prayer" — the prayer for prayer. The first six ask that the prayer itself *ascend / reach / enter / appear / find grace / draw near* (Acts 10:4; 2 Chr 30:27; Ps 88:2; 141:2; Heb 4:16; Ps 119:169). The hinge — *that it return not to me empty* (Isa 55:11, "my word… shall not return unto me void") — *but as Thou knowest, and canst, and wilt*. The second six ask that God *hear / incline Thine ear / attend and consider / understand / hearken / remember to do it* (Ps 119:149; 86:1 + Dan 9:18; Ps 61:1 + Lam 1:12; Ps 139:2; Ps 64:1; Dan 9:19, "O Lord, hearken and do; defer not"). Psalm-lines Coverdale/BCP.
- **DEPRECATIO (321–322):** "The Warding-off" — the great **Ne** brace, all under one governing "that Thou wilt not." I render the governor once and let the ~24 verbs hang, matching the Latin's single *Ne + subjunctive* stacking. The petitions are a Psalm-lament catena: turn away / hide Thy face / set a cloud / shut Thine ear / forsake / leave / despise / keep silence / **sleep** (Matt 8:24, Christ asleep in the storm) / go far / cast off / take away mercy / fail of truth / rebuke in anger / chasten in displeasure / cast from Thy presence / take not Thy Spirit / forget not / be not wroth. Two of the Psalm-refrains recur exactly (Ps 51:11 for both "cast me from Thy presence" and "take not Thy Spirit" — the Miserere's double plea).
- **The broken pattern (322):** *Noli me reprobare a pueris Tuis* (Wisd 9:4) breaks the *Ne*-subjunctive with an explicit imperative — "cast me not off from among Thy children." I keep it inside the brace but parenthesize the English so the shift of construction is visible.
- **The inner brace (322):** *{ tradas / allidas } me in manu iniquitatis* — "that Thou wilt not **deliver / dash** me into the hand of iniquity" (Ps 27:12; Isa 64:7). Two verbs, one object; kept the brace. Then *tardes reverti* ("be slow to return," Hab 2:3) and *perdas cum impiis animam meam* ("destroy not my soul with the wicked," Ps 26:9).
- **The close (322):** the enemy's permitted power (*Si potentiam hostis ad tempus Tua permissione patimur*) answered by the plea not to be *swallowed by his insatiable jaws*. Then the paradox-couplet: *Vincatur / Leo ab ove infirma, / Violens spiritus a debili carne* — "Let the Lion be conquered by the weak Sheep, the violent spirit by the feeble flesh." The Lamb's victory (Rev 5:5) turned to the suppliant's own frailty as the ground of triumph — weakness overcoming violence. §15 ends; the (second) Allegatio (§16) follows.
