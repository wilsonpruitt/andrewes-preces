## Translator's flags

1. **The eighteen articles are set as six rows of three, and the architecture is deliberate.** Under the head-word *Πίστις*: Conception–Nativity–Circumcision · Epiphany–Baptism [only two] · Fast–Temptations–Sufferings · Cross–Death–Burial · Descent–Resurrection–Ascension · Session–Return–Judgement. **Row 2 has only two members** — kept as printed, not padded. The English must hold the rows; run as prose they are a list, but set as printed they are a creed compressed to nouns.

2. **The Greek article-nouns are technical and should take their received English names**: Σύλληψις = the Conception; Ἐπιφάνεια = the Epiphany (the manifestation, not "appearing"); Κατάβασις = the Descent [into hell]; Ἀνάβασις = the Ascension; **Καθέδρα = the Session** (the sitting at the right hand — the word is "seat/chair," and *the Session* is its creedal English); **Ἐπάνοδος = the Return** (the second coming, literally the way back); Κρίσις = the Judgement. *Ἀνακεφαλαίωσις* in the heading is the Pauline "recapitulation" (Eph. i. 10), not merely "summary" — head things up under one Head. Andrewes chose it.

3. **`Fac me horum participem` — "Make me partaker of these" — is the hinge between the two pieces.** It ends the creed and is answered in the next piece by *Obsecro fac participem, mihique applica*. Use the **same English word (partaker)** in both places; the pair is the section's argument.

4. **⚠ *Interpellatio Eucharistica* is a compressed eucharistic theology and every verb counts.** *Recolo, gratias ago, **admoneo**, recordor, commemoro, offero, vel peto ut offeras, Obsecro fac participem, **mihique applica**.* Four things are being said: it is a **memorial** (recolo, recordor, commemoro), a **thanksgiving** (gratias ago), an **offering** — with the astonishing qualification *vel peto ut offeras*, "or I ask that **thou** offer" — and an **application** to the individual (mihique applica). ***Admoneo*** is the sharpest word: "I put [thee] in mind," the anamnesis understood as reminding God of the sacrifice. **Do not soften it to "I remember"** — Andrewes has three other verbs for remembering, and this is not one of them. *Interpellatio* is the legal word for interposing a plea (Rom. viii. 34, *qui etiam interpellat pro nobis*): **a Pleading**, not an "intercession" in the loose sense.

5. **⚠ 364 carries TWO PARALLEL BRACE-COLUMNS, and this page breaks the repo's usual inline-brace rule.** The two left braces (*Per { quæ fecisti … sacrificium }* and *Per { ἐκκένωσιν … σύλληψιν ἐν γαστρί }*) run **beside** a right-hand column of Greek accusatives that is not braced at all; the left *Per* labels sit vertically centred on their braces, between printed rows. Collapsing the braces to one line each — the standing convention — would destroy the parallel. **So on this leaf the braces are opened and closed across rows within a ` | ` two-column encoding**, and the three later braces (which face no column) use the ordinary inline form. The inconsistency is on the page, not in the encoding.

6. **`ἀπαρχὰς αἵματος` = "the firstfruits of blood"** — the Circumcision as the first shedding, set beside *περιτομήν* on the same line. One of the finest phrases in Part II; it must not be flattened to "his first blood."

7. **`Per { ἐκκένωσιν, ταπείνωσιν, ἐνσάρκωσιν, σύλληψιν ἐν γαστρί }` runs backwards on purpose.** Emptying (ἐκκένωσις, the *kenosis* of Phil. ii. 7) → humbling → enfleshing → conception in the womb: the descent traced from heaven downward, ending in the womb, which is where the *other* column's list began (γέννησιν). The two columns move in opposite directions across the same events. **Do not reorder into chronology.**

8. **`οὐκ ἔχων ποῦ τὴν κεφαλήν` is a quotation left unfinished** (Matt. viii. 20, *οὐκ ἔχει ποῦ τὴν κεφαλὴν κλίνῃ* — "hath not where to lay his head"); the verb is gapped in the printing. Supply it in English as the AV does, and note the gap.

9. **`δεσμούς` is printed with a broken sort — the scan shows `δ σμούς`** with a gap where the epsilon should be. Not a variant and not illegible; transcribed whole, flagged. (Compare *tribue*/`tribuc` at §26, printed 353.)

10. **`Per τὰ ἐν { Γεθσημανῆ, / Γαββαθᾶ, / Γολγοθᾶ }` is the same triad as §11**, the great Passion meditation (printed 309–315), where the three place-names structure the whole. Here they are compressed into one brace. **Use the same English forms as §11** so the echo lands.

11. **`Προσευχὴ { Ναὶ, Ascendat. / Μὴ, Ne declines. }` quotes §24's *Divisio* verbatim.** *Ascendat* and *Ne declines* are the second and third lines of the outline of prayer at printed 347 — here re-labelled as prayer's **Yea** and **Nay**, its positive petition and its negative. The two Latin words in a Greek line are the tell. **Cross-reference in the English apparatus**; a reader who met them at §24 must be able to recognise them.

12. **`Ἐξομολόγησις { Χάριτος· Ὠδή. / Ἐπαίνων· Ψαλμός. }`** — confession *of grace* answered by a **Song** (ᾨδή), confession *of praises* by a **Psalm**. The Greek raised points are colons of definition, not full stops; kept as printed.

13. **`Πίστεως. Ἐλπίδος. Ἀγάπης.` — three bare genitives closing the section**, hanging on the *Ἐξομολόγησις* above: confession of Faith, of Hope, of Love (1 Cor. xiii. 13). Kept bare in English — supplying "confession" three times would ruin the cadence, and the genitives are legible from the line above.

14. **No `[?]` in this section.** Every leaf 363–365 is clean in the Google scan; the Greek accents and breathings are legible throughout and are transcribed as printed.
