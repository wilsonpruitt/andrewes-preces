## Translator's flags

- **Parity 5/5.** The *Horologium* is a canonical-hours litany; kept every hour-heading parallel ("Who at the Nth hour …") and the refrain uniform as **"And save us"** (*Et serva nos*; the one variant *Serva nos* at Col 2:14 kept as bare "Save us").
- **Hours-to-Gospel-events** all rendered as events (see transcript flags): Pentecost, Crucifixion + Peter's sheet-vision, the healed fever, Christ's death + the hour of prayer, Andrew's "We have found the Messias," the eleventh-hour labourers, the Supper, the burial, the giving of the keys, David & Paul at midnight + the Bridegroom's cry, Peter's cock-crow, the unknown hour of Advent, dawn.
- **`Chirographum… tollens de medio`** (287) = Col 2:14 AV "blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us… took it out of the way." Kept the AV "handwriting… against us."
- **`nec ad judicium… sed ad peccati remissionem, et legata Novi Testamenti`** (288) = 1 Cor 11:29 ("not unto damnation") + a testamentary figure: the Sacrament conveys "the bequests of the New Testament [Covenant/Will]." Kept "bequests" for *legata* (legal legacies) — the Covenant read as Christ's will.
- **`sed quoad remissionem, ne ad retentionem`** (288): Andrewes asks to share the John 20:23 power of the keys *only in its remitting half*, not the retaining — a deliberate devotional humility. Rendered plainly so the asymmetry lands.
- **`Mane nobiscum… advesperascit… inclinata est jam dies vitæ nostræ`** (290): Luke 24:29, Emmaus — *Mane* is the imperative of *maneo* ("abide"), and Andrewes plays it against the hours-theme. AV "Abide with us: for it is toward evening, and the day is far spent," extended to "the day of our life." Kept the AV phrasing.
- **Inline Greek (290):** `Paracletus, ἄλλος` — "the Paraclete, *another* [Comforter]" (John 14:16, ἄλλον παράκλητον). Kept the Greek with an English gloss; the Spirit's four titles Comforter / Unction / Seal / Earnest in AV vocabulary.
- **`Opera { Creatoris → Justitia / Redemptoris → Misericordia / Spiritus Sancti → Sancta Spiratio }`** (290): the appropriated works of the three Persons. *Sancta Spiratio* is the scholastic term for the Spirit's mode of procession (the Father and Son's "breathing-forth" of the Spirit) — rendered "Holy Spiration" and flagged, not flattened to "inspiration."
- Psalm cribs (§9): Ps 119:55 ("thought upon thy Name in the night-season… kept thy law"), 65:8 ("outgoings of the morning and evening"), 127:3 ("giveth his beloved sleep"), 51:11 ("take not thy holy Spirit"), 63:6 ("upon my bed… in the night-watches"), 4:6 ("lift up the light of thy countenance"), 36:9 ("in thy light shall we see light"), 136:25 ("giveth food to all flesh"), 147:9 ("feedeth the young ravens"), 71:5 ("my hope from my youth"), 102:11 ("my days are gone like a shadow"). Kept BCP.
- `stabili gratia Tua cor nostrum` (290) = Heb 13:9 "the heart be stablished with grace." *Somnum sanitatis* (286, Ps 127:3) = "sleep of health/soundness" — Andrewes' fuller form of "he giveth his beloved sleep."
