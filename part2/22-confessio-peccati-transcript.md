## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 334–339.** *Confessio Peccati* — the Confession of Sin under a fresh all-caps heading (a clean boundary; §21 ended 333). One continuous meditative confession, punctuated by the recurring **Ecce, Domine** refrain (335, 336×2, 337, 338 *Ecce me* / *Et nunc*), that modulates into the **Meditation of the Day of Judgement** and closes on the great **Daniel 9:19** cadence. This is the Latin counterpart to Part I's Greek Meditatio de judicio extremo (252–259). **Boundary confirmed by read-ahead:** §22 ends 339 at *propter Teipsum [Dan. ix. 19.]*; §23 **In magnum pietatis sacramentum** (the mystery of godliness, 1 Tim 3:16 — the Incarnation/economy-of-grace thanksgiving) opens immediately below and runs onto 340.
- **The confession proper (334–335).** Built on the penitential psalms: Ps 69:5 (*Tu scis insipientiam meam* — "thou knowest my foolishness"), Ps 51:3 (*peccatum meum contra me est semper* — "my sin is ever before me"), Job 31:33 (not hiding sin "as Adam"), Ps 141:4 (not making excuses in sin), the *Peccavi / Peccavi Tibi, Domine / Erravi sicut ovis quæ periit* refrain (Ps 106:6; Ps 119:176 "I have gone astray like a lost sheep"), the animal-images of relapse (Jer 31:18 untamed heifer; Prov 26:11 & 2 Pet 2:22 the dog to its vomit / the washed sow to the mire), and the Achan-confession formula (*Do Tibi gloriam… sic et sic feci*, Josh 7:19–20).
- **The Greek couplet (334).** *Ὦ Κύριε, λίνον τυφόμενον μὴ σβέσῃς, / κάλαμον συντετριμμένον μὴ κατεάξῃς* — Matt 12:20, "the smoking flax shalt thou not quench, the bruised reed shalt thou not break." Main text; kept as printed. **Note:** here the reed-clause is in full correct form (*κάλαμον συντετριμμένον μὴ κατεάξῃς*) — this confirms the abbreviated/odd *κάλαμον κατεάγης* flagged at §20 (printed 329) was a scan/print defect there, not Andrewes' reading.
- **The brace catalogues (335–337).** Encoded inline `{ a / b / c }`: *Quod precor a Te* {contrite heart Ps 51:17 / unutterable groanings Rom 8:26 / bloody tears Luke 22:44 — the three things he begs to be *given*}; *Væ mihi* {from dryness Is 24:16 / from hardness of heart Rom 2:5 / from dryness of eyes Jer 9:1 — the three penitential barrennesses}; the fourfold plea *{ contere / scinde / compunge / percute } cor meum* ("break / rend / prick / smite my heart"); *propter { stultum / inutile / nocivum / periculosum } desiderium carnis meæ* (the fleshly desire that is foolish/useless/harmful/dangerous, 335); on 336 *propter { absurditatem / turpitudinem / vilitatem } desiderii* and *{ confusione / opprobrio } dignam*; on 337 the judgement-catalogue as a two-column table (Judex incorruptus | Tribunal horrendum …) and *Nullus qui { assistat / patrocinetur / liberet }* ("none to {stand by / plead for / deliver} me").
- **The double-sided brace (337).** *{ Meritus sum mortem a Te, / Sed adhuc appello ad Te, } Domine, { a Te justo. / ad Te misericordem, }* — a chiastic hinge: the two left lines ("I have deserved death from Thee / Yet still I appeal to Thee") both attach to "Domine," and the two right phrases ("from Thee the just / to Thee the merciful") complete each. The appeal *a solio justitiæ ad thronum gratiæ* ("from the throne of justice to the throne of grace," cf. Heb 4:16) is the meditation's turn. Rendered to preserve the appeal-from/appeal-to symmetry.
- **The Day of Judgement (336–338).** The great catalogue of the *dies iræ*: the thrones set, angels standing, the books opened (Dan 7:9–10 / Rev 20:12), the two-column terrors (incorruptible Judge / dreadful tribunal; unquenchable fire; the restless worm Mark 9:44; measureless chaos), the self-sentence (*Dijudico egomet me dignum… æterni supplicii*), the Mark 4:38 cry (*non ad Te pertinet, si perimus?* — "carest thou not that we perish?"), 1 Tim 2:4 / 2 Pet 3:9 (God "willeth all to be saved… willing that none should perish"), and the prostration (*Flecto genua / Procido in terram / in faciem meam*; the publican's *percutio pectus meum*, Luke 18:13; *De profundis*, Ps 130:1; *Anima mea sicut terra sine aqua*, Ps 143:6).
- **The Dan 9:19 close (339).** The *Propter*-cascade (for thy great mercy, the multitude/riches/abundance/**super**abundance of thy mercies — Ps 51:1, Eph 2:4, 1 Pet 1:3, Rom 5:20) climaxing in the publican's *Propitius esto mihi, Domine, peccatori* (Luke 18:13) + *peccatorum primo* (1 Tim 1:15, "chief of sinners"), the Jas 2:13 *superexaltet misericordia judicium* ("mercy triumpheth over judgement"), Rom 5:20 (*ubi abundavit delictum… superabundet gratia*, "where sin abounded, grace did much more abound"), and the **Daniel 9:19** litany verbatim: *Exaudi / Propitius esto / Intende / Domine intende et fac / Fac, et ne moreris propter Teipsum* ("O Lord, hear; O Lord, forgive; O Lord, hearken and do; defer not, for thine own sake"). The indent-cascade is deliberate (each imperative shorter and more urgent) — preserved.
