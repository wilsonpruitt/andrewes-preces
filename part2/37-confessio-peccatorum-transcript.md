                CONFESSIO PECCATORUM.

    I. Ecce me, Domine,
        Ecce me, peccatorum { Maximum, / Pessimum, / Miserrimum ! }
(Et quid dicam jam, aut in quo aperiam os meum?
Quid respondebo, cum ipse fecerim, fecerim, fecerim?)
    [*Is.* xxxviii. 15.]

<!-- printed 373 (PDF 400) -->

Recogitabo Tibi omnia peccata mea
  In amaritudine animæ meæ:
    O utinam in amaritudine amarissima.   [*Ibid. et Vers.* 17.]
Domine, si sic vivitur, et in talibus est vita spiritus mei,
    corripies me et vivificabis me.   [*Vers.* 16.]
Ecce enim in pace mea
    amaritudo mea facta est amarissima.   [*Vers.* 17.]
At ego sicut pullus hirundinis trinsabo,
    Gemam velut columba.   [*Vers.* 14.]
Deprecor autem Te, Domine, in omnem misericordiam Tuam,
  Ut avertatur a me justissima indignatio, et furor Tuus,
    Quia peccavi, idque graviter, peccavi Tibi,
      Graviter et sæpe peccavi Tibi.
II. O Pater misericordiarum,
        Obsecro paterna viscera miserationum Tuarum,
    Et ne { Vermem immundum, / Canem mortuum, / Cadaver putridum, } ne me despice.
Quin respice me, Domine,
  Oculis illis respice
    quibus { Magdalenam in convivio, / Petrum in atrio, / Latronem in patibulo; }
    Ut cum { Petro defleam, / Latrone confitear, / Magdalena diligam; }
        Diligam Te multum,
        Plurimum, ut cui remissa sunt plurima.
Parce, quæso, Domine; parce, parce pœnitenti;

    Certe { Pœnitere disponenti et cupienti; / Peccata cum amaritudine recogitanti, / Sibi ipsi de peccatis admissis indignanti, / Passionis Tuæ amarissimæ recordanti, / Eique adhærenti. }
Parce, Domine; propitius esto.
  Parce mihi, Domine, propitius esto;
    Miserere mei,
      Quia { non est difficile potentiæ / nec indecens justitiæ / nec insolitum clementiæ } Tuæ.
III. Siccine, ut pro porris et alliis
            Panem Angelorum?
  Siccine ut pro porcorum siliquis
        Mensam paternam?
    Væ misero mihi, væ insensato!
        Quis me fascinavit
            Ut sic desiperem?
O si Tu modo recipere digneris,
  Omnino mihi reverti mens est:
    Melius enim mihi tum fuit, prout nunc est.
Totus ergo confusus,
    { Nec dignus nominare / Nec invocare / Nec in corde cogitare } Nomen Tuum
        Absque ingenita Tua bonitate;
Sed enim illa fretus,
    { Supplex, / Demissus, / Prostratus, } revertor ad Te;

Nec peto ut facias
    Nisi quod fecisti sæpissime,
        Et facis libentissime;

    Nemo sustinebit.
Miserere peccatoris, peccatoris maximi,
  Ac proin maxima misericordia indigentis.

      Mirifica est.
Mirifica misericordiam Tuam erga me:

    Prætende mihi eam.
Nunquam ea, nunquam ea in peccatoris cujusquam venia
        Magis gloriosa { aut fuit, / aut futura est. }
Domine, si vis ut recedam a Te, da mihi alium Te,
    Alioqui non dimittam Te.

IV. Tibi, Domine, confiteor
  (Quia, si volo, abscondere non possum);
    Tibi peccata mea plurima, maxima, gravissima.
Profiteor et dolere me, quod et Tu nosti.
  Sed peccasse me plus magis prout dolor est,
    Qui mihi adjacet quo peccata defleam.
Deest mihi dolor tantus, deest plane.
Longe absum ab eo quam par est esse.
    Multum peccare possum;

        Et ultra,
Hanc enim mensuram nobis commendasti.
    Nobisne, ut nos inter nos eam præstemus,
        Tute vero eandem ut non præstes?
    Præstabis vero et multo magis.

            quam in Teipso est.

        Quantum Tu nos.

  Saltem quadringenties nonagies condonante

    Et demississime, ut par est, humillimeque

        Gauderem si magis;

## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ The whole confession is built on ISAIAH xxxviii — Hezekiah's psalm of the sick king — and head I quotes it with the PERSON CHANGED.** The Vulgate of Is. xxxviii. 15 reads *Quid dicam, aut quid respondebit mihi, cum **ipse fecerit**?* — what shall I say, when **He** hath done it? Andrewes writes *cum ipse **fecerim, fecerim, fecerim***: when **I myself** have done it, three times over. He has taken the sick king's bewilderment at what God did and turned it into the sinner's admission of what he did. **The triple must stand as a triple** — it is not a printer's dittography, and the substitution is not corrected.

2. **`Recogitabo Tibi omnia peccata mea` substitutes *peccata* for the Vulgate's *annos*.** Is. xxxviii. 15 is *Recogitabo tibi omnes **annos** meos in amaritudine animæ meæ* — "I shall go softly all my years." Andrewes: all my **sins**. Same deliberate hand as flag 1, and likewise kept.

3. **⚠ The head-VI passage at 378 EXPANDS §34's four-cell table, and the two must be read together.** §34 (printed 367) prints the bare frame *Vellem, si plus. | Metuo, ne. / Doleo, quod non. | Gauderem, si.* — four verbs of hope with their conjunctions and no objects. Here the same four verbs arrive filled in, in Andrewes' own words: *Metuo quidem ut satis. / **Cuperem ut magis**; / **Gauderem si magis**; / **Doleo, quia non magis**.* **This vindicates leaving §34's table unfilled** — the completion exists, it is Andrewes', and it is eleven pages later. Recorded in §34's flags as well; keep the English of the four verbs consistent across both sections so a reader can see the frame being filled.

4. **`Non amare, ut Petrus` — `amare` is the ADVERB, "bitterly," not the verb "to love."** Luke xxii. 62, *flevit amare*, "he wept bitterly." It sits in a list of four weepings (David washing his bed, Ps. vi. 6; the Magdalen watering his feet; Jeremiah's copious weeping; Peter's bitter weeping), and rendering it "not to love, as Peter" would be a catastrophe that reads perfectly smoothly. Flagged in the largest possible letters.

5. **`vere calcem! in aqua frigida ferventem` — the man as QUICKLIME.** Lime seethes when cold water is poured on it and is dead cold otherwise; so he boils where he should not (*extra, ubi minus oportet*) and is *frigidum, aridum, emortuum* where he most should burn. The accusatives all agree with an understood *me* from *Ah me*. **Keep the chemistry**: without the lime the four lines are only a complaint, with it they are an anatomy.

6. **The five-fold escalation of the Publican's prayer at 377 is a ladder and must not be levelled.** *Propitius esto* — **mihi peccatori** (Luke xviii. 13) → **mihi miserrimo peccatori** → **mihi peccatorum primo** (1 Tim. i. 15) → **mihi toti peccato** (not "a sinner" but *sin itself*) → **peccato καθ᾽ ὑπερβολήν** (Rom. vii. 13, sinful beyond all measure). The one Greek phrase in the section is the top rung, and it is Greek because Paul's phrase is.

7. **`quadringenties nonagies` = four hundred and ninety times**, i.e. seventy times seven made explicit (Matt. xviii. 22), two lines after *septuagies septies* and *Et ultra*. Not "many times."

8. **⚠ `Domine, si vis ut recedam a Te, da mihi alium Te, / Alioqui non dimittam Te` is Jacob at Peniel** (Gen. xxxii. 26, *non dimittam te nisi benedixeris mihi*) turned into an argument: if thou wilt have me depart from thee, give me another Thee to go to. It is the boldest sentence in the section and the English must not blunt *alium Te* into "another like thee."

9. **Internal cross-references, all deliberate, all to be kept audible in English.** (a) 374's *Nec dignus **nominare** / Nec **invocare** / Nec **in corde cogitare** Nomen Tuum* repeats §34's line at printed 366 almost word for word; (b) 375's *Non subsistet ulla caro, / Nemo sustinebit* repeats §36's two proofs (Ps. cxxx. 3, cxliii. 2) at printed 371; (c) the Magdalen/Peter/Thief triad at 373 returns at 378 as the four weepers. Use the same English wording in each place.

10. **`Et ne { … } ne me despice` (373) has the negative on BOTH sides of the brace**, as printed — *Et ne* before the three members and *ne me despice* after. Kept; it is emphasis, not dittography.

11. **`Certe { … }` (374) is a brace of five DATIVE participles** hanging on *parce pœnitenti* from the previous page — spare him that is disposed and desirous to repent, that thinks over his sins with bitterness, that is indignant with himself, that remembers thy most bitter Passion and cleaves to it. The brace crosses the page-turn; the English keeps the dependence visible without repeating the verb.

12. **The 374 *difficile / indecens / insolitum* group is an ordinary right-joining brace**, tying the three predicates to a single *Tuæ*: it is not hard to thy **power**, not unbecoming to thy **justice**, not unwonted to thy **clemency**. Three attributes, one pardon — and the English must keep all three datives, since the argument is that mercy offends none of God's attributes.

13. **No `[?]` in this section.** All eight leaves are clean, and the OCR text layer agrees with the vision transcription throughout, including *trinsabo*, *quadringenties nonagies*, and the *pumex/calcem* pair.
