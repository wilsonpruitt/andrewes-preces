## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 318(mid)–320.** *Triumphus misericordiæ* — "the triumph of mercy," the great climactic mercy-litany of Part II's penitential cycle. It has **three movements**, the two "Misericordia" headings marking the inner litanies. The governing frame is **Per et propter** ("By and for the sake of…"), carried through all.
- **Movement (a): the *Per et propter* list 1–20 (318–319).** (1) the Name and the glory of the Name (Ps 79:9); (2) the promised truth and the interposing of the oath (Heb 6:17); (3) the consolation of love, (4) the bowels of mercies (Phil 2:1); then the **seven epithets of Misericordia** (5–11): *magna* (great, Ps 51:1), *multa* (manifold, Ibid.), *antiqua* (of old, Ps 25:6), *copiosa* (plenteous, Ps 130:7), *æterna* (everlasting, Ps 136), *nimia* (exceeding, Eph 2:4, "His great love"), *mirifica* (marvellous, Ps 17:7, "shew thy marvellous lovingkindness"); (12) the riches of mercy (Eph 1:7), (13) the overflow of mercy (Eph 1:8).
- **The Greek "hyper-" escalation (319) — the rhetorical peak.** Andrewes stacks the intensives: the base words **πλεονασμός** (increase) / **περισσεία** (abundance) — glossing items 12–13, printed as labels at the head of 319 — escalate into **ὑπερπλεονασμός** (super-increase, item 14 *Excessus*, 1 Tim 1:14 "was exceeding abundant"), **ὑπερπερισσεία** (super-abundance, item 15 *Superabundantia*, Rom 5:20 "grace did much more abound"), and the adverb **ὑπερβαλλόντως / ὑπερβάλλον** (surpassingly, item 16 *Hyperbole*, Eph 2:7 "exceeding riches of His grace"). This is the grammar of grace piled past measure — keep all the Greek + gloss it so the "hyper-" crescendo lands in English. Then (17) mercy's triumph over all His works (Ps 145:9), (18) over judgment (Jas 2:13, "mercy rejoiceth against judgment"), (19) the satisfaction and merits of Christ, (20) the consolation of the Holy Spirit (Phil 2:1).
- **Movement (b): the first *Misericordia* litany (319).** Mercy that we are not consumed (Lam 3:22), *preventing / following / surrounding / pardoning / crowning* (Ps 79:8; 23:6; 32:10; 103:13; 103:4 the crowning) — the classic preventing-and-following grace. Then the **four dimensions** in a Greek-Latin brace: **Μῆκος** *Longa* (length), **Πλάτος** *Lata* (breadth), **Βάθος** *Profunda* (depth), **Ὕψος** *Alta* (height) — Eph 3:18, the dimensions of Christ's love applied to mercy. Then mercy's reach: *from everlasting* (Ps 25:6), *to everlasting* (Ps 89:1), *up to heaven* (Ps 108:4), *down to hell* (Ps 86:13, "delivered my soul from the lowest hell"), *upon all* (Rom 11:32, "concluded all… that He might have mercy upon all"), *tender* (Luke 1:78, "tender mercy of our God"). Closes on the personal turn: *Ego distuli pœnitentiam, et Tu prolongasti patientiam* ("I put off repentance, and Thou didst prolong Thy patience") — *Misericordia, fons qui nunquam potest exhauriri* ("Mercy, a fountain that can never be drained").
- **Movement (c): the second *Misericordia* litany 1–24 (320)** — mercy's sweetness and its **gospel exempla**. Its qualities: *dulcis* (sweet, Ps 69:16), *better than life* (Ps 63:3), as great as His majesty (Ecclus 2:18, *Syr.* = the edition's siglum for Sirach/Ecclesiasticus — note the bracket gloss "[i. e. *Ecclus.*]"), unto seventy times seven (Matt 18:22), hating nothing He made (Wisd 11:24), not neglecting the young ravens (Ps 147:9) nor the sparrow (Matt 10:29), willing all to be saved (1 Tim 2:4), unwilling any should perish (2 Pet 3:9). Then the **exempla** — the sheep carried on the shoulders (Luke 15:5), the house swept for the drachma (Luke 15:8), the ten thousand talents forgiven (Matt 18:32), the half-dead man's wounds bound (Luke 10:34), the glad running to meet the wicked son (Luke 15:20), Jonah freed (Jon 2:10), Peter received again (John 21:15), Thomas the doubter not cast out (John 20:27), Paul the blasphemer converted (1 Tim 1:13), the adulteress freed (John 8:11), Magdalene the sinner admitted (Luke 7:47), the thief made Thy companion in Paradise (Luke 23:43). Then mercy's posture and place: standing at the door and knocking (Rev 3:20), the Lord Himself entreating His own servants (2 Cor 5:20), *Cujus locus* — whose place is the **throne of grace** (Heb 4:16) and the **ark of propitiation** (Exod 25:22, the mercy-seat) — and *Cujus tempus dies salutis* ("whose time is the day of salvation," 2 Cor 6:2). §14 ends here; **PRÆORATIO (§15)** follows.
- **Psalm cribs Coverdale/BCP (§9)** throughout the mercy-catena (Ps 51:1 "have mercy… according to thy lovingkindness"; Ps 103:4 "who crowneth thee with mercy"; Ps 86:13 "the nethermost hell"). Wisdom/Sirach from the Apocrypha (AV Apocrypha register). *Propitiationum arca* = the mercy-seat (ἱλαστήριον), Exod 25:22 — render "the ark of atonement / the mercy-seat."
