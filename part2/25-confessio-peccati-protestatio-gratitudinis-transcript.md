# Part II — Preces Quotidianæ — §25 *Confessio Peccati et Protestatio Gratitudinis* — raw transcript

Source: 1853 Parker ed., printed pp. 348 (foot) – 350 (PDF 371–373), images `raw/end/p-NNN.jpg`. Offset **+23**, verified by eye every leaf 348–351 (no duplicates in this run). **LATIN throughout — no Greek in this section.** Opens under a fresh all-caps heading **CONFESSIO PECCATI** with the italic sub-heading *et Protestatio Gratitudinis* — the deliberate counterpart to §24's *Confessio Laudis* on the same opening. Three movements: the *minor sum* self-abasement (348 foot, Gen. xxxii. 10), the catena of unworthiness turning into the **self-oblation at the spiritual Altar** (349), and the closing *Doxologia* + the **Pater noster set out in its seven numbered petitions** (350). **Section end is hard**: a fresh small-caps heading **ORATIO DOMINICA VARIATA** opens printed 351, and is §26. Note this section carries **no *Confessio Peccati* catalogue of sins** despite its title — the confession is made by self-abasement and thanksgiving, not by enumeration (contrast §22, which bears the same name and is a full catalogue).

## Translator's flags

1. **The title is not describing a catalogue.** *Confessio Peccati et Protestatio Gratitudinis* — but no sins are enumerated here. The confession is made in the manner of Jacob (*minor sum omnibus miserationibus Tuis*), of David (*Quis ego sum … canem mortuum similem mei*), and the whole section turns on the hinge *Quid retribuam* — confession collapsing into thanksgiving. §22 bears the same name *Confessio Peccati* and is a full catalogue; these are two different things under one heading, and the English titles must not blur them.

2. **`minor sum omnibus miserationibus Tuis` (Gen. xxxii. 10)** — the Latin is "I am less than all thy mercies," the AV "I am not worthy of the least of all the mercies." Andrewes' Latin is the comparative, not the AV's superlative-of-unworthiness; translated from his Latin with the AV divergence noted rather than silently conformed.

3. **`canem mortuum similem mei` (2 Sam. ix. 8)** — AV "such a dead dog as I am." Kept AV. Note the Latin adds *et quæ domus mea* ("and what is my house") from the parallel 2 Sam. 7:18, which the AV of 9:8 does not carry: a cento of the two Davidic self-abasements, not a single citation.

4. **`et lau- / dare Te`** — printer's turned line at the foot of 349; the hyphen is kept as printed per the transcription rule (cf. §7, §21). Not a scan artifact.

5. **The self-oblation (349) is liturgical, not free composition.** *Ipse, Domine, ab anima mea suscipe spirituale hoc sacrificium: illudque recipiens ad Te in spirituale Tuum Altare, digneris vicem ejus mittere in me gratiam SS. Spiritus Tui* is the prayer of oblation from the Greek liturgies (the *ἐπουράνιον θυσιαστήριον* of the Anaphora — cf. Part I's communion devotions, printed 238–248). Render in the settled Divine-Liturgy English of those pages: "receive … upon thy spiritual Altar … vouchsafe to send down upon me in return the grace of thy Most Holy Spirit." **`SS.` = *Sanctissimi*** (Most Holy), an abbreviation, not a plural.

6. **`peccatum omne, tam voluntarium quam involuntarium`** — the liturgical pair ἑκούσιον καὶ ἀκούσιον, "voluntary and involuntary," standard in the Greek absolutions. Keep the pair; do not soften to "witting and unwitting."

7. **`Sanctifica spiritum, animum et corpus meum`** — the 1 Thess. 5:23 trichotomy, but the middle term is ***animum*, not *anima***: mind/soul as the seat of will and thought. Rendered "my spirit, my soul, and my body" following the AV of that verse, with the Latin's distinction noted here.

8. **`Ipsi gloria in Ecclesia et in Christo` (Eph. iii. 21)** — Andrewes' Latin reads "in the Church **and** in Christ"; the AV reads "in the church **by** Christ Jesus." A genuine divergence in the Latin witness, not a slip; translated as printed.

9. **The Pater noster brace (350).** A printed brace joins *Nomen* (petition 1) and *Regnum* (petition 2) to a single *Tuum.* set to the right of the brace's tip — one possessive governing both nouns. `{ Nomen / Regnum } Tuum` is written inline across the two numbered rows; ` | ` alone cannot show a join that spans rows (same problem noted at §5, printed 284). **English cannot reproduce it** — "thy Name / thy Kingdom" must repeat the possessive — so the English page repeats it and flags the loss.

10. **The seven-fold numbering is Andrewes' own**, not the 1853 editor's: the Lord's Prayer set out as seven petitions with the three expansions (*Sicut in cœlo…*, *Sicut et nos dimittimus…*) unnumbered beneath their petitions. Preserve the numerals and the indent levels — the numbering is the point of setting it here at all.

11. **`Debita nostra` / `Debitoribus nostris`** — Andrewes' Latin follows Matt. 6:12 (*debts / debtors*), not the BCP's *trespasses*. Use **debts/debtors**, against the liturgical habit; the BCP register governs the diction of this edition, not its choice of textual base.

12. **No `[?]` in this section.** Every leaf 348–351 is clean in the Google scan.
