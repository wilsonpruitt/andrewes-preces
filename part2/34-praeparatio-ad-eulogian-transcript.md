## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ The heading names the section's purpose, and the last line discloses it.** *Præparatio ad Εὐλογίαν* = **preparation for the Blessing** — Andrewes' Greek word for the Eucharist (*εὐλογία*, the cup of blessing, 1 Cor. x. 16). The section is therefore an approach to communion in four acts — **thanksgiving, hope, faith, doxology** — and it closes on **Rev. xix. 9, *Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb***, which is what the whole preparation has been walking toward. The English must let that last line land as an invitation, not as one more quotation.

2. **⚠ The macro-form: §§33–34 are a matched pair of "with them, so I."** §33 rolled the **fathers who were delivered** (as thou didst deliver Noah, Abraham …). Here Andrewes rolls first the **confessors** (David, Paul, John, Peter, Nathanael, the Samaritans, Martha, the Eunuch, the Synod, Andrew), then the **heavenly quire** (angels, all created things, the martyrs, the four and twenty elders). All three lists are exemplar-tagged in the same way, and all three are joined to the worshipper — *καὶ ἡμᾶς*, Πιστεύω, DOXOLOGIA. Keep the tag-and-saying shape identical in English across §33 and §34, or the architecture disappears.

3. **The exemplar-tags are GENITIVES depending on the heading, not vocatives or labels.** ΔΑΒΙΔ · ΠΑΥΛΟΥ · ἸΩΑΝΝΟΥ · ΠΕΤΡΟΥ · ΝΑΘΑΝΑΗΛ · ΣΑΜΑΡΕΙΤΩΝ · ΜΑΡΘΑΣ · ΕΥΝΟΥΧΟΥ · ΣΥΝΟΔΟΥ · ἈΝΔΡΕΟΥ — each hangs on **Πιστεύω**: "I believe — David's [word]: that I shall see the goodness of the Lord …" (ΔΑΒΙΔ and ΝΑΘΑΝΑΗΛ are indeclinable and take their case from the company). English renders them as possessives (DAVID'S. PAUL'S. …), which keeps the dependence visible. Same in the Doxologia: ἈΓΓΕΛΩΝ, ΚΤΙΣΜΑΤΩΝ ΠΑΝΤΩΝ, ΤΩΝ ΜΑΡΤΥΡΩΝ, ΤΩΝ ΚΔ΄. ΠΡΕΣΒΥΤΕΡΩΝ = *the angels' · all created things' · the martyrs' · the four and twenty elders'*.

4. **⚠ RULING settled on a 600-dpi crop (`raw/end/crop-upsilon-367-390.jpg`): this fount's capital upsilon is drawn with flat arms and reads as a Τ.** The plates print ΠΑΤΛΟΤ, ἸΩΑΝΝΟΤ, ΠΕΤΡΟΤ, ΕΤΝΟΤΧΟΤ, ΣΤΝΟΔΟΤ, ΜΑΡΤΤΡΩΝ, ΠΡΕΣΒΤΤΕΡΩΝ. Every one of those is an **upsilon**, not a tau — the sort is the same in ΜΑΡΤΥΡΩΝ where the first Τ *is* a tau and the second is not. **Transcribed as Υ throughout**, which is a reading of the sort, not an emendation of the text. Recorded here because a later pass looking only at the 200-dpi images will think the transcript has silently corrected the plate.

5. **`do` (366) stands alone and is kept alone.** *De ingenti tamen bonitate Tua confisus, **do*.* — the object is *laudes*, four lines down; the sentence gives before it says what it gives. English keeps the abruptness ("I give them"), and does not smooth it into "I offer thee praise."

6. **`Confiteantur … benedicant` (Ps. cxlv. 10) are jussive subjunctives** where both the AV ("shall praise … shall bless") and Coverdale ("praise thee … give thanks") are indicative. Andrewes' Latin asks for it rather than states it, so the English is jussive — *Let thy works confess thee.* CONVENTIONS §9 gives Coverdale the first crib, but not against the grammar in front of us.

7. **`[Psal. cxix. 114.]` is a wrong reference and is KEPT AS PRINTED.** *Verbum Tuum in quo mihi spem dedisti* is verbatim Vulgate Ps. cxviii. **49** (= AV cxix. 49, "Remember the word unto thy servant, upon which thou hast caused me to hope") — the same verse §21 used. Ps. cxix. 114 is *adjutor meus et susceptor meus es tu*, which this is not. Not corrected; joins the running list (`[Jer. l. 24.]` §23, `[Eph. i. 8.]` §27, `[Dan. vi. 4.]` §28).

8. **`Dominus spei` (Rom. xv. 13) for the *Deus spei* of both Vulgate and AV** ("the God of hope"). Kept as printed and rendered *the Lord of hope*; it is a small substitution but a substitution, not a variant reading we should quietly normalise.

9. **⚠ The four-cell table at the foot of 367 is elliptical and STAYS elliptical.** *Vellem, si plus. | Metuo, ne. / Doleo, quod non. | Gauderem, si.* — four verbs of hope, each with its bare conjunction and nothing after it: the grammar of hope set out as a frame to be filled by the man praying. Every completion I could supply ("I would wish, if I could hope more…") is my sentence, not Andrewes'. **Rendered bare, four cells, no supplied objects**, and the English says on the page that the blanks are the printed text. Note the chiasm: the left column holds the wish and the grief, the right the fear and the joy.

10. **⚠ Gal. ii. 16 and Jac. ii. 22 are set side by side, unreconciled, and must stay that way.** *Justified by the faith of Christ and not by the works of the law* is followed within four lines by *faith wrought with works, and by works is faith made perfect* — with 1 Tim. ii. 5–6 between them. A Jacobean bishop putting those two texts in one breath at the end of a creed is making a deliberate point. The English adds no connective, no ordering, no gloss.

11. **`ΣΥΝΟΔΟΥ ΤΩΝ ΑΠΟΣΤΟΛΩΝ ΚΑΙ ΠΡΕΣΒΥΤΕΡΩΝ`** = **the Synod of the Apostles and Elders**, i.e. Acts xv, the council at Jerusalem. Andrewes files a **council's** confession in a list otherwise made of persons, and calls it by the conciliar word. Rendered *the Synod*, not "the council of Jerusalem" and not "the apostles and elders" — the noun is the point.

12. **`ΕΥΝΟΥΧΟΥ` is the one entry not introduced by Ὅτι** — Acts viii. 37 gives it as accusative-and-infinitive (*Τὸν Υἱὸν τοῦ Θεοῦ εἶναι τὸν Ἰησοῦν Χριστόν*), because there the words follow "I believe" directly. Andrewes leaves the construction as scripture has it; the English follows suit ("that Jesus Christ is the Son of God") without regularising the row.

13. **`ΤΩΝ ΜΑΡΤΥΡΩΝ` for the multitude of Rev. vii. 10.** The Greek can be *of the witnesses*, but the company in Rev. vii is the one that came out of great tribulation, and Andrewes' own usage elsewhere in the volume is martyrological. Rendered **of the martyrs**, flagged because *witnesses* is defensible.

14. **`ἘΠΙΘΑΛΑΜΙΟΝ` is Andrewes' own label, not scripture's** — the **marriage-song**. It is the only tag in the Doxologia naming a genre rather than a singer, and it prepares the last line.

15. **Accent and sort defects on 367–369, all kept as printed.** (a) `Κυριου` in the David line is printed **unaccented**; (b) `εις` in the Paul line is printed **without its breathing** (for εἰς) — and the same word is correctly accented `εἰς` in the Martha line on the next page, so it is a sort, not a system; (c) `ΚΤΙΣΜΑΤΩΝ ΠΑΝΤΩΝ` is followed by a **damaged stop** that could be a period or a comma — transcribed `[?]`; (d) `θρόνου` is lower-case for all created things and `Θρόνου` capital for the martyrs, two lines apart — **verbatim, not regularised**. Recheck (a)–(c) on a cleaner digitization before print.

16. **No other `[?]` in this section.** The plates at 365–369 are clean apart from item 15; the duplicate scans (PDF 391–392, 395–396) were compared against the first pass and agree line for line.
