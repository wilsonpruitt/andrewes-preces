## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ This is the *Commendatio Animæ* litany, put into Greek.** The Latin commendation of a departing soul runs *Libera, Domine, animam ejus, sicut liberasti Noe de diluvio … Abraham de Ur Chaldæorum … Isaac de hostia … Job de passionibus … Danielem de lacu leonum … tres pueros de camino ignis ardentis … Susannam de falso crimine …* — one of the oldest prayers in the Western books, said at a deathbed. Andrewes has taken its form, **turned it into Greek**, extended the roll of the delivered into the New Testament (the disciples in the storm, Peter, Paul), and changed the petition from *his* soul to ***us***: `καὶ ἡμᾶς ῥῦσαι`. **The English must carry the litany's cadence** — "as thou didst deliver … so deliver us also" — because the recognition is half the meaning. A reader who knows the deathbed prayer should feel it under this page.

2. **`Sicut patres, et nos libera, Domine` is a title-line, not a heading**, printed in ordinary type and centred; it functions as the litany's refrain-formula stated once at the head instead of repeated after every name. Set it apart in English, but do not promote it to a section title.

3. **The `ἀπό` drops after David, and the bare genitive carries on alone.** Νωὲ **ἀπὸ** τοῦ κατακλυσμοῦ … Δαβὶδ **ἀπὸ** τοῦ … then Ἠλίαν **τῆς** Ἰεζαβούλ, Ἐζεκίαν **τοῦ** Ῥαψάκη, Ἐσθὴρ **τοῦ** Ἀμάν, and so to the end. A deliberate acceleration — the list tightens as it lengthens. **Do not restore the preposition** in the transcript, and let the English quicken correspondingly.

4. **Twenty-one deliverances, and the roll is Old Testament until the last four.** Noah, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Job, Moses, the people, David, Elijah, Hezekiah, Esther, Joash, Jeremiah, the three children, Jonah — then **the disciples, Peter, Paul**. Andrewes extends a litany that traditionally stops at the Maccabees; the NT names are his addition and are the hinge on which *καὶ ἡμᾶς* turns.

5. **David's brace has three lines and seven enemies** — *Σαοὺλ, Γολιάθ · Κεϊλᾶ, Ἀχιτόφελ · Ἀβσαλώμ, Δωήγ, Σεβά*. **Κεϊλᾶ is a place, not a person** (Keilah, 1 Sam. xxiii — the city whose men would have delivered David up); the rest are men. Do not regularise it into a personal name.

6. **`θηρίου` (Paul) is ambiguous and stays so.** Either the viper at Malta (Acts xxviii. 3–5) or "I have fought with beasts at Ephesus" (1 Cor. xv. 32). Rendered "the beast" without deciding; the singular slightly favours the viper, the company of *shipwreck* and *stoning* slightly favours Ephesus.

7. **`ναυηγίας` is printed thus for `ναυαγίας`** (shipwreck). Kept as printed and flagged; recheck on a cleaner scan before print.

8. **`Ἰεζαβούλ` for Jezebel** — the LXX has Ἰεζάβελ. Kept as printed.

9. **`Ἰωὰς τῆς Ἀθαλίας`** = Joash hidden from Athaliah (2 Kings xi) — the infant king in the temple. **`Ἰερεμίαν τοῦ βόθρου`** = Jeremiah from the miry dungeon (Jer. xxxviii). **`Ἰωσὴφ ἀπὸ τῆς διαβολῆς τῆς δεσποίνης`** = from **the slander of his mistress** (Potiphar's wife) — *δεσποίνη* is the mistress of the house; the brace pairs the slander with the prison it caused.

10. **`Μωϋσὴν ἀπὸ { Φαραὼ, / Λιθασμοῦ }`** — from Pharaoh, and from **stoning** (Exod. xvii. 4, "they be almost ready to stone me"). The second member is easy to miss and is not the Red Sea; that belongs to the people, on the next line.

11. **`ἐλπίζοντας ἐπὶ Σοί` — "who hope in thee," accusative plural agreeing with `ἡμᾶς`.** The last word of the section is the ground of the whole list: not that we are like the fathers, but that we hope in the same God. Keep it as a participle attached to *us*, not as a separate clause.

12. **No `[?]` in this section.** Every leaf 364–366 is clean in the Google scan; accents and breathings are legible throughout and transcribed as printed.
