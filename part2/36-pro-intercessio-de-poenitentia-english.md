## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ The English begins headless because the Latin does — the defect is in the 1853 printing.** No title, no opening petition: the intercession starts at *Pro spiritu vulneratis*, and the page before it ends a third of the way up, so nothing was crowded out. Consecutive leaves, OCR agrees. **Nothing is supplied** — not a title, not a first clause — and the gap goes on the watch list for restoration from Brightman or Harley 6616 before print. A reader of the finished book must be able to see that a page is missing here, not be smoothed past it.

2. **⚠ `præcipue non orantibus` — "and chiefly for them that pray not" — is the line the whole litany exists for**, and the English keeps it last, keeps it short, and keeps it accusative of *fact*, not of ability. **Not** "those who cannot pray": *non orantibus* is those who do not. Having named the dying, the condemned, the desolate and the endangered, Andrewes turns at the end to everyone outside the reach of his own prayer, which is the most Andrewes sentence on the page.

3. **`animam agentibus` = "them that lie a-dying."** *Animam agere* is to be giving up the ghost. It stands first in its line, between the wounded in spirit and the sick who are still taking medicine — the order is a descent, and the English keeps it.

4. **⚠ *De Pœnitentia* is an outline and stays an outline.** Contrition (with six marks) → Confession (with two) → a numbered argument in three steps: *all have sinned* (three proofs) · *what then? is it for nought?* · *God hath granted repentance unto life* (three assurances, two similitudes, six ways of God's suing). **No line is made into a sentence**, no headless phrase is given a verb, and the numbering is exactly as printed, including the nested 1/2/3 under the first head.

5. **The ellipses are kept, because the reference is the rest of the sentence.** *There is place left for pardon, **if*** [Heb. 12:17] / *sin do but couch* [Gen. 4:7]; *What then? **Is it for nought?*** [Ps. 89:47, "hast thou made all men in vain?"]; *By promising, **if***; *By threatening, **unless***. Where the English needs three words to be readable at all they are bracketed as the editor's, not run in: *If thou, Lord, [shouldest mark iniquities]*; *sin do but couch [at the door]*.

6. **`secunda tabula` = "a second plank," and it is a term of art.** Penance as the plank a man clings to after the shipwreck of sin — Jerome's *secunda post naufragium tabula*, standard in the schools, and set here beside the city of refuge. Rendering it "a second chance" or "a second table" (as of the Law) would lose the image and mislead in two different directions.

7. **`Ἀγανάκτησις` is rendered "Indignation," the same word §19 used.** It is 2 Cor. vii. 11 in both places, and §19's anatomy of godly sorrow already fixed the English: the sinner's anger turned against himself. Kept identical so the cross-reference survives translation.

8. **`Deus vero solicitat` — "And God, for his part, sueth to us."** *Solicitare* here is to press, urge, court; the six gerunds that follow are God's means of pressing (proclaiming, complaining, swearing, waiting, promising, threatening). "Solicits" in modern English has drifted too far to carry it, and "urges" loses the wooing. *Dejerando* is **swearing an oath** — Ezek. xxxiii. 11, "As I live, saith the Lord God, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked," the same verse §21 used: God under oath.

9. **⚠ The two tables at the foot read ACROSS, and the English preserves the rows.** *Prayer offereth the soul, overcometh the devil, and is toward God; fasting offereth the body, overcometh the flesh, and is toward ourselves; almsgiving offereth our goods, overcometh the world, and is toward our neighbour.* — the three Lenten works against the three enemies. The printed braces span rows (the §32 layout exception, used again and disclosed): collapsing them into inline braces would break the alignment on which the whole sense depends. *Bonum* is rendered **"our goods"**, not "the good": it is the third thing offered, beside soul and body.

10. **`Ps. 130:3` and `Ps. 143:2` take Coverdale's wording** ("who shall abide it?" / "shall no man living be justified") per §9; `Job 9:3` takes the AV's "he cannot answer him one of a thousand," there being no Coverdale for Job.
