## Translator's flags

- **Parity 3/3.** The great intercessory sweep — Church → Clergy → People → Commonwealths → Realm/City → King → magistrates/army/youth → benefactors — kept with "For …" biddings matching *Pro …*.
- **Hebrew (274):** `הושיעה נא` / `הצליחה נא` = *Hoshia-na* / *Hatzlicha-na*, Ps 118:25 (the Hosanna verse). BCP Ps 118:25: "Help me now, O Lord: O Lord, send us now prosperity." Rendered the glosses plainly ("Save now" / "Prosper now") and kept the Hebrew per §11.
- **Greek King-block (274)** translated in full (not glossed): `ὅπλῳ ἀληθείας καὶ εὐδοκίας στεφάνωσον αὐτόν` echoes Ps 5:12 LXX ("as with a shield of good pleasure hast Thou crowned us") — "with the shield of truth and of good pleasure crown him"; then "speak good things unto his heart" / "for Thy Church and Thy people."
- **Inline Greek pun (273):** `ὀρθοτομεῖν` (2 Tim 2:15, AV "rightly dividing the word") ‖ `ὀρθοποδεῖν` (Gal 2:14, "walk uprightly") — kept both Greek verbs with an em-dash gloss so the *ortho-* rhyme and the cut-straight/walk-straight antithesis survive.
- `Errantes / Incredulos` etc. (273): AV echoes — "correct them that err," "convert the unbelieving." *Scandala* → "Offences" (AV sense of σκάνδαλον), not the modern "scandals."
- Psalm cribs (§9): Ps 79:13 ("render… sevenfold into their bosom"), Ps 25:12 (BCP "his seed shall inherit the land"; pluralized here for the benefactors), Ps 41:1 (BCP "Blessed is he that considereth the poor and needy" — Andrewes pluralizes). Item 8's `apparere in justitia, et satiari gloria` recalls Ps 17:15 (Vulg. *satiabor cum apparuerit gloria tua*) — "appear in righteousness… satisfied with glory."
- `proficiant… et sapientia et gratia, apud Deum et homines` (274) = Luke 2:52 (*in wisdom and… favour with God and man*) → rendered *gratia* as "favour" to land the Lukan echo.
- `ne plus sapiat quam oportet` (273) = Rom 12:3 (AV "not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think") — kept the "wise above that it ought" cadence for the People.
- `reducere` (item 6) = "turn back/again" (the eyes of mercy that had been averted); kept the sense of returning regard.
