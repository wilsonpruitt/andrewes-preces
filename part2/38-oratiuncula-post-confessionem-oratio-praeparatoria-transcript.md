                *Oratio Præparatoria.*

## Translator's flags

1. **The sentence runs across the page-turn and must not be broken.** *Observando Sabbatum perpetuum* ends 379; *A peccato ejusque { Occasione, / Fomite, / Periculo. }* opens 380 and completes it — **a perpetual sabbath kept FROM sin and from its occasion, its fuel, and its peril.** The three brace-members are the standard moral-theology series (the occasion of sin, the *fomes peccati* or tinder, and the danger), and *fomes* is a technical term: the kindling left in us, not merely "temptation."

2. **⚠ The closing epigram is a chiasmus and the English must keep the turn.** *Sicut enim pœnitentia destruit vetera peccata, / **Sic nova peccata pœnitentiam***. Penitence destroys old sins — new sins destroy penitence. The second limb has **no verb of its own**; it borrows *destruit* across the comma, which is what makes it snap. Supplying a second verb in English is permissible only if the symmetry survives it.

3. **⚠ `Quis mihi det, ut prius moriar quam Te denuo offendam` is the section's petition and it is a request to die.** "Who will grant me that I may die before I offend thee again." Not softened, not bracketed, not turned into a wish for perseverance — this is the same register as §29's prayer for the departed, which was likewise translated plainly and left to speak.

4. **`Servator` not `Salvator`, four times in three lines** — *O Servator mi, Christe, Servator mi … Christe Servator mi, O Servator mi?* The vocative is repeated in a chiasm around the petition, and the repetition is the prayer. English keeps four occurrences and keeps the mirrored order.

5. **⚠ `metuo ne … Cupio … doleo si minus` — the four-verb frame surfaces a THIRD time.** §34 printed the empty frame (printed 367); §37 head VI filled it in (printed 378); here the *Oratio Præparatoria* uses three of the four verbs again — *Sed, Domine, **metuo ne** meum non sit; / **Cupio** certe, et **doleo** si minus.* It is evidently a habit of mind and not a one-off schema. **Translate the verbs consistently with §§34 and 37** so the recurrence is visible; do not import objects between the three places.

6. **`(sic Psaltes, Psal. cviii. 1.)` is a parenthetical attribution, not a reference tag** — "so saith the Psalmist." Andrewes quotes *Paratum cor meum, Deus* and then immediately doubts it of himself; the parenthesis is what sets up the doubt, so it must read as an aside in English, not be normalised into the bracketed-reference style used elsewhere.

7. **⚠ `Ponam peccata mea coram me, / Ut non sint coram Te` — the closing couplet turns on a preposition.** I will set my sins before **me**, that they may not be before **thee**: Ps. li. 3 (*my sin is ever before me*) answered by Ps. li. 9 (*hide thy face from my sins*). The whole logic of confession in two lines, and the English must keep *coram me* / *coram Te* in the same words, since the identity of the phrase is the argument.
