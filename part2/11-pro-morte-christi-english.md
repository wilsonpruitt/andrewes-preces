## Translator's flags

- **Parity 7/7** against the seven page-markers (309 foot, 310, 311, 312, 313, 314, 315 top). Section is *Pro morte Christi* — the Passion meditation, translated from the Latin with the parallel/inline Greek kept as main text.
- **The two opening grounds (309):** *Obedientia usque ad mortem crucis* renders Phil. 2:8 verbatim ("obedient unto death, even the death of the cross"); *Desiderio ejus coarctante* points to Luke 22:15 ("With desire I have desired to eat this passover") — the constraining desire that pressed Him to the Passion. Kept both as the double motive.
- **Ὠδῖνες / Αἰσχύνη / Κατάρα τοῦ σταυροῦ (310)** = "the pangs / the shame / the curse of the cross," Greek beside the Latin *Dolore / Pudore / Maledicto Crucis*. Rendered "Pang / Shame / Curse" to hold the three refs (Acts 2:24 the "pains" of death; Heb 12:2 the "shame" He despised; Gal 3:13 the "curse" He was made). Both layers are main text; the Greek is retained inline.
- **ῥαπίσματι (311):** kept the Greek in the English ("to buffetings (ῥαπίσματι)") — Mark 14:65, the blows of the officers; ῥάπισμα = a stroke with rod or open hand. The whole "I." block is the buffeting-catalogue; the verbs are all passive infinitives ("to be smitten… accused… mocked…"), matching the Latin's *Quod … percuti, accusari, derideri*.
- **The head-to-foot anatomy (II, 312):** fourteen members of the body, each with its Passion (head crowned, eyes weeping, ears reviled, mouth given gall, face spat on, back scourged, neck bowed, hands stretched, knees bent, feet nailed, breast grieved, heart pierced, blood flowing, soul in agony). This is the meditative heart of the section — the whole body given over. Kept the anatomical parallelism.
- **Pudor. / Dolor. (313):** the marginal one-word gloss against *Nudari* (33) and *In cruce extendi* (34) — the stripping is the **Shame**, the stretching is the **Pain**. Kept apposed in the right column.
- **Πλάνον / Ἄγνωστοι βάσανοι (314):** "to be called a deceiver (Πλάνον)" (Matt 27:63, the chief priests' word πλάνος) and the climactic "torments unknown (Ἄγνωστοι βάσανοι)" — item 10 stands with no scripture ref: the sufferings that pass all record and knowing. Kept the Greek + English; this is the summit of the suffering-list before it turns to prayer.
- **The hinge (314):** *Per pœnas Tuas … a pœnis inferni libera* — "By Thy pains … deliver my soul from the pains of hell." The pun *pœnas … pœnis* (Thy pains → hell's pains) is the pivot from catalogue to petition; rendered plainly to keep the turn.
- **The Seven Last Words (314):** given in received English liturgical form (AV/BCP) rather than back-translated from the Latin — "Father, forgive them"; "Woman, behold thy son"; "To day shalt thou be with Me in paradise"; "Eli, Eli" glossed "My God, my God"; "I thirst"; "It is finished"; "Father, into thy hands." The Latin *etc.* on the seventh preserved as "etc." (Andrewes abbreviates the commendation).
- **The five-wounds absolution-plea (314–315):** the *ignosce per illud* refrain rendered "forgive through it/them" — the wound of each member pleads pardon for the sins of the answering member of the suppliant (head's senses, hands' unlawful touch/act, side's lustful thoughts "in the heat of concupiscence," feet "swift to evil," the whole body's misused offices). The elliptical Latin (no repeated verb after *quicquid*) supplied with "[I have offended]" in brackets per §6.
- **The wound-dimensions (315):** *En { multitudinem / longitudinem / latitudinem / profunditatem eorum } a capite usque ad calcem: Et per Tua sana mea!* — "Behold the multitude, length, breadth, depth of them [my wounds], from head to heel: and by Thy [wounds] heal mine!" Isa 1:6 ("from the sole of the foot even unto the head there is no soundness") behind *a capite usque ad calcem*; the four dimensions echo Eph 3:18. Supplied "[wounds]" for the elliptical *Tua … mea*.
- **The offering-close (315):** the seven post-mortem mysteries (precious death, opening of the side, streams of blood and water, *emendicatio corporis* = "the begging of the body" [Joseph of Arimathea craving it of Pilate, Mark 15:43], the deposition, the burial *in alieno* [in another's tomb], the *triduana* [the three days' rest in the grave]). Then the offering proper: *admoneo Te* = "I put Thee in remembrance" (the anamnesis word), *offerre … Patri Tuo* = "to offer all these for me unto Thy Father" — Christ pleading His own Passion to the Father on the suppliant's behalf. Closes on the antithesis *amaritudines … Charitatem* ("bitternesses … the Love above all") — the love that outweighs all the bitterness. §11 ends here; Transfiguratio (§12) follows.
