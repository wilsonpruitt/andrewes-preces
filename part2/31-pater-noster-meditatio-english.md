## Translator's flags

1. **CORRECTION: the second brace-member on 360 is Greek — `νοΐ`, "in mind."** At 200 dpi it read as a Latin *vot*; a 600-dpi crop settles it. **All four members are Greek datives — καρδίᾳ, νοΐ, ψυχῇ, πνεύματι — "in heart, in mind, in soul, in spirit,"** with νοΐ ‖ πνεύματι taken from 1 Cor. 14:15. An earlier note in this session's resume file called it a doubtful Latin word; that is superseded.

2. **This is the third Pater noster in Part II and must not sound like the other two.** §25 sets the seven petitions bare; §26 re-says them six times out of Scripture; here each petition is **turned into a confession of not having kept it**. The governing move is *Non habui tamen* — "Yet I have not so held it" — and the English of that line should be as flat and heavy as the Latin.

3. **Petition 1's ascent is a bishop's.** The Name is to be hallowed "by all men, and by some more than by others, and by **me first of all above many**" — office confessed before failure is confessed. Keep the three rungs; softening the middle one ("by some especially") loses the discrimination Andrewes is making against himself.

4. **`facessant a me` = "let them be gone from me"** — the Latin of dismissal, sharper than "depart." The two wills braced above it are John 1:13's: *carnis* the will of the flesh, *viri* **the will of man** (the human begetter), not "of a husband."

5. **`{ a terra hac, / de terra hac, }` is a preposition-pun and gets two different English prepositions.** *A terra hac* = **by** this earth — the earth as the agent that does God's will; *de terra hac* = **upon** this earth — the earth as what God's will is done to. And the earth is himself (*quæ ego sum*): to do, and to be done to. Rendering both as "by" or both as "on" collapses the petition.

6. **`Da panem Angelorum` — the manna of Ps. 78:25, and the Eucharist.** It stands deliberately against the three temporal goods braced above (health, peace, enough): those for the body, this *unto everlasting salvation*. Keep "the bread of Angels" — not "angels' food" — so the Psalm is audible.

7. **Four figures for four failures in petition 5, kept distinct.** *Æs ingens debitorum* — the huge sum owed (the ten thousand talents, Matt. 18:24); *prolapsiones fœdas* — foul **slidings-away**; *recidivas crebras* — frequent **relapses** (a physician's word, the sickness that comes back); *volutabra diutina* — long **wallowings**, from *volutabrum*, the swine's wallowing-place (2 Pet. 2:22). Levelling them into "sins" would waste the whole line.

8. **Ps. 130 given plainly, against Coverdale's paraphrase.** The BCP has "If thou, Lord, wilt be extreme to mark what is done amiss," but Andrewes' Latin is the Vulgate's plain *Si iniquitates observaveris* — so **"If thou, Lord, wilt mark iniquities"**, with Coverdale's "who may abide it" kept for the second half. A deliberate partial departure from §9, noted.

9. **`Abyssus abyssum invocat` is inverted, and the English shows it.** Ps. 42:7 is a drowning cry; Andrewes adds the purpose-clause *Ut de abysso eripiat* so that the second deep is God's mercy and the verse becomes a plea — **"Deep calleth unto deep, that out of the deep it may pluck me forth."** The subject of "pluck" is left as vague in English as in the Latin.

10. **`ibi parce, hic ure, hic seca, Domine` — Augustine's surgeon's prayer, recast.** The tag is *hic ure, hic seca, ut in æternum parcas* ("burn here, cut here, that thou mayest spare for ever"); Andrewes re-hangs it on his two ages — **"there spare, here burn, here cut."** Keep the three imperatives bare and in his order; the whole point is that the sparing has moved to the far end.

11. **`a meis` left ambiguous — "from mine own."** Most likely his own sins, but it can mean his own people, and the Latin does not decide. The English does not decide either.

12. **The chiasmus on 363 is printed as a brace and is reproduced in full.** *{ most often, most grievously / most grievously, most often }* — the same two superlatives in both orders. It is a figure, not a variant; printing only one limb would silently edit him.

13. **`Domino Reverendo, familiaque` — "the Reverend Lord, and his household," and no more than that.** Who is meant cannot be settled from this witness, and the English must not resolve it into "my Bishop" or "my patron." Its place in the ring — after brothers and sisters, before kindred and friends — is the only evidence, and the reader can weigh it.

14. **The closing intercession widens in rings, and the cascading indents carry it**: me → parents → brethren and sisters → the Reverend Lord and household → kindred, friends, neighbourhood, country → the whole Christian people → Amen. The indentation is the argument; keep it exactly.

15. **`vel inter novissimos novissimum`** — "though among the last the very last," picking up *vel novissimum / subter pedes sanctorum Tuorum* from petition 2 on 361. The two are a deliberate echo across three pages and should use the same English word (**last**) in both places.
