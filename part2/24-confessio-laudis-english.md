## Translator's flags

1. **Title.** *Confessio Laudis* is the third heading of that name in Part II (cf. §8, and *Confessio Laudum* at §10 and §13). Englished throughout this edition as **the Confession of Praise** — *confessio* in Andrewes' usage is the whole act of owning what is true of God, of which praise and the confession of sin are the two faces; §25's *Confessio Peccati* is its deliberate counterpart on the same opening. Keeping one English word for both is the point.

2. **The estate-catalogue.** Each row is an antithesis: what God made him, against what he was spared. `Homo civilis, non Barbarus` — the antithesis is city-dwelling civility against the outlander, not "civil" in the modern manners sense; **a man of the city, not a barbarian**. `Liber, non Verna` — *verna* is the slave born in the master's house, hence **free-born, not house-born a bondman**. `Literatus, non mechanicus` = the liberal arts against the manual trades. `Educatus, non expositus` — *expositus* is the exposed child, put out to die: **brought up, not cast out**.

3. **The crow-proverb.** `non { mali corvi / malum ovum }` is the proverb *mali corvi malum ovum*, "of a bad crow, a bad egg," printed as a brace split across two lines to face the two-line *Ex honesta parentela*. The English keeps the proverb whole inside the brace rather than reproducing the printer's split, which would leave it unintelligible; the Latin page carries the split as printed.

4. **`non absorptus ab { illis, / illa, }`** — the brace pairs by number: *illis* answers *periculis* (perils, plural), *illa* answers *infamia* (infamy, singular). English **them / it** preserves the pairing.

5. **`ut neque adulari opus habeam, neque mutuo accipere`** — the point of "an honest estate" is independence: he need neither flatter a patron nor borrow. Rendered plainly; *mutuo accipere* is "to borrow," not "to receive in exchange."

6. **Scripture given in AV form.** 1 Pet. 1:3–4 ("begotten us again unto a lively hope … reserved in heaven"), Eph. 1:3, and 2 Cor. 1:4–5 are the AV verbatim, with Andrewes' first-person adjustments kept (*benedixisti mihi*, "hast blessed **me**"; *consolatio mea*, "**my** consolation"). Dan. 2:23 is not AV-verbatim: Andrewes' Latin has *secundum partem aliquam* ("in some measure") and *sermonem aperuisti mihi* ("hast opened unto me the matter") where the AV reads "hast made known unto us the king's matter" — translated from his Latin, in AV register, with the divergence noted here.

7. **The `Opus manuum` pair-table.** Six titles in two columns, then two centred: what he is by creation on the left, what he is by redemption on the right. *Servus emptionis* = **the servant of thy purchase** (the bought slave, answering *pretium sanguinis*), not "a bought servant" loosely.

8. **The *Divisio* is printed in two columns but reads as one running outline** — the left column entire, then the right, which takes up *Deprecatio* with its objects (*Peccati, Pœnæ*). The transcript keeps the printed rows (page-faithful, per the mirror rule); this English page sets them in the running order and says so in a note above the block, because a reader given the printed rows alone will read *Compellatio | Peccati* as a pair and the outline will make no sense. **This is the one place in the section where the English departs from the printed row-order, deliberately.**

9. **The 348 columns are true parallels** and are left in printed order: a second scheme of the parts of prayer on the left (with the connective formulae *Quia / Propter vel Per / Secundum / Et annunciabo*, and the alternative opening *Vel sic*), and on the right a single list headed *Per.* — the divine acts *through* which the prayer is offered, Creation through Inspiration. The two right-column lines with no left member are kept as printed.

10. **Ἄσκησις = the Exercise.** The one Greek word in the section, and not "asceticism": the discipline proper to a man's state, daily or nightly, penitential for evils and thankful for goods, then *peculiaris* — particular to age, calling, and estate. The brace *si { senex; / in Clero; / Episcopus. }* is Andrewes' own case set as a condition, and is left conditional in English ("if an old man; in the Clergy; a Bishop") rather than resolved into a statement about himself.

11. **`Peccantem me … et non pœnitentem`** — the accusative opens the sentence and hangs until *Magis deceret* ("it were more fitting"). English keeps the suspension ("Me, that sin … it were more fitting to lie prostrate") rather than reordering into a subject-first sentence: the self-accusation must stand before the verb, as it does in the Latin, for the *Tamen* ("Yet") to turn it.
