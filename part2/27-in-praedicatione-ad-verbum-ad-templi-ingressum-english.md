## Translator's flags

1. **Titles.** *In Prædicatione* = **In Preaching** (not "on preaching" — it is the devotion used *in* the act); *Ad Verbum* = **At the Word**, i.e. at the opening of Scripture; *Ad Templi ingressum* = **At the Entering of the Temple**. Andrewes' *Templum* throughout is the church he is walking into, and is kept as **Temple** because the psalms he is quoting say so.

2. **The figure-table reads left-to-right as thing-then-figure.** The World *is* the Sea; Men, the Fishes; the Church, the Boat; the Preacher, the Fisherman; the Word, the Net. *Lembus* is the small fishing boat of Luke 5, so **Boat**, not "ship" or "vessel."

3. **`pruna geminæ naturæ` = "O thou Coal of the twofold Nature."** Isaiah 6:6 — the live coal taken with the tongs from the altar and laid on the prophet's lips, read as the incarnate Word in whom Godhead and manhood are one; the same coal the Greek liturgies see laid on the communicant's lips. **Capitalised as a vocative to Christ**, because that is what the Latin's *O Tu* makes it. Rendering it "burning coal of the two natures" would be accurate and dead; the address must survive.

4. **`ter sancti` = thrice-holy**, the Trisagion word — not "most holy."

5. **`Illumina sensus cordis` is Eph. 1:18 in substance, printed as 1:8.** Rendered with Coverdale's/AV's "the eyes of your understanding being enlightened" behind it — "Lighten the understanding of my heart" — and the reference is left as the 1853 prints it. See the transcript flag; the edition does not silently correct references.

6. **Psalm-lines in Coverdale (§9), and this section is nearly all psalm.** 119:18 ("O open thou mine eyes: that I may see the wondrous things of thy law"), 119:12 ("Blessed art thou, O Lord: O teach me thy statutes"), 51:15 ("Thou shalt open my lips, O Lord: and my mouth shall shew thy praise" — recast as the imperative Andrewes has), 81:10 ("open thy mouth wide, and I shall fill it"), 5:7 ("I will come into thine house, even upon the multitude of thy mercy: and in thy fear will I worship toward thy holy temple"), 28:2, 48:9 ("We wait for thy loving-kindness, O God: in the midst of thy temple"), 26:8, 26:7, 27:4 ("to behold the fair beauty of the Lord, and to visit his temple"), 27:8 ("My heart hath talked of thee, Seek ye my face: Thy face, Lord, will I seek"), 118:19.

7. **`Ego in multitudine misericordiæ Tuæ accedam` keeps its emphatic *Ego***, which Coverdale renders "But as for me, I will come into thine house." The English here keeps the plainer "I, in the multitude of thy mercy, will come" so the line-keying holds; the emphasis is in the word order.

8. **`voluptatem Domini` (Ps. 27:4)** — Coverdale's "the fair beauty of the Lord," Vulg. *voluptatem*. Rendered **the delight of the Lord**, keeping Andrewes' noun rather than importing Coverdale's phrase, because *decorem* (beauty) is used two lines earlier for a different word and the distinction should not collapse.

9. **The commemoration (356) is the liturgical diptych.** *Comprecantium mihi* = **praying together with me**, not "praying for me" — the brethren stand about him and pray *with* him; the absent are absent "for reasonable causes," the anaphora's own formula. Keeping both halves is what makes this a priest's prayer at the church door rather than a private meditation.

10. **The last two lines are a deliberate repetition, and the English repeats itself exactly.** The Greek couplet at the foot of 356 is LXX Ps. 25:8 — the same verse given in Latin twelve lines above. Andrewes seals the devotion by saying it once more in Greek; **the English of the two must be word-for-word identical**, or the seal reads as two different verses. This is the one place in the section where identical English on two lines is correct and required.

11. **`Aperite mihi portas justitiæ` is plural — "Open me the gates"** — addressed to the gatekeepers of Ps. 118, not to God. The English keeps the plural imperative without a named addressee, as the Latin does.
