## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 270–272**, opening with the caps title PRECES MATUTINÆ. Ends 272 with "ad thronum gratiæ Tuæ." (whitespace after); printed 273 begins the **Intercessio** (*Pro Ecclesia Catholica*).
- **This is a psalm-catena**: nearly every couplet is a Vulgate psalm-verse with its ref on the right. Apply CONVENTIONS §9 rigorously — crib the Coverdale/BCP Psalter first: Ps 65:2 ("O thou that hearest the prayer, unto thee shall all flesh come"), Ps 55:17 ("evening, and morning, and at noonday"), Ps 5:3, Ps 141:2 ("Let my prayer be set forth in thy sight as the incense"), Ps 63:7 ("thou hast been my helper"), Ps 123:2 ("as the eyes of servants… of a maiden…"), Ps 91:11 ("give his angels charge over thee"), Ps 25:4, Ps 119:133, Ps 17:5, Ps 119:132, Ps 42:7 ("One deep calleth another"). BCP wording is the target where it agrees with the Vulgate.
- **Inline Greek glosses** (§17): `φιλάνθρωπε` glosses *Amator hominum* (Tit. 3:4, "lover of men"); `πολυεύσπλαγχνος` glosses *Multorum bonorum viscerum* (Jas. 5:11, "of tender mercy"). Keep the Greek in the English line as an apposed gloss, per §11 (never silently drop a script).
- `abyssus abyssum invocat` (272) = Ps 42:7 Vulg./Coverdale "One deep calleth another" — but Andrewes then coins `Abyssus miseriæ nostræ abyssum misericordiæ tuæ` ("the deep of our misery [calleth] the deep of Thy mercy"), an antithetical echo, NOT the psalm; keep the coined line distinct.
- The `peccatorum { primo / miserrimo }` brace (272) puns 1 Tim 1:15 (*peccatorum primus*, "chief of sinners") against a superlative *miserrimo* ("most wretched") — hold both in the brace.
- `Superexaltet misericordia Tua justitiam Tuam` (272) = Jas 2:13 "mercy rejoiceth against judgment" (AV) / *superexultat* — render so mercy "exalts itself above" / "triumphs over" Thy justice.
