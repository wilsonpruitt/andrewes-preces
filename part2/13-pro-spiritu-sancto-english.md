## Translator's flags

- **Parity 3/3** against the three page-markers (316 mid, 317, 318 top). A second *Confessio Laudum* heading opens the **Pro Spiritu Sancto** thanksgiving — the Spirit's whole work, from the *Veni Creator* to the final perfecting.
- **Veni Creator (316):** given in the received English hymn form ("Come, Creator Spirit, / visit the minds of Thine own, / fill with grace from on high / the hearts which Thou hast made") rather than a fresh translation, per the hymn's fixed liturgical identity. Andrewes quotes only the first stanza.
- **The four visible comings (316–317):** rendered by the left-margin labels — **Shadow** (the overshadowing at the Conception, Luke 1:35), **Dove** (the Baptism, Matt 3:16), **Breath** (*Habitus* — the breathing on the Apostles, John 20:22), **Fiery tongues** (Pentecost, Acts 2:3). *Habitus* is hard: literally "manner/bearing," here the *form* of Christ's breathing — rendered "Breath" for the visible sign. Kept the labels as a left column.
- **The -κλησις wordplay (317)** is the heart of the section and is preserved in full: the Spirit's threefold visitation matched Latin-to-Greek —
  - **In calling** = **Ἔκκλησις** (calling-out) / **Ἀνάκλησις** (calling-back) / **Ἐπανάκλησις** (re-calling), beside the Latin "calling-away from sin / calling-forth from the world / calling-back from relapse";
  - **In invocation** = **Ἐπίκλησις** (the calling-upon — the epiclesis);
  - **In advocacy** = **Παράκλησις** (the Paraclete's comfort/advocacy).
  The Greek kept + parenthetically glossed so the *vocatio / ‑κλησις* echo carries into English. This is Andrewes the Greek scholar at play: the Spirit is the God who calls, is invoked, and comforts — all one root.
- **Divisio (317):** 1 Cor 12:4–6, the dividing of gifts / ministrations / operations, then named *charismata / opera / fructus* ("gifts, works, fruits"). Kept the brace and the re-naming.
- **The works (317–318):** *compunctio redarguentis* = "the compunction of Him that convinceth" (John 16:8, the Spirit's conviction of sin); *unctio docentis* (1 John 2:20, "ye have an unction"); the bringing-to-remembrance (John 14:26); the shedding-abroad of love (Rom 5:5); the help of weakness in prayer (Rom 8:26, "the Spirit itself maketh intercession"); the witness of adoption (Rom 8:16); the sealing (2 Cor 1:22); the *arrhabo* / earnest (Eph 1:14, ἀρραβών, "the earnest of our inheritance") — rendered "the earnest of experience."
- **The seven-fold inward work with glosses (318):** Visitation (→ "to visit the heart"), Indwelling, Purging, Enlightening (→ "illumination"), Strengthening, Adorning, Perfecting (→ "a bringing-home"). The right-column glosses kept apposed. This traces the Spirit's inward course from first visit to final home-bringing (*perductio* = the leading-all-the-way-home). Close on *Dux veritatis* (John 16:13, "He will guide you into all truth") and *Subministratio virtutis* ("the supply of power"). §13 ends; Triumphus misericordiæ (§14) follows.
