Opens under the Greek heading **Πατὲρ ἡμῶν** and works the Lord's Prayer through in its **seven numbered petitions**, each turned from petition into confession: what the petition asks, then that he has not kept it, then the plea. This is the **third** treatment of the Pater noster in Part II — after §25's bare seven-fold setting (printed 350) and §26's six scriptural re-sayings (351–354) — and the only one that is a meditation. **Section end is hard**: the Amen at the foot of the intercession closes it, and a fresh italic heading *Articulorum Fidei Ἀνακεφαλαίωσις.* opens on 363, which is §32.

## Translator's flags

1. **`νοΐ`, not `vot` — CORRECTION, verified at 600 dpi.** The second member of the 360 brace is Greek, not Latin: **νοΐ**, dative of νοῦς, printed with the iota's diacritic. Re-rendered as a crop at 600 dpi (`raw/end/crop-vot-383.jpg`) after the 200-dpi image left it doubtful; the nu is unmistakable at that size. So **all four members are Greek datives — καρδίᾳ, νοΐ, ψυχῇ, πνεύματι** (heart, mind, soul, spirit), and the pairing νοΐ ‖ πνεύματι is 1 Cor. xiv. 15 (*ψαλῶ τῷ πνεύματι, ψαλῶ δὲ καὶ τῷ νοΐ*). **A resume note earlier in this session recorded it as a Latin `vot[o]` and flagged it doubtful; that read was wrong and is corrected here.**

2. **The third Pater noster of Part II, and the only meditation.** §25 (printed 350) sets the seven petitions bare and numbered; §26 (351–354) re-says them six times out of six quarters of Scripture; this one takes them one at a time and **turns each petition into a confession that he has not kept it** — *Non habui tamen* under petition 1 is the pattern for the whole. The three must read as three different things in English; do not harmonise their diction.

3. **Petition 1 is Greek then Latin, and the seam is the argument.** Ἅγιος εἶ σύ / ἅγιον τὸ ὄνομά σου ὑπὲρ πᾶν ὄνομα (Phil. ii. 9) states the fact in Greek; the Latin then says by whom that Name ought to be hallowed — *ab omnibus, et a quibusdam plus magis aliis, meque imprimis super multos* ("by all, and by some more than others, and by me first of all above many") — which is a bishop confessing his office before confessing his failure. Keep the ascent: all → some → me.

4. **`Non habui tamen:` is the hinge of the whole section** and is printed on its own line, deeply indented. *Habere* here = to hold [as holy], picking up *habendum* three lines above. "Yet I have not so held it:" — the colon must stay, because what follows is the specification.

5. **`facessant a me` (petition 3)** — *facesso* in the imperative/subjunctive of dismissal: **be gone, get them hence**, not merely "depart." The two wills are John i. 13's (*neque ex voluntate carnis, neque ex voluntate viri*), so *viri* is "of man" in the sense of the human male begetter, not "of a husband."

6. **`fiat { a terra hac, / de terra hac, } quæ ego sum` — a preposition-pun that must be kept.** *A terra hac* = **by** this earth (the earth as agent, doing God's will); *de terra hac* = **concerning/upon** this earth (the earth as object, having God's will done to it). And *quæ ego sum* — "which I am": the earth in question is himself. Two prepositions, one clod. English needs two distinct renderings and cannot use "by" twice.

7. **`Da panem Angelorum` (petition 4)** — Ps. lxxviii. 25, *panem angelorum manducavit homo*, the manna; read here (as everywhere in Andrewes) toward the Eucharist, and set against the three merely temporal goods braced above it (*sanitatem, pacem, sufficientiam*). The contrast — health/peace/enough for the body, the Angels' bread *ad salutem æternam* — is the petition's whole shape.

8. **The debt-vocabulary of petition 5 escalates and must not be levelled.** *Æs ingens debitorum* = the huge sum of debt (Matt. xviii. 24, the ten thousand talents); *prolapsiones fœdas* = foul slidings-away; *recidivas crebras* = frequent relapses (a medical word — the sickness that returns); ***volutabra diutina*** = **long wallowings**, the *volutabrum* being the swine's wallowing-place (2 Pet. ii. 22, *sus lota in volutabro luti*). Four different figures for four different failures.

9. **`Abyssus abyssum invocat … Ut de abysso eripiat` — Andrewes inverts the verse.** Ps. xlii. 7 is a cry of drowning ("one deep calleth another"); here the second *abyssus* is God's mercy, and the whole becomes a plea: deep calls to deep **so that it may pluck me out of the deep**. The added purpose-clause is his, not the psalm's. Translate so the inversion is visible.

10. **`ibi parce, hic ure, hic seca, Domine` is Augustine, and is famous.** *Hic ure, hic seca, ut in æternum parcas* (Enarr. in Ps.; the surgeon's prayer — burn here, cut here, that thou mayest spare hereafter). Andrewes has re-cast it around his two *sæcula*: **there spare, here burn, here cut**. Keep the imperatives bare and in that order; a reader who knows the tag must hear it.

11. **`a meis` (362) is ambiguous and stays ambiguous.** *Libera me, Domine, a meis* — "from mine own": most naturally his own sins (answering *Libera animam meam* on the next line), but *mei* can mean one's own people. Rendered "from mine own" without resolution.

12. **The chiasmus-brace on 363 holds the same two words in both orders.** *quia { sæpissime, gravissime / gravissime, sæpissime } peccavi Tibi* — most often-most grievously, most grievously-most often. It is not a variant reading and not an accident: the brace makes the two superlatives commute. **Print both limbs in both orders in English.**

13. **`Domino Reverendo, familiaque` (363) — uncertain, flagged not resolved.** In the closing intercession, between *fratribus, sororibus* and *Cognatione, amicis*, stands "the Reverend Lord, and his household." Whose — his diocesan? his patron? the master of the house he serves in? — cannot be settled from this witness. Rendered literally and flagged; **do not domesticate it into "my Bishop."**

14. **The intercession widens by rings and the widening is the form**: me → parents → brothers and sisters → the Reverend Lord and household → kindred, friends, neighbourhood, country → the whole Christian people → Amen. Preserve the cascading indents, which do the widening typographically.

15. **No `[?]` in this section**, after the 600-dpi check on 360. Every leaf 360–363 is otherwise clean in the Google scan.
