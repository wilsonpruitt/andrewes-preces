# Part II — Preces Quotidianæ — §1 *Duo in me cognosco* — English

Line-keyed English of `01-duo-in-me-cognosco-transcript.md` (printed 267–269). **Latin-only section → translated from the Latin** (CONVENTIONS §6). Register: thou-form AV/BCP (§8–10); one English line per source line, same indent, same brace structure. Parity target: 3 pages / 3 pages.

<!-- printed 267 (PDF 288) -->

TWO things I know in myself, O Lord,
        The nature, which Thou madest,
        The sin, which I have added thereto;
I confess that by my fault I have marred the nature,
    but remember that I am a wind that passeth away,
            and cometh not again;   [Ps. 78:39]
For of myself I cannot come back from sin.
        O come! take from me what I have done,
        Let there abide in me what Thou hast done;
That it perish not, which Thou hast redeemed with Thy precious blood.
        O come! let not my wickedness destroy what Thy good-
            ness hath redeemed.
O Lord my God, if I have wrought that I should be guilty before Thee,
Could I ever have wrought that I should not be Thy servant?
If thereby I have taken innocence from myself,
Have I thereby also made an end of Thy mercy?
If I have committed that whereby Thou couldest condemn me,
Hast Thou also lost that whereby Thou art wont to save?
It is true, O Lord, my conscience deserveth damnation,
    But Thy mercy surpasseth every offence.

<!-- printed 268 (PDF 289) -->

    Spare me therefore,
Because it is not { hard for Thy power / unseemly for Thy justice / strange to Thy clemency },
        to spare him that offendeth.
Thou who hast { made / redeemed } me, { destroy / condemn } me not.
Thou who hast made me by Thy goodness,
    Let not Thy work perish by mine iniquity.
        Own in me what is Thine,
            And take from me what is mine.
Look upon me, unhappy,
        O boundless Pity:
        upon me, wicked,
            O Mercy open to all.
Sick, I come to the Almighty,
    Wounded, I run to the Physician.
Keep for me the gentleness of Thy compassion,
    Thou who so long hast held back the sword of vengeance.
Blot out the multitude of my crimes,
    Renew the multitude of Thy compassions.
However much I am
        { unclean, / blind, / sick, / yea, though dead, } Thou canst { cleanse / enlighten / heal / raise } me.
Whatsoever I am, whether good or evil,
        I am ever Thine.
If Thou { cast me out / despise me }, who shall { receive / regard } me?

<!-- printed 269 (PDF 290) -->

Thou canst more { forgive / spare } than I can { commit / sin }.
    Let not hurtful delight oppress me,
    At the least, let not perverse custom overwhelm me.
        From depraved and unlawful desires,
        From vain, hurtful, unclean thoughts,
        From the illusions of malignant spirits,
        From the pollutions of mind and body.

## Translator's flags

- **Parity 3/3.** Section is one continuous prayer; no internal caps-opener breaks.
- `spiritus vadens, non rediens` (267) → AV/BCP "a wind that passeth away, and cometh not again" (Ps. 78:39). Coverdale/BCP Psalter crib per §9: "he remembereth that they are but flesh; and that they are even a wind that passeth away, and cometh not again." Kept the BCP wording exactly.
- `Eja!` → "O come!" (hortatory, urging God to act), not "Alas" — distinguished from lament *Eheu/Heu*.
- **The genitive-shared brace** on 268 (`{ difficile potentiæ / indecens justitiæ / insolitum clementiæ } Tuæ`): the trailing *Tuæ* governs all three genitives; folded "Thy" into each English item ("Thy power / Thy justice / Thy clemency") so the brace reads without a dangling possessive. *insolitum* = "unwonted/unusual" → "strange to."
- **Ordered brace-pairs preserved:** *creasti→perimas, redemisti→condemnes* (made→destroy, redeemed→condemn); *immundus→mundare … mortuus→resuscitare* (unclean→cleanse … dead→raise); *ejicis→recipiet, despicis→respiciet* (cast out→receive, despise→regard); *remittere/parcere ‖ committere/peccare* (forgive/spare ‖ commit/sin). Item order held in both braces so each antithesis lands on its partner.
- `Pietas immensa` / `Misericordia publica` (268) are vocative apostrophes to God-as-attribute → "O boundless Pity" / "O Mercy open to all" (*publica* = public, available to all, not "common/vulgar").
- The closing `A pravis… A vanis… Ab illusionibus… A pollutionibus…` (269) is an ablative-of-separation litany (an implied *libera me* / *ab his…*) → rendered as bare "From …" petitions, matching its litany shape; section ends mid-page.
- `numerositatem criminum` / `multitudinem miserationum` (268): deliberate *number ↔ multitude* pairing (my crimes' count blotted, Thy mercies' multitude renewed) — kept "multitude" for both to hold the echo, since English lacks a graceful "numerousness."
