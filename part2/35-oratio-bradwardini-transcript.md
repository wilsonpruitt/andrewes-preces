                ORATIO THO. BRADWARDINI,

            Lib. I. De Virtute, cap. i. coroll. 39.

  Teipsum, Deus meus, Teipsum propter Teipsum super
alia cuncta amo.  Teipsum desidero.  Teipsum finaliter
concupisco.  Teipsum propter Teipsum, non aliud quod-
libet, semper et in omnibus quæro.  Ex totis meis præ-
cordiis et medullis, cum gemitu et ploratu, cum labore
continuo, et dolore.  Quid ergo finaliter mihi reddes?
Si non Teipsum mihi reddideris, nihil reddis.  Si non
Teipsum mihi donaveris, nihil donas.  Si non Teipsum
invenio, nihil invenio.  Nequicquam me præmias, sed
crucias vehementer.  Prius enim quam Te quæsivi, spe-
ravi Te tandem invenire, et tenere.  Et hac spe melliflua
in omnibus laboribus meis dulciter consolabar.  Nunc
autem si Teipsum mihi negaveris, quicquid aliud dederis,
tanta spe frustratus (nec ad tempus modicum, sed in
perpetuum) nonne semper amore languebo, languore
mœrebo, mœrore dolebo, dolore lugebo et flebo, quia
semper inanis et vacuus remanebo?  Nonne inconsola-
biliter contristabor?  incessabiliter querulabor?  inter-
minabiliter cruciabor?  Non est hoc Tuum, optime, cle-
mentissime, amantissime Deus; nullatenus congruit, nus-
quam decet.  Fac igitur, optime Deus meus, ut in vita
præsenti semper Teipsum propter Teipsum amem præ
omnibus, quæram in omnibus, et tandem in futura in-
veniam ac teneam in æternum.

## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ A borrowed prayer, named — the only attributed author in Part II so far.** Everything around it is Andrewes' own compilation of scripture and liturgy; here he copies out **Thomas Bradwardine** (Archbishop of Canterbury, d. 1349) and prints the citation. The English must keep the heading and citation exactly as printed and must not absorb the prayer into Andrewes' voice: the point of the page is that a bishop of 1620 found a fourteenth-century archbishop had already said it.

2. **The citation is kept as printed: `Lib. I. De Virtute, cap. i. coroll. 39.`** The work is Bradwardine's *De Causa Dei contra Pelagium*, whose Book I concerns the divine goodness; "De Virtute" is how the 1853 has it. **Not corrected, not expanded** — the standing rule on references (cf. `[Jer. l. 24.]` §23, `[Eph. i. 8.]` §27, `[Dan. vi. 4.]` §28, `[Psal. cxix. 114.]` §34).

3. **⚠ First prose in Part II — the print pipeline needs to know.** Every other section transcribed so far is set in sense-lines with hanging indents, and `tools/transcript2tex.py` will eventually have to distinguish the two. The line-breaks recorded above are **compositor's justification only**; the English does not line-key to them (see the English file's header). Flagged here because a page-fit failure in the mirror layout would be silently caused by treating this page like the others.

4. **⚠ NEW WATCH ITEM — the 370/371 join: the next section's HEADING IS MISSING in the printing.** Printed 370 ends *in æternum* a third of the way up the page; printed 371 opens cold with **`Pro spiritu vulneratis, animo ægris, perplexis.`** — the middle of an intercession litany, with no heading and no first member, where every other piece in Part II carries one. **Confirmed not a scan artifact**: the leaves are consecutive (PDF 397, 398), there is no duplicate in this stretch, and the OCR text layer (`raw/preces1853_djvu.txt`) reads the same two pages in the same order with nothing between them. Restore from Brightman/Harley or a cleaner digitization before print. Recorded in `next-session-resume.md` §Watch items.

5. **The rhetorical machinery that the English must carry, since it is the whole prayer.** (a) ***Teipsum*** eight times, always in the emphatic first position — Thyself, thyself, thyself; (b) the **gradatio** *amore languebo, languore mœrebo, mœrore dolebo, dolore lugebo et flebo*, where each verb's noun becomes the next clause's cause; (c) the three **-biliter** adverbs in a row, *inconsolabiliter · incessabiliter · interminabiliter*; (d) *finaliter* used twice, of the longing and of the reward. None of these is ornament — they are the argument.

6. **No `[?]` on this page.** The plate is clean and the OCR agrees with the vision transcription word for word, including *Nequicquam*, *melliflua*, and the *mœrebo/mœrore* pair.
