# Part II — Preces Quotidianæ — §41 *Oratio præparatoria ante* Εὐλογίαν + *Monita et Meditationes præparatoriæ in Vespertina ad Deum elevatione mentis* (→ *Scrutinium et Inquisitio, vel Examen*) — raw transcript

Source: 1853 Parker ed., printed pp. 384 (mid) – 387 (PDF 415, 416, 421, 422), images `raw/end/p-NNN.jpg`. Offset **+31 at 384–385, +35 at 386–387** — see flag 1; **every leaf eye-verified**, and printed 385 carries the signature `AND. PRECES. — 3 D`.
**LA + GK + HEBREW.** Two pieces filed together per the few-short-sections rule: a twelve-line prayer, then the evening self-examination, which is the substantial part and runs to the end of the penitential matter.
**⚠ The *Monita* is a SCHEMA, not a prayer** — like §24's *Divisio* and §36's *De Pœnitentia*. It is a rule of life with its authorities attached (Augustine, **Cicero**, **Seneca**, **Ausonius out of Pythagoras**, **Gregory of Nyssa**, and a **Rabbi**), and it carries the deepest nested brace in Part II.
Section boundary: begins mid-384 with the italic heading *Oratio præparatoria ante* Εὐλογίαν, directly after §40's cue-line close; ends on 387 at *…perfectus virtutis habitus generetur.*, below which a printed rule and an **editorial note announcing the hymns** close the whole penitential sequence. **§42 = the ΥΜΝΟΣ ΕΩΘΙΝΟΣ at printed 388, which returns to Part I's Greek-verso / Latin-recto mirror** — a different discipline from all of Part II.

## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ OFFSET: +31 at printed 384–385, +35 at printed 386–387.** The spread **384/385 is scanned three times** (PDF 415/416, 417/418, 419/420) and the spread **386/387 twice** (PDF 421/422, 423/424). Every leaf was eye-verified against the running head; 385's signature `AND. PRECES. — 3 D` confirms +31 independently. This is the **second offset break in two sections** (+27→+31 at printed 382, now +31→+35 at printed 386), and it is exactly the balloon STRUCTURE.md's anchor table predicted for 384–387.

2. **⚠⚠ NEW WATCH ITEM — THE HEBREW AT PRINTED 386 IS GARBLED IN THE PLATE, and is reconstructible.** The line reads as set:
   **ראה חזתלת התועלת והתשועה נכזבה**
   The second word, **חזתלת**, is not a Hebrew word. What the sentence requires is **תוחלת** (*tocheleth*, "hope") — and Andrewes' own Latin gloss on the facing page proves it: *Ecce **spes** fructus et salutis…*, "Behold, the **hope** of profit and of salvation." The corruption is a compositor's, and its mechanism is visible: **two sorts transposed (ת ↔ ח) and a zayin set for a vav (ז for ו)** — vav and zayin are near-identical sorts, and the whole word is upside-down work for a compositor with no Hebrew. The intended line is therefore **ראה תוחלת התועלת והתשועה נכזבה** — "See, the hope of the profit and of the deliverance is proved false."
   **Confirmed a PLATE defect, not a scan artifact:** the duplicate scan at PDF 423 reads the same five garbled letters (`raw/end/crop-hebrew-386-dup-423.jpg`), and the reading was taken at 600 and 900 dpi (`crop-hebrew-386-421.jpg`, `crop-heb386-w2b-421.jpg`).
   **Kept exactly as printed in the transcript, per the standing rule.** The English brackets the reconstruction and discloses it. **Restore from Brightman/Harley or a cleaner digitization before print** — this is the fifth watch item and the only one that garbles a whole word of a script the reader cannot check.

3. **`Rab. J.` is not identified here.** The tag is all the 1853 gives — presumably *Rabbi J[ehuda]* or *R. Jonah* (Jonah Gerondi's *Sha'arei Teshuvah* is the obvious candidate for a saying about not deferring repentance till the morrow), but **the edition does not say and neither do we.** Left as printed, unexpanded, and put on the pre-print verification list beside §39's Fulgentius. The English keeps the abbreviation.

4. **⚠ LAYOUT EXCEPTION, the deepest yet: the *Quid hodie* brace on 385 is FOUR levels deep and uses the ` | ` encoding** (the §32 / §36 exception, disclosed again). The structure as printed: *Quid hodie* governs **two branches** — first a 2×2 brace of the day's four acts (*Egeris / Dixeris* ‖ *Legeris / Scripseris*, doing and saying alongside reading and writing), then *quod* with four consequences, two of which open braces of their own (*vel regimen { animi / corporis }*, *operetur salutem { tuam / aliorum }*). Collapsing any level to an inline brace loses the fact that **the four acts are questioned under all four consequences at once** — that is what the tall brace means. Printed rows are kept throughout (including *patrem, etc.* and *augeat scientiam.* on their own turned lines).

5. **The `In bello / cantus est` figure is the section's key and its two Greek words must not be flattened.** In war the trumpet-call is either **ἐγκελευστικός** — the *charge*, "fitted for the doing" — or **ἀνακλητικός** — the *recall*, "whereby the stragglers are called back." Andrewes then applies it: the mind is roused in the morning and, at evening, **called back as by a recall (*quasi anacletico*) to itself and to its own captain**. Note the 1853 latinises the second word as ***anacletico*** in the application line while printing it in Greek in the brace — kept both ways as printed. The military sense governs the whole *Monita*: the evening examination is a **recall**, not a reveille.

6. **`Βαθεῖα ἡ καρδία παρὰ πάντα` and `Cor profundum est et tortuosum` are NOT an exact doublet** (contrast §27's closing couplet). The Greek is LXX Jer. xvii. 9, "the heart is deep beyond all things"; the Latin adds *et tortuosum*, crooked, from the Vulgate's *pravum … et inscrutabile*. **Both lines stand and are translated distinctly** — the English must not conform one to the other, because Andrewes is stacking two readings of the same verse (deep, and crooked) and the third line, *Homo vetus mille involucris obtegitur*, depends on both.

7. **⚠ NEW RULING — same-line Greek + Latin gloss is rendered ONCE; a separated doublet is rendered twice.** Twice in this section the plate gives a Greek phrase and its Latin equivalent **on the same line**: *Itaque πρόσεχε σεαυτῷ. Attende tibi ipsi.* (385) and the Nyssa brace *κοιμωμένων φύλαξ, dormientium custos* (386). These are **glosses for the reader, not two texts**, and printing the same English twice on one line reads as a compositor's error rather than as a seal. This **refines §27's ruling** rather than contradicting it: §27's case was two *separate* lines twelve lines apart, where identical English was the only way to show they were the same verse. The rule is now: **same line → one English rendering, Greek kept visible; separated → two identical renderings.**

8. **`καὶ ἔσκαλλε τὸ Πνεῦμα` (386) — LXX Ps. lxxvi. 7, and the verb is a SPADE.** *σκάλλω* is to hoe, to dig over, to grub up; the LXX has *ἔσκαλλον τὸ πνεῦμά μου*, "I dug over my spirit" (Coverdale, "and my spirit made diligent search"; AV, "my spirit made diligent search"). Andrewes puts it in the **third person about David** — *Rex David, exacto die, meditabatur, καὶ ἔσκαλλε τὸ Πνεῦμα* — and drops the *μου*, so the object is bare *the spirit*. The English wants the digging kept: "and dug over his spirit" or Coverdale's "made diligent search," but **not** "searched his soul," which is a dead modern idiom and loses the spade.

9. **`In hoc Areopagitico et nocturno examine` — the Areopagus is the point.** The nightly self-examination is a **sitting of the Areopagus**, Athens' homicide court, which sat at night and in the dark: hence *Vide ne patronum te ostendas peccatorum, sed judicem* — show thyself not the sinner's **advocate** but his **judge**, and *in tribunali mentis tuæ*, at the bar of thine own mind. The forensic vocabulary is continuous with §7's *Allegatio* and §32's *Interpellatio*; **keep the court words** (advocate, judge, bar, sentence) rather than softening to "examine yourself honestly."

10. **`O quis dabit menti meæ flagella, Quæ peccatis meis non parcant?`** — Ecclus. xxiii. 2, "who will set scourges over my thoughts?" The AV/Douay read *flagella super cogitatum meum* / "over my heart"; Andrewes has *menti meæ* and adds the relative *quæ … non parcant*, **that they spare not my sins**. His addition is the sharpest thing on the page and stands.

11. **`Ausonius ex Pythagora` quotes the Golden Verses in Ausonius' Latin, and the couplet is VERSE** — two hexameters, *Nec prius in dulcem declinent lumina somnum, / Omnia quam longi repetiveris acta diei.* The English keeps them as two lines and should keep some metrical shape; this is the only classical verse quoted in Part II. *Declinent lumina* = let not thine eyes sink; *longi … diei* = the long day's, with the adjective doing the work of a complaint.

12. **`Cato diurni negotii a se rationem exigebat` — Cicero on Cato, and *rationem exigere* is an ACCOUNTING word**: Cato *required of himself an account* of the day's business, as a steward audits. It pairs with the Genesis line above it (God closing each day of creation *per recognitionem operum cujusque diei*, by the review of that day's works, *et vidit quod bona essent*) — **God, Cato, Pythagoras and David are set side by side as four precedents for the same practice**, pagan and scriptural without apology. That juxtaposition is characteristic and should not be tidied.

13. **`Χωρῆσαι εἰς μετάνοιαν καὶ εὐλογίας` (384) glosses the Latin *vacare*, and both are about MAKING ROOM.** *Vacare* = to be free for, to have leisure for; *χωρῆσαι* = to have room for, to hold. The prayer asks to be made empty enough for repentance and for the blessing — the same word-field as §40's *Occasions*. Note the plural **εὐλογίας** against the heading's singular Εὐλογίαν; kept as printed.

14. **`Domine, labia mea aperies` is the Ps. li. 15 versicle** the reader says at every office (BCP: "O Lord, open thou our lips"). Kept in its received English form, singular for plural as the Latin has it.

15. **The closing note is the 1853 EDITOR'S, not Andrewes'** — *Sequitur Hymnus Matutinus … De utroque consulendus est Rev. Usserius, pp. 41, 43. Lib. de Symbolis.* It refers to **Ussher's *De Romanæ Ecclesiæ Symbolo Apostolico*** and to the **Codex Alexandrinus** in the King's Library, whose canticle-appendix is the source of the morning hymn (the *Gloria in excelsis*) and the evening hymn (*Φῶς ἱλαρόν* / *Te decet laus*). It is transcribed because it is printed and because it marks the boundary, but it is **editorial apparatus and must be set as such in print** — not as devotion, and not in the English translation's own voice.

16. **No `[?]` in this section** apart from the garbled Hebrew word at 386, which is legible but wrong (flag 2) and is therefore transcribed as printed rather than bracketed. All four leaves are otherwise clean, and both scans of 386/387 agree.
