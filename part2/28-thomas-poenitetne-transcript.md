## Translator's flags

1. **`Εἶπα`, not `Εἶτα` (356).** The first word of the Ps. xxxii citation is *Εἶπα* ("I said"), matching LXX Ps. xxxi. 5 (*εἶπα Ἐξαγορεύσω κατ᾽ ἐμοῦ τὴν ἀνομίαν μου τῷ κυρίῳ*). Read carefully at 200 dpi; the π is clear. **A provisional read of this leaf earlier in the session recorded it as *Εἶτα* — that was wrong and is corrected here.**

2. **`κατ᾽ ἐμοῦ` is not decorative — "I will confess mine iniquity AGAINST MYSELF."** The LXX has the accused turning witness for the prosecution against himself; the AV ("I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD") and Coverdale both drop it. **The English must keep it**; it is the hinge on which the whole section's self-examination turns.

3. **Reference numbering is Greek/Vulgate, not AV.** `*Psal.* xxxii. 6.` for a verse the AV numbers 32:5 (LXX Ps. xxxi. 5); the 1853 follows the Latin verse-division. Kept as printed; the English gives the AV numbering in brackets so the reader can find it, with the divergence noted here.

4. **`[Dan. vi. 4.]` for "thrice, as Daniel" is wrong as printed** — Daniel's three times a day is vi. 10 (Vulg. vi. 11); vi. 4 is the satraps seeking occasion against him. **Kept as printed**, flagged, not corrected — the same discipline as `[Eph. i. 8.]` at §27 and `[Jer. l. 24.]` at §23. Note Part I's front matter already flagged Dan. 6:10 for a different reason.

5. **`1 Reg. viii. 22—53`** = 1 Kings viii (Solomon's dedication prayer, the longest prayer in Scripture) — the 1853's *1 Reg.* for the Vulgate's 3 Reg., as at §5 and §26. Kept.

6. **The ladder's logic is descent with a floor, and every rung must keep its sting.** Each pair concedes the greater and then **still demands the lesser**: not seven times, yet three; not at length, yet briefly; not all night, yet one hour; not on the ground in ashes, **yet not in beds**; not in sackcloth, **yet not in purple**; not fasting from everything, yet from dainties. The negative rungs (*tamen non in lectis*, *tamen non in purpura et bysso*) are concessions that still forbid — English must not soften them into positives.

7. **`tamen non in lectis` (Ps. lxiii. 6) — a deliberate tension, left standing.** Ps. 63:6 is precisely the psalm of remembering God *upon the bed* ("si memor fui tui super stratum meum"), yet Andrewes cites it against praying at ease in bed. Translated literally ("yet not in beds") and flagged; not resolved.

8. **`a cupediis` (Dan. i. 8)** = from **dainties/delicacies** — Daniel refusing the king's meat, not fasting outright. *Cupedia* is the choice food, the luxury, and is the exact opposite pole to *omnino ab omni* on the line above.

9. **`quinta parte superaddita` (Lev. v. 16)** = the trespass-offering restitution, the principal **with a fifth part added** — set against Zacchæus' voluntary fourfold. The lesser rung is here the *legal minimum*, which is the ladder's whole point: below zeal, the law still stands.

10. **`tricesimam partem` (Deut. xxvi. 12) — an oddity, kept as printed.** Deut. 26:12 is the third-year tithe (a **tenth**, in the third year), not a thirtieth. Whether *tricesimam* is Andrewes' own reduction to a smaller proportion or a corruption of *tertiam* cannot be settled from this witness. **Not repaired.**

11. **The six questions (357) re-use §19's five affects — reordered, and with *Pœnitet* added at the head.** §19 (printed 326) ran *Dolet, Pudet, Piget, Horret, Tædet*, each with its Greek noun; here they are asked as impersonal questions in a different order — *Pœnitetne? Pigetne? Pudetne? / Doletne? Horretne? Tædetne?* — three and three. **Keep the printed order and the impersonal construction**; the English of the verbs should match §19's ("it grieves / shames / vexes / dreads / wearies") so a reader can hear the earlier section under this one.

12. **`Θωμᾶς·` is an exemplar-label, not a heading** — printed with a Greek raised point (ano teleia), introducing his words as the ladder below introduces David's and Daniel's. Do not promote it to a section title.

13. **No `[?]` in this section.** Every leaf 356–359 is clean in the Google scan, and the Greek accents and breathings on 356–357 are legible and transcribed as printed.
