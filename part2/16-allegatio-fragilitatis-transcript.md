# Part II — Preces Quotidianæ — §16 *Allegatio* (fragilitatis humanæ) — raw transcript

Source: 1853 Parker ed., printed pp. 322(mid)–323(top) (PDF 345–346), images `raw/end/p-NNN.jpg`. Offset **+23**. **LATIN** (no Greek). Title **ALLEGATIO** — the SECOND Allegatio in Part II (cf. §7, the forensic six-part plea); this one is the **plea of human frailty**: "Remember what my substance is" — dust and ashes, grass and flower, flesh and a passing spirit, rottenness and worm; a stranger and sojourner, dwelling in a house of clay, few days and evil, today not tomorrow, in the body of death, in a world of corruption lying in the wicked one. A short scripture-catena of mortality with one brace (pulvis/fœnum/caro/putredo) + a cascading-indent close. Follows §15 (Præoratio/Deprecatio, ends 322 after "Violens spiritus a debili carne"); **PECCATORUM CONFESSIO (§17) begins mid-323** after "Memor esto hujus." Translate the Latin (§6).

## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 322(mid)–323(top).** *Allegatio* — the **second** so-titled in Part II (§7 was the forensic six-count plea *Ex parte Dei…*). This one "alleges" the suppliant's **frailty and mortality** as its ground: it opens *Memorare quæ mea substantia* ("Remember what my substance is," Ps 89:47) and unrolls a catena of the dust-images.
- **The frailty-brace (322):** *Quod { pulvis et cinis / Fœnum et flos / Caro et spiritus vadens / Putredo et vermis }* — "that [I am] **dust and ashes** (Gen 18:27, Abraham's word), **grass and a flower** (Isa 40:6, "all flesh is grass"), **flesh and a passing breath** (Ps 78:39, "a wind that passeth away and cometh not again"), **rottenness and a worm** (Job 17:14, "I have said to corruption, Thou art my father; to the worm, Thou art my mother")." Keep the brace; the four pairs are the four biblical figures of mortality.
- **The mortality-catena (322–323):** a stranger and sojourner on the earth (Ps 39:12, "I am a stranger with thee, and a sojourner"), dwelling in a house of clay (Job 4:19), *few days and evil* (Gen 47:9, Jacob to Pharaoh), *today and not tomorrow* (Prov 27:1, "boast not thyself of to morrow"), *in the morning and not unto evening* (Isa 38:13, Hezekiah's lament, "from morning even to night wilt thou make an end of me"), *now and not by and by* (*Nunc et non statim*).
- **The cascading close (323):** the stair-step indent (kept as increasing 4-space levels) — *in the body of death* (Rom 6:6 / cf. 7:24), *in a world of corruption* (2 Pet 1:4), *lying in the wicked one* (1 John 5:19, "the whole world lieth in wickedness / the wicked one"), *Memor esto hujus* ("Remember this," Ps 74:18/22). The descent of clauses mirrors the descent into mortality; hold the indentation as the section's shape.
- **No Greek.** Psalm-lines Coverdale/BCP (§9); the rest a Genesis/Job/Isaiah/Epistle catena (AV). §16 ends on *Memor esto hujus*; **Peccatorum Confessio (§17)** — the confession of sins proper, opening *Peccavi* — follows.
