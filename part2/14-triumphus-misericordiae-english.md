## Translator's flags

- **Parity 3/3** against the three page-markers (318 mid, 319, 320). *Triumphus misericordiæ* = "the Triumph of Mercy," the climax of Part II's penitential cycle, in three movements under the frame **Per et propter** ("By and for the sake of").
- **The Greek "hyper-" escalation (319)** is the rhetorical summit and is kept in full. The base pair — *abundance* (**περισσεία**) / *increase* (**πλεονασμός**), heading the page as glosses to items 12–13 (the riches and overflow of mercy) — is out-piled by the intensives: *super-increase* (**ὑπερπλεονασμός**, 1 Tim 1:14 "exceeding abundant"), *super-abundance* (**ὑπερπερισσεία**, Rom 5:20 "did much more abound"), *surpassingly* (**ὑπερβαλλόντως**, Eph 2:7 "exceeding riches"). Rendered Latin term + transliterated-glossed Greek so the crescendo of grace-past-measure survives — this is the theological point (mercy that outruns its own vocabulary).
- **The seven epithets of Mercy (318):** great / manifold / of old / plenteous / everlasting / exceeding / marvellous — each keyed to a Psalm or Epistle (Ps 51:1; 25:6; 130:7; 136; Eph 2:4; Ps 17:7). Cribbed to Coverdale/BCP ("marvellous lovingkindness," Ps 17:7). Kept the brace.
- **The four dimensions (319):** the Greek **Μῆκος / Πλάτος / Βάθος / Ὕψος** (length/breadth/depth/height) beside the Latin *Longa / Lata / Profunda / Alta* — Eph 3:18, the dimensions of Christ's love here applied to mercy. Kept both columns.
- **Mercy's reach (319):** from everlasting (Ps 25:6), to everlasting (Ps 89:1), up to heaven (Ps 108:4), down to hell (Ps 86:13, "the nethermost hell"), upon all (Rom 11:32, "concluded all… that He might have mercy upon all"), tender (Luke 1:78, "the tender mercy of our God"). The *preventing/following* pair (Ps 79:8; 23:6) is the classic grace that goes before and comes after. Personal close: *Ego distuli… Tu prolongasti* — "I put off repentance, and Thou didst prolong Thy patience" — then the fountain image (*fons qui nunquam potest exhauriri*, "a fountain that can never be drained").
- **The gospel exempla (320)** rendered as a gallery of the forgiven — the sheep, the drachma, the ten thousand talents, the half-dead man, the prodigal (*filio nequam* = "the wicked/naughty son"), Jonah, Peter, Thomas, Paul, the adulteress, Magdalene, the thief. Each a present participle in Latin (*reportans, everrens, dimittens…*), kept as active participles in English ("bringing back… sweeping… forgiving…") to hold mercy as ceaseless action.
- **Item 3, *Syr.* [i. e. *Ecclus.*]:** the edition's own bracket-gloss identifying *Syr.* (its siglum, from the Syriac/older naming) as Ecclesiasticus (Sirach) 2:18 — kept the identification; rendered "[Ecclus.]".
- **Item 23, *Cujus locus*:** mercy's *place* — the **Throne of grace** (Heb 4:16) and the **ark of atonement / mercy-seat** (*propitiationum arca*, Exod 25:22, ἱλαστήριον). Item 24, *Cujus tempus*: mercy's *time* — "the day of salvation" (2 Cor 6:2). §14 ends; PRÆORATIO (§15) follows.
