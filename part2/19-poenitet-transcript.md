## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 326(mid)–327(top).** *Pœnitet* — "it repenteth [me]," the anatomy of **penitence** built on **2 Cor 7:11** (Paul's list of what "godly sorrow" works: "what carefulness… what clearing… what indignation, what fear, what vehement desire, what zeal, what revenge"). Andrewes maps the Pauline affects to Greek terms and scriptural stings.
- **The five affects (326)** — each a Latin impersonal verb + its Greek noun + the *thing* it feels: (1) *Dolet, λύπη* — "it grieves, **sorrow** [λύπη], [for] the wounds [*Plagæ*]" (2 Cor 7:11 / the "godly sorrow" λύπη of 7:10); (2) *Pudet, αἰσχύνη* — "it shames, **shame** [αἰσχύνη], [for] the stains, blot, filth [*Maculæ, labis, sordium*]" (Rom 6:21, "what fruit had ye… whereof ye are now ashamed?"); (3) *Piget, ἀγανάκτησις* — "it vexes, **indignation** [ἀγανάκτησις], [for] the guilt [*Reatus*]" (2 Cor 7:11); (4) *Horret, φόβος* — "it dreads, **fear** [φόβος], [for] the punishment [*Supplicii*]"; (5) *Tædet, κοπιῶ* — "it wearies, **I toil** [κοπιῶ], [under] the bondage, the yoke [*Servitutis, jugi*]" (Matt 11:28, "Come unto me, all ye that labour [κοπιῶντες] and are heavy laden"). Keep each triplet (verb ‖ Greek ‖ object). Note **κοπιῶ** is a 1st-person verb ("I labour"), not a noun — the one break in the pattern, drawn straight from Matt 11:28.
- **The nine inward acts (326):** fear (φόβος), indignation (ἀγανάκτησις), self-judgement (*Judicium*, κρίσις — 1 Cor 11:31, "if we would judge ourselves"), self-avenging (*Vindicta*, ἐκδίκησις — 2 Cor 7:11, "yea, what revenge"), hatred [of the sin] (Jude 23, "hating even the garment spotted by the flesh"), flight from occasions (Gal 5:13), self-humbling (Jas 4:10, "humble yourselves"), the **smiting of the breast** (Luke 18:13, the publican), the **smiting of the thigh** (*femoris*, Jer 31:19, "after that I was instructed, I smote upon my thigh" — Ephraim's repentance). Keep 8–9 as the paired penitential gestures.
- **The thirteen outward acts (327, items 10–13 shown):** (10) the laying-aside of finery, sackcloth (*Depositio excellentiæ, Cilicium* — Jer 4:8, "gird you with sackcloth, lament and howl"); (11) fasting (Joel 2:12, "turn ye… with fasting"); (12) prayer, devotion, commemoration; (13) works of mercy (Prov 16:6, "by mercy and truth iniquity is purged"). *Cilicium* = a hair-shirt / sackcloth (cloth of Cilician goat-hair).
- **The close (327):** *Da semper dolere, et semper de dolore gaudere* — "Grant me **ever to grieve, and ever to rejoice in the grieving**." The paradox of penitence: the *charismofpenthos*, the gift of tears that is itself a joy (cf. 2 Cor 7:10, "godly sorrow worketh repentance… not to be repented of"). Render the antithesis crisply — it is the section's point. §19 ends; **Miserere (§20)** — the mercy-plea proper, with its own Greek series (Μὴ στήσῃς / μὴ λογίσῃ / μὴ μνησθῇς) — follows.
- Scripture cribs: 2 Cor 7:11 (AV) is the governing text; the Psalm-lines elsewhere Coverdale/BCP (§9).
