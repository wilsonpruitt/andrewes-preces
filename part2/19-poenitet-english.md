## Translator's flags

- **Parity 2/2** against the two page-markers (326 mid, 327 top). *Pœnitet* — "It repenteth me," the anatomy of penitence built on **2 Cor 7:11**, Paul's list of what godly sorrow works. Andrewes keys each Pauline affect to a Greek term (kept + glossed) and a scriptural sting.
- **The five affects (326):** the Latin impersonal verbs (*Dolet / Pudet / Piget / Horret / Tædet*) rendered "it grieves / shames / vexes / dreads / wearies," each with its Greek noun and the thing it feels — the wounds, the stains, the guilt, the punishment, the yoke. **κοπιῶ** (item 5) is a first-person verb ("I labour"), not a noun — kept as such since it quotes Matt 11:28 directly ("all ye that labour [κοπιῶντες]"), the one deliberate break in the pattern (the soul that toils under sin's yoke turning to the one who gives rest).
- **The nine inward acts (326):** fear, indignation, **self-judgement** (κρίσις, 1 Cor 11:31 "if we would judge ourselves we should not be judged"), **self-avenging** (ἐκδίκησις, 2 Cor 7:11 "what revenge" — the soul's holy vengeance on its own sin), hatred of the sin (Jude 23), flight from occasions (Gal 5:13), self-humbling (Jas 4:10), and the two penitential gestures — **smiting the breast** (Luke 18:13, the publican, "God be merciful to me a sinner") and **smiting the thigh** (Jer 31:19, Ephraim, "I smote upon my thigh"). Kept 8–9 paired.
- **The thirteen outward acts (327):** sackcloth (*Cilicium*, the hair-shirt of Cilician goat-hair; Jer 4:8), fasting (Joel 2:12), prayer/devotion/commemoration, works of mercy (Prov 16:6, "by mercy and truth iniquity is purged"). *Depositio excellentiæ* = "the laying-aside of finery" — the penitent strips his adornment for sackcloth.
- **The close (327):** *Da semper dolere, et semper de dolore gaudere* — "Grant me ever to grieve, and ever to rejoice in the grieving." The paradox at the heart of Andrewes' penitence: the gift of tears is itself a gladness (2 Cor 7:10, "godly sorrow… not to be repented of"). The antithesis *dolere / de dolore gaudere* kept sharp — it is the whole section's resolution. §19 ends; the Miserere (§20) follows.
