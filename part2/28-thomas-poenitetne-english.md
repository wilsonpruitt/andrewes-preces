## Translator's flags

1. **Untitled in the source.** The 1853 gives this section no heading — only the exemplar-label *Θωμᾶς·* over the Greek. The bracketed English title names it from its two ends; it must be shown as editorial (brackets, not a printed heading) wherever it appears.

2. **"against myself" must survive (Ps. 32).** The LXX has *ἐξαγορεύσω **κατ᾽ ἐμοῦ** τὴν ἀνομίαν μου τῷ Κυρίῳ* — "I will confess mine iniquity **against myself** unto the Lord": the penitent turning witness for the prosecution against himself. **Both the AV ("I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD") and Coverdale drop the phrase**, so this is the one psalm-line in the section where §9's Coverdale-first rule is overridden by the Greek Andrewes actually quotes. The whole self-examination that follows hangs on it.

3. **Verse numbering.** The 1853's `Psal. xxxii. 6` follows the Latin verse-division for what the AV numbers 32:5; the English carries both (`[Ps. 32:6 (AV 32:5)]`) so the reader can find it without the Latin page being altered.

4. **`[Dan. vi. 4.]` is a wrong reference in the 1853** — Daniel's praying three times a day is 6:10 (Vulg. 6:11); 6:4 is his enemies seeking occasion against him. **Reproduced as printed** in both layers, flagged here, not corrected. The edition does not silently mend references.

5. **The ladder is a descent that never reaches the ground, and the English must keep every rung's demand.** Each pair yields the greater and then still requires the lesser — and three rungs are *negative* concessions that go on forbidding: **yet not in beds**, **yet not in purple and fine linen**, and (by implication) *yet from dainties*. Rendering these as positives ("yet at least remember him in bed") would invert the sentence. The rhythm is deliberately bare — *Si non … tamen* — and the English keeps it bare: **If not … yet**.

6. **`tamen non in lectis` (Ps. 63:6) is cited against itself, and is left so.** Ps. 63:6 is the verse of remembering God upon the bed; Andrewes uses it as the floor beneath which prayer must not sink — not on the ground, not in ashes, *yet not in beds either*. The tension is his, not a mistranslation; flagged, not resolved.

7. **`a cupediis` = from dainties** (Dan. 1:8, Daniel refusing the king's meat) — the luxury, the delicacy; the exact counter-pole to *omnino ab omni*, "from all things altogether" (Ps. 107:18, "their soul abhorred all manner of meat"). If not the total fast, then at least the table's luxuries.

8. **`quinta parte superaddita` = the fifth part added** (Lev. 5:16), the trespass-offering's legal restitution, set under Zacchæus' voluntary fourfold. The rung below zeal is **the law**, which is exactly why the ladder does not end in excuse.

9. **`tricesimam partem` — "the thirtieth part," kept as printed** though Deut. 26:12 is the third-year **tithe**. Whether Andrewes wrote a smaller proportion deliberately or *tricesimam* corrupts *tertiam* cannot be decided from this witness. Not repaired; see the transcript flag.

10. **The six questions answer §19.** They are the affects of *Pœnitet* (printed 326) asked back at the penitent, reordered and with repentance itself set at the head. The verbs are kept **impersonal** — "Doth it repent? Doth it vex? Doth it shame? / Doth it grieve? Doth it dread? Doth it weary?" — using the same English as §19's *Dolet / Pudet / Piget / Horret / Tædet* ("it grieves / shames / vexes / dreads / wearies"), so the earlier anatomy is audible under this examination. **Do not supply a subject** ("Dost thou repent?"); the Latin's impersonal construction is what makes it an examination of the state rather than of the man.

11. **`Θωμᾶς·` = "Thomas:"** — an exemplar-label with a Greek raised point, introducing his words exactly as the ladder below introduces David's and Daniel's. Set as a label, never as a title.

## Collation findings — Brightman 1903 + recension parallels (2026-08-04)

> Applied to the ENGLISH layer only. **No transcript is altered**; the 1853 plate stands as printed. Method and full ledger: `apparatus/BRIGHTMAN-collation.md`.

- **✅ `tricesimam partem` IS THE READING — a question the flags called unsettleable is now settled.** Brightman gives *"If not the half; **Dt. xxvi 12** — yet **the thirtieth part**?"* — the same word, the same reference, **and he declined to mend it too.** So *tricesimam* is **not** a corruption of *tertiam*: the oddity (Deut. xxvi. 12 is the third-year *tenth*) is Andrewes' own, and stands. Supersedes the earlier "cannot be settled from this witness."
- **✅ `[Dan. vi. 4.]` IDENTIFIED: read Dan. i. 8.** Brightman sets *"If not wholly from all; **Dan. i 8** — yet from dainties?"* — Daniel purposing not to defile himself with the king's meat. **Kept as printed**, per the standing discipline; the right reference is now on record.
- Brightman's neighbouring refs **confirm** ours: `S. Mk. xii 41` (the widow) and `S. Lk. xix 8` (Zacchæus).
