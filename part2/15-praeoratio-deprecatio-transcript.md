## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 321–322.** Two caps-titled parts of one oration movement — **PRÆORATIO** and **DEPRECATIO** — kept in one file as the positive and negative sides of the same daily prayer (the plea *for* hearing and *against* wrath). No Greek in this section.
- **PRÆORATIO — "the fore-prayer" (321):** the prayer for the prayer itself. Two sixfold movements: **(a) the ascent of prayer** — *let it ascend* (Acts 10:4, "thy prayers… are come up for a memorial"), *reach Thee* (2 Chr 30:27), *enter* (Ps 88:2, "let my prayer enter into thy presence"), *appear in Thy sight* (Ps 141:2, "let my prayer be set forth… as incense"), *find grace* (Heb 4:16), *draw near* (Ps 119:169, "let my cry come near before thee"). The hinge: *Et peto ne revertatur ad me vacua* ("and I ask that it return not to me empty," Is 55:11) — *Sed sicut scis, et potes, et vis* ("but as Thou knowest, and canst, and wilt"). Then **(b) the plea to be heard** — *hear* (Ps 119:149), *incline Thine ear* (Ps 86:1; Dan 9:18), *attend and consider* (Ps 61:1; Lam 1:12, "behold and see"), *understand* (Ps 139:2), *hearken* (Ps 64:1), *remember to do it* (Dan 9:19, "O Lord, hear… defer not"). Psalm-cribs Coverdale/BCP (§9).
- **DEPRECATIO — "the warding-off" (321–322):** the great **Ne { … }** brace — twenty-odd petitions all governed by the one *Ne* ("that Thou wilt **not** …"). Render "that Thou wilt not" once and let the verbs hang under it: *turn away in anger* (Ps 27:10), *turn away Thine eye* (2 Chr 30:9), *hide Thy face* (Job 13:24), *set a cloud between* (Lam 3:44, "thou hast covered thyself with a cloud"), *shut Thine ear* (Lam 3:56), *forsake me* (Ps 38:21), *leave me for ever* (Ps 27:9), *despise* (Ibid.), *keep silence* (Ps 39:13, "hold not thy peace at my tears"), *sleep* (Matt 8:24, the sleeping Christ in the storm), *go far from me* (Ps 35:22), *cast off* (Ps 44:23), *take away Thy mercy* (Ps 89:33), *fail of Thy truth* (Ps 89:49), *rebuke me in anger* (Ps 6:1), *chasten me in wrath* (Ps 38:1), *cast me from Thy presence* (Ps 51:11), and — breaking the *Ne* pattern with an explicit imperative — *Noli me reprobare a pueris Tuis* ("cast me not off from among Thy children," Wisd 9:4), *take not Thy [Holy] Spirit from me* (Ps 51:11), *forget me not for ever* (Ps 74:19), *be not wroth very sore* (Is 64:9).
- **The inner brace (322):** `{ tradas [Psal. xxvii. 12.] / allidas [Is. lxiv. 7.] } me in manu iniquitatis` — "that Thou wilt not **deliver / dash** me into the hand of iniquity" (Ps 27:12 "deliver me not over unto the will of mine enemies"; Is 64:7 "hast consumed us, because of our iniquities"). Two verbs sharing one object; keep the brace. Then *tardes reverti* ("be slow to return," Hab 2:3, "though it tarry, wait for it"), *perdas cum impiis animam meam* ("destroy not my soul with the wicked," Ps 26:9).
- **The close (322):** *Si potentiam hostis ad tempus Tua permissione patimur, nequaquam ejus insatiabilibus faucibus absorbeamur* — "If for a time, by Thy permission, we suffer the enemy's power, let us in no wise be swallowed by his insatiable jaws." Then the **Vincatur couplet**: *Leo ab ove infirma, / Violens spiritus a debili carne* — "Let the Lion be conquered by the weak Sheep, the violent spirit by the feeble flesh" — the paradox of the Lamb's victory (Rev 5:5, the Lion who is the Lamb) applied to the suppliant's frailty. §15 ends here; **ALLEGATIO (§16)** follows.
- **Scripture cribs:** the whole section is a Psalm-catena (Coverdale/BCP per §9) with the prophets and Wisdom woven in. *Occludas aurem* / *sileas* / *dormias* draw on the "sleeping God" laments; the Matt 8:24 *dormias* is the one Gospel note — Christ asleep in the boat, "carest thou not that we perish?"
