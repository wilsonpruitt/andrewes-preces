# Part II — Preces Quotidianæ — §23 *In magnum pietatis sacramentum* (Upon the Great Mystery of Godliness) — English

Line-keyed English of `23-in-magnum-pietatis-sacramentum-transcript.md` (printed 339 foot – 345 top). **Pure Latin source — no Greek in this section**; translated from the Latin (§6). Psalm-lines crib the Coverdale/BCP Psalter ahead of the AV (§9); the AV governs the NT and prophetic citations. Register thou-form AV/BCP (§8–10). Brace catalogues inline `{ a / b / c }`; two-column rows ` | `. One continuous act of thanksgiving-and-petition in three movements — the mystery of the Incarnation and the whole economy of grace (339–341), the petitions for the Spirit, the Church, and faith (341–342), the intercession for peace and for those who are prayed for (343) — closing in the doxology and **Hosanna / Benedictus** (344–345). Parity target 7/7.

## Translator's flags

- **Parity 7/7** against the seven page-markers (339 foot – 345 top). Pure Latin section; nothing to construe from the Greek. Section boundary hard at both ends: it opens after §22's Dan. 9:19 close, and ends where CONFESSIO LAUDIS (§24) begins mid-345.
- **The incipit (339).** *In magnum pietatis sacramentum* is an accusative of direction — the prayer turns and looks *upon* the mystery — and it governs the whole cascade of accusatives that follows (*Deum … Hominem … Filium … Christum … Dominum*). "Upon the great mystery of godliness" keeps 1 Tim. 3:16's AV wording (*"great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifest in the flesh"*) and the Creed's *pro nobis hominibus et propter nostram salutem* ("for us men, and for our salvation") is rendered in its received English form. **Christum Spiritus** kept literally ("the Christ of the Spirit") — the Anointed of the Spirit, balancing *Filium Patris*; do not flatten to "Christ".
- **✅ THE 340 DEFECT IS RESOLVED (2026-08-04) — and the supplied word is now `[upon us]`, not `[in us]`.** Two independent witnesses agree verbatim. **(1) Our own Part III §1** — the Harley recension of this very section — reads the passage whole: `3. In Spiritum Sanctum, / Et in Eo / Potentiam ex alto sanctificantem, / Et vivificantem ad immortalitatem, / **Extrinsecus et invisibiliter, sed** / **Efficaciter et manifeste in** / **Nos operantem,** / Illuminatione justitiæ, / Infusione gratiæ, / In Reprehensione, Doctrina, Tolerantia, Subventione…`. **(2) Brightman 1903** renders the same: *"in the Holy Ghost… from without and invisibly, but effectuously and manifestly operating upon us by illumination of righteousness, infusion of grace, in reproof, teaching, bearing with, help, witnessing with."* **So the lost antecedent is the Holy Ghost — our conjecture, confirmed — and the lost hinge is `Extrinsecus et invisibiliter, sed`, NOT a *non tantum*.** ⚠ **`in Nos operantem` is accusative**, so the supplied bracket is **`[upon us]`**; `[in us]` was a locative the Latin does not have. **The Latin transcript stays unrepaired as printed** — the repair is recorded here, not applied to the plate. ⚠ **METHOD, worth more than this repair: the volume repairs its own defects across the recension boundary.** Part III is the Harley recension of Part II's penitential matter, so a defective Part II plate may stand whole in Part III. **Check the recension parallel FIRST on any Part II defect — it is free.**
- **(superseded, kept for the record) The 340 defect — `[in us]` was mine.** The printed page joins *Dominum nostrum,* (339) to *qua Creator, / qua Redemptor, / Sed efficaciter et manifeste operantem* with no antecedent and no *non tantum*; the sense demands the Holy Ghost as subject and a "not only … but" hinge. I have left the Latin unrepaired in the transcript and rendered the English as literally as it will bear, supplying only the bracketed `[in us]` to carry *operantem* into English syntax. **This is the one supplied word in the section — flagged here and in the transcript. Restore from Brightman / the Harley MS before print, and either repair the Latin or set the lacuna visibly; do not let the English quietly paper over it.**
- **The Spirit's six operations (340).** *Reprehensione / Doctrina / Tolerantia / Subventione / Contestatione / Consolatione* — rendered "reproof / doctrine / long-suffering / succour / testimony / consolation", holding the 2 Tim. 3:16 pair (*reproof, doctrine*) in AV wording and *contestatio* as "testimony" so that it chimes with the *contestatio Sigilli et Arrhabonis* two pages later (342), which is the same word doing the same work.
- **The economy of grace (340).** The six agent-nouns are datives of the person thanked, each yoked to the mystery in which he is such: *mundator* (Tit. 2:14 "purify unto himself") → cleanser, in Conception and Nativity; *liberator* (Heb. 2:15 "deliver them who… were subject to bondage") → deliverer, in Passion/Cross/Death; *triumphator* (Col. 2:15 "triumphing over them") → conqueror, over hell in the Descent and over death in the Resurrection; *præcursor* (Heb. 6:20 "whither the forerunner is for us entered") → Forerunner, in the Ascension; *advocatus* (Rom. 8:34) → Advocate, in the Session; *instaurator fidei* (Heb. 12:2 "the author and finisher of our faith") → finisher, in the second Advent. Each English title takes its noun from the AV of the cited verse — that is the whole design of the catalogue.
- **The Abaddon table (341) — the jewel of the section.** A five-fold antithesis of *names*: Christ is set against each name of the enemy by a countervailing name of his own. *Abaddon* (Rev. 9:11, the Destroyer) ↔ **JESUS** (the Saviour) — the etymologies are the argument, so both names are left untranslated and the pun is carried in the flag rather than glossed on the page. Then Satan/Adversary ↔ Mediator; Devil/Slanderer (διάβολος = calumniator) ↔ Advocate; the Accuser (Rev. 12:10) ↔ Intercessor; the captor ↔ Redeemer. "Against" supplies in English what the Latin datives carry after *opponit*.
- **The two dispositions (341).** *Cum frigidus sum… / Cum fervidus sum…* — a matched pair, cold in prayer and hot in lust, each with its remedy: the Throne (Heb. 4:16 "the throne of grace"), his appearing and intercession (Heb. 9:24; Rom. 8:34) against the coldness; the dread judgement-seat and the last trump (1 Cor. 15:52) against the heat. *Cathedra* rendered "Throne" to chime with *Sessio* on 340; "Seat" was the alternative and is defensible.
- **Faith (342).** The Luke 17:5 petition, then the five negations and five affirmations, each keyed to its verse: mustard seed (Luke 17:6), not dead (Jas. 2:20), not for a time (the printed ref is Rev. 2:13, though Luke 8:13 *"for a while believe"* is the sense — see transcriber's flag), not hypocritical (2 Tim. 1:5 "unfeigned"), working by love (Gal. 5:6), wrought with works (Jas. 2:22), ministering virtue (2 Pet. 1:5), overcoming the world (1 John 5:4), most holy (Jude 20 "your most holy faith"). AV diction throughout; *subministratricem virtutis* kept as "ministering virtue" rather than modernised to "supplying".
- **The intercession (343).** *Deprecor Te… ut auferatur ira Tua ab hac urbe, ab hac domo, quia peccavimus Tibi* is Daniel 9:16 turned upon Andrewes' own city and his own house — the same chapter whose 19th verse closed §22, so the two sections are stitched at the seam. *Da redamare mei amantes, etsi mihi ignotos* ("to love again them that love me, though they be unknown to me") and *da mihi ostendere illis misericordiam Dei in precibus meis* are among the most personal lines in Part II; kept plain. *Ibi satiari gloria* echoes Ps. 17:15 (BCP "I shall be satisfied… with thy likeness") but says *glory*, so "satisfied with glory" stands.
- **The psalm-catena (344) — Coverdale/BCP throughout (§9).** Ps. 66:14 ("O come hither and hearken, all ye that fear God: and I will tell you what he hath done for my soul") and Ps. 104:33 ("I will sing unto the Lord as long as I live: I will praise my God while I have my being") are given in Coverdale verbatim, as is Ps. 34:3. Three places where I follow **Andrewes' Latin against the BCP**, each because the Latin is doing work: (a) Ps. 111:1 — the BCP has "secretly among the faithful", but *in concilio sanctorum* is "in the assembly of the saints", which is what the sentence needs beside "and in the congregation"; (b) Ps. 51:15 — the BCP has "open thou my lips", but the Latin reads *os meum* ("my mouth"), kept; (c) Ps. 118:28 — the BCP doubles "thou art my God" and closes "I will praise thee", where the Latin has *exaltabo Te*, so "I will exalt thee". Ps. 145:21's fourfold *in sæculum… in sæcula sæculorum* is rendered as a rising fourfold ("for ever, and for ever and ever, world without end, world without end") rather than collapsed — the escalation is the point.
- **The doxology (344–345).** *Gloria Deo in excelsis* → the Gloria's opening in its received English (Luke 2:14 AV), then the twelve-membered grid of Rev. 5:12–13 / 7:12, read across the rows in printed order: *Virtus / Potestas / Potentia* rendered "Power / Might / Strength" per Rev. 5:12 and 7:12 AV, *Salus* "Salvation" (Rev. 7:10). The verb is supplied once ("be unto our God") where the Latin leaves the datives to hang. The section then closes on the Sanctus-Benedictus (Matt. 21:9) — the same Hosanna that ends the deprecation block of Part I, and a deliberate liturgical cadence to end the day's thanksgiving on.
- **Line-keying.** Printed hanging-indents and cascades mirrored line-for-line between the layers, including the two blank-line movement breaks and the wrapped reference at 343 (Deut./Is.). Brightman and Newman consulted only after drafting (§12); nothing imported.
