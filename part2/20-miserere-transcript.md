## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 327(mid)–331(mid).** *Miserere* — the mercy-plea proper, following §19 *Pœnitet*. Two large movements: **(A)** an opening *Ne*-series of petitions for mercy (327), and **(B)** the **four-evils-of-sin framework** (328–331): sin considered as *Reatus* (guilt), *Macula* (stain), *Languor* (sickness), *Servitus* (bondage) — a scholastic quadripartition Andrewes turns into a prayer, each evil answered by its own litany of Latin+Greek imperatives. This parallels the Part I evening confession's anatomy of sin. **Boundary confirmed by read-ahead:** §20 closes 331 with the *Amove {occasiones/scandala}* brace; *Quies animæ* (§21) opens immediately below with "Adhuc peccatum excubat præ foribus" (Gen 4:7).
- **The opening *Ne*-series (327).** A cascade of psalm-verbs, most doubled with the Greek imperative: *Gratiam fac, χάρισαι* (Rom 8:32, "freely give"); *Ne statuas, Μὴ στήσῃς* (Acts 7:60, Stephen's "lay not this sin to their charge" — LXX-flavoured μὴ στήσῃς αὐτοῖς); *Ne imputes, μὴ λογίσῃ* (Ps 32:2, "unto whom the Lord imputeth not iniquity" — BCP "and in whose spirit there is no guile"); *Ne memineris, μὴ μνησθῇς* (Ps 79:8, "O remember not our old sins" — BCP-Coverdale, which stands with Andrewes' LXX). The *Esto {placabilis / præstabilis / exorabilis}* brace = "Be Thou {ready to be appeased / ready to make good / ready to be entreated}" (Exod 32:12 "turn from thy fierce wrath"; Joel 2:13 "he is gracious and merciful… and repenteth him of the evil"; Ps 77:7 "will the Lord absent himself for ever?"). Close: *Ne ira Tua veniat super me, Sed gratia Tua, quæso, præveniat me* — "Let not Thy wrath come upon me, but let Thy grace, I beseech, **prevent** [go before] me" — the technical BCP sense of *prevent* (praevenire, "come before"), as in the Collect "Prevent us, O Lord, in all our doings."
- **The four evils (328).** Rendered with the scholastic terms in play: **Reatus** = *guilt* / liability; **Macula** = *stain* / defilement; **Languor** = *sickness* / infirmity; **Servitus** = *bondage* / slavery. Keep the four as a titled table (they head the four litanies that follow).
- **I. Reatus — Ὑπόδικον (328).** Ὑπόδικον = "liable to judgment / under sentence" (Rom 3:19, "that all the world may become **guilty** [ὑπόδικος] before God"). Rubric *Ne ad damnationem* = "[let it] not [be] unto condemnation." Eleven imperatives of remission: *Parce φεῖσαι* (spare, Job); *Tolle, aufer, αἶρε* (take away — Jo 1:29, "the Lamb of God, which taketh away [ὁ αἴρων] the sin of the world"); *Dissipa διασκέδασον* (scatter/blot out, Is 38:17 "thou hast cast all my sins behind thy back"); *Veniam da συγχώρησον* (forgive); *Indulge ἄφες* (remit); *Ignosce σύγγνωθι* (pardon); *Libera a pœna, ῥῦσαι, condona* (deliver from the penalty, and remit); *Propitius esto ἱλάσθητι* (be propitious — Luke 18:13, the publican's "God be merciful [ἱλάσθητι] to me a sinner"); *Reconciliare καταλλάγηθι* (be reconciled, 2 Cor 5:18); *Repropitiare* (be propitiated again, 1 Kgs 8:39). Keep each Latin+Greek pair.
- **II. Macula — Κηλίς / Δυσειδές (328–329).** Κηλίς = "stain, spot"; Δυσειδές = "the ill-favoured / unshapely thing" (deformity of sin). Ten imperatives of cleansing, doubling Ps 51 (the *Miserere* psalm proper): *Averte faciem a peccatis, ἀπόστρεψον* (Ps 51:9, "hide thy face from my sins"); paired warning *Ne avertas a miseria* (Ps 44:24, "wherefore hidest thou thy face?"); *Præteri ὑπέρβαινε* (pass over — Mic 7:18, "passeth by the transgression"); *Dissimula ὑπέριδε* (overlook — Acts 17:30, "the times of this ignorance God winked at [ὑπεριδών]"); *Sustine ἀνέχου* (forbear, Rom 2:4); *Tege κάλυψον* (cover — Ps 85:2, "thou hast covered all their sin"); *Munda* (Ps 51:2, "wash me throughly… and cleanse me"); *Purga καθάρισον* (Ps 19:12, "cleanse thou me from secret faults"); *Ablue λοῦσαι* (Is 1:16, "wash you, make you clean"); *Dealba λεύκανον* (make white — Is 1:18, "though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow"); *Dele ἀπάλειψον* (blot out — Is 44:22, "I have blotted out, as a thick cloud, thy transgressions"). Ps-lines follow Coverdale/BCP (§9).
- **III. Languor — Πληγή / Τὸ νοσηρὸν σύντριμμα (329).** Πληγή = "wound/stroke"; τὸ νοσηρὸν σύντριμμα = "the sickly bruise / diseased fracture." Three imperatives of healing: *Cura, θεράπευε / ἐπιμέλειαν ἔχε* (heal, tend — Luke 10:35, the Samaritan's "take care of him"); *Medere ἴασαι* (Ps 147:3, "he healeth the broken in heart"); *Sana* (make whole). Then *Remissius fac, σῶσον ἀπό* ("deal more gently, save from") and *Extirpa, κάλαμον κατεάγης* — the bruised-reed line of Matt 12:20 ("a bruised reed shall he not break," κάλαμον συντετριμμένον); the printed Greek *κάλαμον κατεάγης* keeps the "broken reed" image (see below).
- **IV. Servitus (329–331).** The longest limb, four sub-cycles: **(a)** the paired opening imperatives *Vindica / Erue* (avenge / rescue) ‖ *Libera / Salva* (free / save); **(b)** *Propono — Προαιρῶ* ("I purpose / I set my choice"), a six-fold declaration of resolve keyed to 2 Cor 7:11 (the affections of godly earnestness — ἐπιπόθησις "vehement desire," ζῆλος "zeal," σπουδή "diligence," ἀπολογία "clearing of yourselves"): *Statui ἔστησα* (Ps 119:106, "I have sworn, and I will perform it"), *Concupivi ἐπιθυμῶ*, *Desidero ἐπιπόθησις*, *Zelo ζηλῶ*, *Studeo σπουδή*, *Allego ἀπολογία*; **(c)** the two *Quod {…}* braces of the vow's content — **1. Quod** "[I purpose] that I will {turn away from evil (ἀποστρέψω, Is 30:15) / forsake it (ἀπολείπειν, Is 55:7) / depart from it (ἀφίστασθαι, 2 Tim 2:19) / say 'it sufficeth' (ἀρκετός, 1 Pet 4:3 "the time past of our life may suffice us") / do it no more henceforth (οὐκέτι)}," and **2. Quod** "that I will {turn to Thee (ἐπιστρέψω, Lam 3:40) / turn my feet (Ps 119:59) / lift up my hands (Lam 3:41) / decline [from evil] (ἐκκλίνειν, Ps 34:14) / break my [self-]will / [make] the crooked straight / set a hedge (Hos 2:6)}"; **(d)** *Infirmus sum* — "I am weak," the Rom 7 anatomy of the divided will (*Non facio quod volo, facio quod nolo* — 7:19; *Lex membrorum captivat* — 7:23; *Quis me liberabit?* — 7:24), with the birth-pang image (*Veniunt filii ad partum* — Is 37:3, "the children are come to the birth, and there is not strength to bring forth") and the flood image (*Fluctus intrant ad animam meam* — Ps 69:1, "the waters are come in unto my soul"); **(e)** *Fer opem* — "Bring help," a 22-fold cry doubling the psalter's aid-verbs, closing on *Sepi spinis* (Hos 2:6, "I will hedge up thy way with thorns") and *Amove {occasiones / scandala}* ("remove {the occasions / the stumbling-blocks}").
- **The *Fer opem* Greek pairs (330–331):** *Succurre βοήθει* + *Deus in adjutorium [meum intende]* (Ps 70:1, the office-opening versicle); *Opitulare ἀνάληψαι*; *Subveni ἀντιλαβοῦ*; *Converte ἐπίστρεψον* + *Exurge, Domine* (Ps 3:7); *Quære ζήτει* (Luke 19:10, "to seek and to save that which was lost"); *Inclina cor, Κλῖνον καρδίαν* (Ps 119:36, "incline my heart unto thy testimonies"); *Dirige gressus, Ἴθυνον ἴχνη* (Ps 119:133, "order my steps in thy word"); *Ure renes, νεφρούς* (Ps 26:2, "try my reins and my heart"); *Confige carnes, σάρκας* (Ps 119:120, "my flesh trembleth for fear of thee" — *confige*, "nail/pierce," follows the Vulgate "confige timore tuo carnes meas"); *Trahe me post Te, εἵλξον* (Cant 1:4, "draw me, we will run after thee"); *Ne ad vomitum, ἐξέραμα* (2 Pet 2:22, "the dog is turned to his own vomit again"); *Ne tentatio, nisi humana* (1 Cor 10:13, "there hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man"). Ps-lines Coverdale/BCP (§9).
