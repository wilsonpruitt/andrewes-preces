## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 309(foot)–315(top).** *Pro morte Christi* — "For the death of Christ," the great Passion meditation. It opens with the two grounds of the death (obedience unto the death of the cross, *Phil.* 2:8; and the constraining desire, *Luc.* 22:15 — "with desire I have desired"), then prays through the sufferings "for those things He suffered in **Gethsemane · Gabbatha · Golgotha**" (the three-place brace on 309, keyed to *Matt.* 26:36 / *Jo.* 19:13 / *Vers.* 17). Each place then unfolds as a numbered catalogue.
- **Parallel-Greek brace (310):** `{ Dolore / Pudore / Maledicto } Crucis` set beside `{ Ὠδῖνες / Αἰσχύνη / Κατάρα } τοῦ σταυροῦ` with refs `[Act. ii. 24.] / [Heb. xii. 2.] / [Gal. iii. 13.]` — "the **pangs / shame / curse** of the cross," Latin and Greek in parallel. Ὠδῖνες = birth-pangs (Acts 2:24, "the pains of death"); Αἰσχύνη = shame (Heb 12:2, "despising the shame"); Κατάρα = curse (Gal 3:13, "made a curse for us"). Both layers are main text — render the English keyed to the Greek/Latin pair.
- **The numbering restarts by place, not continuously.** After the opening (unnumbered "Pro morte Christi" grounds), the *anima turbari* agony list runs 1–8 (310). Then **Gethsemane** (heading, 310) runs its own 1–7 + the "raptari ad" 8-station drag (Annas → Caiaphas → Pilate → Herod → Pilate again → Praetorium → Gabbatha → Patibulum, 311). Then **Golgotha** (heading, 311) carries the big Roman-numeral cycle **I–VIII** with Arabic sub-counts. **I** = the *ῥαπίσματι* (buffetings, *Marc.* 14:65) sub-list 1–13 (311). **II** = the members-of-the-body list 1–14 (head/eyes/ears/mouth/face/back/neck/hands/knees/feet/breast/heart/blood/soul, 312) — a head-to-foot anatomy of the Passion, closing on *Eli, Eli.* **III–V** share one running count 1–15 (312: the trial-shuttling and the mock-coronation) continuing 16–18 on 313. **VI** 19–28, **VII** 29–40, **VIII** 1–10 (the crucifixion proper through death and the piercing, 313–314). Keep every numeral exactly as printed — the odd restarts and the Roman/Arabic interleave are the author's structure, not error.
- **Inline Greek (311):** `Quod ῥαπίσματι` (*Marc.* 14:65) — "that [He was subjected] to buffetings/blows"; ῥάπισμα = a blow with the open hand or rod. Kept in Greek with the Latin frame.
- **The "Pudor. / Dolor." marginal gloss (313):** against items 33 (*Nudari*, "to be stripped") and 34 (*In cruce extendi*, "to be stretched on the cross") stand the single words **Pudor** ("Shame") and **Dolor** ("Pain") in a right column — the stripping = the shame, the stretching = the pain. Keep them apposed (` | ` / trailing column).
- **Inline Greek (314):** `Πλάνον vocari` (*Matt.* 27:63) — "to be called a **deceiver/impostor**" (πλάνος; cf. §10's *Planus*), and the climactic `Ἄγνωστοι βάσανοι` — "**unknown torments**," the sufferings that pass knowledge (item 10, left without a scripture ref — the pains no evangelist recorded). Both main text; render the Greek + English.
- **The hinge-prayer (314):** `Per pœnas Tuas, quas ego indignus hic recolo, / Animam meam a pœnis inferni libera.` — "By Thy pains, which I unworthy here recall, deliver my soul from the pains of hell." This turns the catalogue into petition; render it as the pivot it is.
- **Septem verba Christi novissima (314):** the **Seven Last Words** in a right-brace catalogue — (1) *Pater, dimitte* [Luke 23:34], (2) *Mulier, ecce filium!* [John 19:26], (3) *Hodie Mecum eris in Paradiso* [Luke 23:43], (4) *Eli, Eli* [Matt 27:46], (5) *Sitio* [John 19:28], (6) *Consummatum est* [Vers. 30], (7) *Pater, in manus Tuas, etc.* [Luke 23:46]. Render the words in their received English liturgical form (BCP/AV: "Father, forgive them"; "Woman, behold thy son"; "To day shalt thou be with Me in paradise"; "My God, my God"; "I thirst"; "It is finished"; "Father, into thy hands").
- **The five-wounds absolution-plea (314–315):** `Qui gloriosum caput vulnerari voluisti, ignosce per illud, quicquid capitis mei sensibus deliqui` — five parallel stanzas, one per wounded member (head / hands / side / feet / whole body), each: "Thou who didst will Thy [member] to be [wounded], **forgive through it** whatsoever I have sinned by [the corresponding member of mine]." A penitential application of the wounds — the head's senses, the hands' unlawful touch and act, the side's lustful thoughts "in the heat of concupiscence," the feet "swift to evil," the whole body's misused offices. Keep the "forgive through it" (*ignosce per illud/illas/illos*) refrain.
- **The wound-dimensions (315):** `En { multitudinem / longitudinem / latitudinem / profunditatem eorum } a capite usque ad calcem: Et per Tua sana mea!` — "Behold the **multitude, length, breadth, depth** of them [my wounds], from head to heel: and by Thy [wounds] heal mine!" Echoes Isa 1:6 ("from the sole of the foot even unto the head") and the "breadth and length and depth and height" of Eph 3:18 turned to the wounds.
- **The offering-close (315):** the seven post-mortem mysteries — `1. Pretiosa mors / 2. Lateris apertura / 3. Sanguinis et aquæ fluenta / 4. Emendicatio corporis / 5. Depositio de cruce / 6. Sepultura in alieno / 7. Triduana` ("the precious death, the opening of the side, the streams of blood and water, the **begging of the body** [*emendicatio* — Joseph of Arimathea begging it from Pilate], the taking-down from the cross, the burial in another's tomb, the three-days'"). Then the offering: `Per hæc omnia admoneo Te, et rogo Te. Obsecro Te, ut offerre digneris pro me hæc omnia Patri Tuo` — "By all these I put Thee in remembrance, and pray Thee: I beseech Thee to vouchsafe to offer all these for me to Thy Father." Closes on the antithesis `Omnes amaritudines quas passus es, / Charitatem super omnes qua passus es` — "All the bitternesses Thou didst suffer; the love above all with which Thou didst suffer." §11 ends here; **Transfiguratio (§12)** begins mid-315.
- **Scripture cribs:** Gospel Passion narratives throughout (AV), with the Psalm-22 crucifixion verses (*Manus extensæ / Pedes clavis suffixi / Manus et pedes fodi*, all *Psal.* xxii. 16) cribbed to Coverdale/BCP per §9. *Baptismo sanguinis baptizari* (Luke 12:50) = the "baptism of blood" — Christ's own word for His Passion.
