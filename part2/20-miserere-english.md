## Translator's flags

- **Parity 5/5** against the five page-markers (327 mid, 328, 329, 330, 331 top). *Miserere* — the mercy-plea proper, the anatomy of sin as **four evils** answered by four litanies of doubled Latin+Greek imperatives. Boundary confirmed by read-ahead: §20 closes 331 with *Amove {occasiones/scandala}*; §21 *Quies animæ* opens immediately below.
- **The opening *Ne*-series (327).** Psalm-verbs, most doubled with the Greek. Coverdale/BCP governs the psalm-lines (§9): *Cito anticipent nos misericordiæ* = Ps 79:8 BCP "let thy loving mercy **speedily prevent** us" (the AV "let thy tender mercies speedily prevent us" agrees here); *Ne memineris, μὴ μνησθῇς* = Ps 79:8 BCP "O remember not our old sins." **Two technical BCP renderings kept:** *prevent* in its old sense "go before, come to meet" — *anticipent* (v.4) and the closing *præveniat me* ("let thy grace… prevent me," as the Collect "Prevent us, O Lord, in all our doings"). *Gratiam fac, χάρισαι* — Rom 8:32 "shall he not… freely give [χαρίσεται] us all things." *Ne statuas, Μὴ στήσῃς* — Acts 7:60, Stephen's "lay not this sin to their charge." The *Esto* brace: *placabilis / præstabilis / exorabilis* = "ready to be appeased / ready to make good / ready to be entreated" (Exod 32:12; Joel 2:13 "he… repenteth him of the evil"; Ps 77:7 "will the Lord absent himself for ever?" — the interrogative kept as *Nunquid in finem?* "Wilt thou be wroth for ever?").
- **328 — the four evils.** *Reatus / Macula / Languor / Servitus* rendered Guilt / Stain / Sickness / Bondage — the four heads under which the rest of §20 prays. Kept as a titled 2×2 table.
- **I. Guilt — Ὑπόδικον.** ὑπόδικος = "liable to judgement, under sentence" (Rom 3:19, "that all the world may become **guilty** before God"); glossed inline. Eleven imperatives of remission, several echoing their proof-texts: *Tolle, aufer, αἶρε* — John 1:29 "the Lamb of God, which **taketh away** the sin"; *Dissipa διασκέδασον* — Is 38:17 "thou hast cast all my sins behind thy back"; *Propitius esto ἱλάσθητι* — Luke 18:13 the publican's "God be merciful to me." *Repropitiare* ("be propitiated again," 1 Kgs 8:39) has no single English verb; rendered "be propitiated again."
- **II. Stain — Κηλίς / Δυσειδές.** "spot" and "the ill-favoured / unshapely thing" — the deformity of sin; both glossed. Ten imperatives of cleansing, doubling **Ps 51** (the *Miserere* psalm) and **Is 1:16–18**: *Averte faciem a peccatis* — Ps 51:9 "hide thy face from my sins"; the paired *Ne avertas a miseria* — Ps 44:24 "wherefore hidest thou thy face?"; *Munda / Purga / Ablue* — Ps 51:2 "wash me… cleanse me," Ps 19:12 "cleanse thou me from secret faults," Is 1:16 "wash you, make you clean"; *Dealba λεύκανον* — Is 1:18 "they shall be as white as snow"; *Dele ἀπάλειψον* — Is 44:22 "I have blotted out… thy transgressions." *Præteri ὑπέρβαινε* / *Dissimula ὑπέριδε* — Mic 7:18 "passeth by the transgression" / Acts 17:30 "the times of this ignorance God winked at [ὑπεριδών]."
- **III. Sickness — Πληγή / Τὸ νοσηρὸν σύντριμμα.** "wound" / "the diseased bruise"; glossed. Three imperatives of healing: *Cura θεράπευε / ἐπιμέλειαν ἔχε* — Luke 10:35, the Samaritan's "take care of him"; *Medere ἴασαι* — Ps 147:3 "he healeth the broken in heart"; *Sana*, "make whole." *Extirpa, κάλαμον κατεάγης* keeps the bruised-/broken-reed of Matt 12:20 ("a bruised reed shall he not break"); the printed *κατεάγης* is transcribed verbatim (flag in transcript) and rendered "the broken reed."
- **IV. Bondage — the longest limb (329–331).** Four sub-cycles. **(a)** *Vindica / Erue ‖ Libera / Salva* — "Avenge / Rescue ‖ Free / Save." **(b)** *Propono — Προαιρῶ* ("I purpose — I set my choice"), a six-fold resolve keyed to **2 Cor 7:11** (the affections of godly earnestness: ἐπιπόθησις "vehement desire," ζῆλος, σπουδή "diligence/carefulness," ἀπολογία "clearing of yourselves"); *Statui ἔστησα* = Ps 119:106 "I have sworn, and I will perform it." **(c)** the two *Quod {…}* braces of the vow — the negative turning-from-sin (turn away, forsake, depart, say "it sufficeth" [1 Pet 4:3, "the time past… may suffice us"], no more henceforth) and the positive turning-to-God (turn to Thee [Lam 3:40], turn my feet [Ps 119:59], lift up my hands [Lam 3:41], decline from evil [Ps 34:14], break my will, make the crooked straight, set a hedge [Hos 2:6]). **(d)** *Infirmus sum* — "I am weak," the **Rom 7** anatomy of the divided will: *Non facio quod volo…* (7:19), *Lex membrorum captivat* (7:23), *Quis me liberabit?* (7:24), with Is 37:3 "the children are come to the birth" (extremity without strength) and Ps 69:1 "the floods are come in unto my soul." **(e)** *Fer opem* — "Bring help," a 22-fold cry doubling the psalter's aid-verbs, opening on the office-versicle *Deus in adjutorium* (Ps 70:1, "O God, make speed to help me") and closing on Hos 2:6 "I will hedge up thy way with thorns" + *Amove {occasiones / scandala}* — "Remove {the occasions / the stumbling-blocks}."
- **Fer opem psalm-echoes (330–331), Coverdale/BCP (§9):** *Inclina cor, Κλῖνον καρδίαν* — Ps 119:36 "incline my heart"; *Dirige gressus, Ἴθυνον ἴχνη* — Ps 119:133 "order my steps in thy word"; *Ure renes, νεφρούς* — Ps 26:2 "try my reins and my heart"; *Confige carnes, σάρκας* — Ps 119:120, rendered "pierce my flesh [with thy fear]" after the Vulgate *confige timore tuo carnes meas* (BCP-AV "my flesh trembleth for fear of thee" — the Vulgate/Andrewes image is sharper, so followed and noted); *Trahe me post Te, εἵλξον* — Cant 1:4 "draw me, we will run after thee"; *In camo constringe* — Ps 32:9 "whose mouths must be held with bit and bridle"; *Ne ad vomitum* — 2 Pet 2:22; *Ne tentatio, nisi humana* — 1 Cor 10:13 "such as is common to man." Elliptical Latin (many lines are bare imperatives or objects) filled with the minimum bracketed supplement to stay line-keyed.
- **Cribs:** the governing texts are Ps 51 (Miserere), 2 Cor 7:11 (godly earnestness), and Rom 7 (the divided will); all psalm-lines checked against Coverdale/BCP first per §9. Brightman/Newman consulted after drafting (§12), not imported.
