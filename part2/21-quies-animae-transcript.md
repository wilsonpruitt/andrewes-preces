## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 331(mid)–333.** *Quies animæ* — "the rest / quiet of the soul," a catena of pure consolation set immediately after the *Miserere* plea: having cried for mercy, the soul now hears God's own words of promise back to it. Three sub-headed movements, **all Latin, all Scripture** (Andrewes' Vulgate-flavoured wording): **Quies animæ** (331–332), **Recordare** (332), **Misericordia superexaltans** (332–333). Boundary confirmed by read-ahead: §22 **CONFESSIO PECCATI** opens with a fresh centred all-caps heading on printed 334.
- **Quies animæ (331–332).** The soul's rest built from Genesis + Pentateuch promises: Gen 4:7 (*Adhuc peccatum excubat præ foribus* — "sin lieth at the door," the one *warning* note, cf. AV; Andrewes/Vulgate has sin "keeping watch/couching" at the door); Gen 18:32 (*Non delerem propter decem* — "I will not destroy it for ten's sake," Abraham's plea for Sodom); Gen 22:14 (*In monte providebit Dominus* — "In the mount the LORD will provide," Jehovah-Jireh); the great **Exod 34:6–7 mercy-formula** (*Dominator, Dominus Deus, misericors et clemens…* — "The LORD, the LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth… forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin"); Lev 26:40–42 (affliction → confession → "then will I remember my covenant"); Deut 30:1–6 (the return from exile of the heart — "the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart… to love the LORD"); closing on **Ps 42:11/12** (*Quare tristis es, anima mea?* — "Why art thou cast down, O my soul?"), which spills into 332 (*Spera in Deo…* "hope thou in God") and Ps 116:7 (*Convertere, anima mea, in requiem tuam* — "Return unto thy rest, O my soul" — the verse that names the whole section, *Quies animæ*). Ps-lines Coverdale/BCP (§9).
- **Recordare (332).** "Remember" — the soul asks God to remember his word (Ps 119:49, *Memor esto verborum Tuorum servo Tuo* — "Remember thy word unto thy servant, upon which thou hast caused me to hope"; v.116 "Uphold me according unto thy word, that I may live"), then rehearses how God *does* remember: Ps 103:9–10 ("He will not always chide… he hath not dealt with us after our sins") and Ps 78:38–39 (*Recordatus est quia caro sumus* — "he remembered that they were but flesh; a wind that passeth away, and cometh not again"). Ps-lines Coverdale/BCP (§9) — note BCP Ps 103:9 "neither keepeth he his anger for ever."
- **Misericordia superexaltans (332–333).** "Mercy that rises above / triumphs over [judgement]" (cf. Jas 2:13, *superexultat misericordia judicio*). The Isaiah/Ezekiel invitation to return: Is 1:18 ("though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow"); Is 30:15 ("in returning and rest shall ye be saved"); Is 42:3 (the **bruised reed** — *Calamum quassatum non confringet* — here spelled out in full, cf. the abbreviated κάλαμον form flagged in §20); Is 43:25 ("I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake"); Is 44:22 ("Return unto me; for I have redeemed thee"); Is 46:4 ("even to your old age I am he… I will carry, and will deliver you"); the **Suffering-Servant** of Is 53:4–6 (*Vere languores nostros Ipse tulit* — "Surely he hath borne our griefs… with his stripes we are healed… the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all"); Is 65:24 ("before they call, I will answer"); and the great Ezekiel refrains, Ez 18:23/30/32 & 33:11 (*Vivo Ego; nolo mortem impii* — "As I live… I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live"), closing on Is 55:7 ("let him return unto the LORD… for he will abundantly pardon" — *quia multus est ad ignoscendum*). Follow AV cadence where the Latin agrees; these are prophets, not Psalms, so AV is the model (§9).
