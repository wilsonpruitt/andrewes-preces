## Translator's flags

- **Parity 2/2** against the two page-markers (322 mid, 323 top). *Allegatio* rendered "The Plea" — the **second** of Part II's two Allegationes (§7 was the forensic six-count plea). This one alleges the suppliant's **mortality** as the ground of mercy: "Remember what my substance is" (Ps 89:47).
- **The frailty-brace:** the four biblical figures of mortality kept paired — *dust and ashes* (Gen 18:27, "which am but dust and ashes"), *grass and a flower* (Isa 40:6, "all flesh is grass, and all the goodliness thereof is as the flower"), *flesh and a passing breath* (Ps 78:39, "a wind that passeth away"), *rottenness and a worm* (Job 17:14). Rendered *spiritus vadens* "a passing breath" (the fleeting *ruach*, not the Holy Spirit).
- **The mortality-catena:** stranger and sojourner (Ps 39:12), house of clay (Job 4:19, "them that dwell in houses of clay"), *few and evil days* (Gen 47:9, Jacob to Pharaoh — kept the AV cadence), *today not tomorrow* (Prov 27:1), *morning not evening* (Isa 38:13, Hezekiah). The sequence tightens the span of life clause by clause.
- **The cascading close:** the stair-step indent preserved — *now and not by and by* (*Nunc et non statim*), *in the body of death* (Rom 6:6; the *corpus mortis* of 7:24), *in a world of corruption* (2 Pet 1:4), *lying in the wicked one* (1 John 5:19, "the whole world lieth in wickedness"), *Remember this* (Ps 74:18). The descending indentation is the section's shape — the soul sinking through its own mortality to the final "Remember this." §16 ends; the Confession of Sins (§17) follows.
