# Part II — Preces Quotidianæ — §29 *Actus Adorationis* (An Act of Adoration) — English

Line-keyed English of `29-actus-adorationis-transcript.md` (printed 358 – 359 upper). **Pure Latin source — no Greek in this section**; translated from the Latin (§6). Register thou-form AV/BCP (§8–10), and specifically **the BCP Litany's** wording for the three Persons-vocatives, which Andrewes is quoting (see flag 1). Brace triads inline `{ a / b / c }`. One continuous act of adoration: three Persons with three works apiece, the Trinity-address, the two *Whose* triads, and the four-fold *Give* — for the living, the departed, the Church, and sinners. Parity target 2/2.

## Translator's flags

1. **The three vocatives are the Litany, and are given in the BCP's words.** *Pater de cœlis Deus / Fili Redemptor mundi Deus / Spiritus Sancte Paraclete Deus* → **"O God the Father, of heaven / O God the Son, Redeemer of the world / O God the Holy Ghost."** Andrewes is quoting a text his readers said aloud every Sunday; translating the Latin afresh ("Father, God from heaven") would make a familiar thing strange and hide what he is doing. *Paraclete* is given as **the Comforter**, the BCP's word, with the Greek term noted here.

2. **Nine works, three per Person, and the ninth changes tense.** The Father's and the Son's clauses are all aorist-past (*creasti, tradidisti, voluisti, abluisti, ascendisti*); the Spirit's third — *qui corda Sanctorum Tua gratia visitas et confirmas* — is **present**: "who **dost** visit and confirm." It is the only clause without a scripture reference, because it is not an event but the work going on now, in the man praying. Kept, and it should not be harmonised to the past.

3. **`Beata, benedicta` splits into two English words.** *Beata* is blessed-as-happy (the Trinity's own beatitude); *benedicta* is blessed-as-spoken-well-of (blessed by us). Rendered **"Happy, blessed"** — the only way to keep both without a gloss. English has one word where the Latin has two, and that is a real loss here.

4. **`semper laudabilis, semper tamen ineffabilis` — "ever to be praised, YET ever unspeakable."** The *tamen* is not "but"; it is the concession that makes the adoration an adoration. Praise is owed precisely where praise cannot arrive.

5. **`Pater bone, Fili pie, Spiritus benigne` — three near-synonyms kept distinct.** *Bone* = good; *pie* = **merciful** (the Latin *pietas* of covenant-faithfulness toward one's own, not "pious"); *benigne* = gracious, open-handed. Flattening all three to "gracious" would collapse a deliberate triad.

6. **`Cujus opus vita, amor gratia, contemplatio gloria`** — three verbless equations, kept clipped: **whose work is life, love is grace, contemplation is glory**. To be made, to be loved, to see him: the whole economy in six words, and expanding them into clauses destroys it.

7. **⚠ `defunctis requiem et lucem perpetuam` — "to the departed rest and light perpetual."** The Requiem's own words (*requiem æternam … et lux perpetua luceat eis*), in the private devotions of a Jacobean English bishop, set in a plain series beside mercy for the living, truth for the Church, pardon for sinners. **Translated plainly, unsoftened and unbracketed.** The edition prints what Andrewes prayed; whatever is to be said about it belongs in an introduction, not in the translation.

8. **The two relative clauses are a matched pair and stay parallel** — *whom the present world yet holdeth in the flesh* / *whom the world to come, unclothed of the body, hath already received*. **`exutos corpore` = "unclothed of the body"** (2 Cor. 5:4, "not for that we would be unclothed"). The four-fold *Give* that follows is built on this pairing; break the parallel and the prayer for the departed loses its ground.

9. **`Qui vivorum dominaris simul et mortuorum` (Rom. 14:9)** — *simul* = **alike**, at once: one lordship over both, which is exactly the warrant for the two-fold gift on the lines below. The AV's "Lord both of the dead and living" is behind it; the order is reversed to follow the Latin.

10. **`toto cordis affectu nunc, et in sæcula benedico`** — the *nunc* and the *in sæcula* hang on either side of the verb: now, and unto all ages. Kept split across the two printed lines as they stand, so the "now" belongs to the adoring and the "for ever" to the blessing.
