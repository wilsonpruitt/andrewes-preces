## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 286–290.** *Sacrificium Vespertinum* (a brief vesper opening) + *Horologium* ("the Clock/Book-of-Hours") — the **most characteristically Andrewes structure in Part II**: a walk through the canonical hours (3rd, 6th, 7th, 9th, 10th, 11th, the Supper-hour, evening, deep night, midnight, cock-crow, the unknown hour, dawn), each hour keyed to a Gospel event and answered by the refrain *Et serva nos* ("And save us"). Keep the refrain uniform and the hour-headings parallel ("Who at the Nth hour …").
- **The hours-to-events map** (translate the event, keep the hour): 3rd = Pentecost (Acts 2:15); 6th = Crucifixion (Matt 27:45) *and* Peter's sheet-vision (Acts 10:9–11); 7th = the nobleman's son's fever left him (John 4:52); 9th = Christ tasted death (Mark 15:34) and the hour of prayer (Acts 3:1); 10th = Andrew's "We have found the Messias" (John 1:41); 11th = the last labourers hired (Matt 20:6–9); Supper-hour = institution of the Eucharist (John 13:2 / 1 Cor 11); evening = the burial (Mark 15:42); deep night = the giving of the power to bind/loose (John 20:22–23); midnight = David & Paul singing (Ps 119:62 / Acts 16:25) and the Bridegroom's cry (Matt 25:6); cock-crow = Peter's repentance (Matt 26:74–75); the unknown day/hour = the Advent (Matt 24:50); dawn = light (Ps 43:3, 74:16, Matt 5:45).
- **`Chirographum` (287):** Col 2:14 — the "handwriting of ordinances that was against us," which Christ blotted out and took out of the way (*tollens de medio*). Use the AV "handwriting… against us… took it out of the way."
- **`ne ad judicium… sed ad peccati remissionem` (288):** 1 Cor 11:29 — receive the Sacrament "not unto judgment/damnation, but unto remission of sin"; the *legata Novi Testamenti* = "the legacies/bequests of the New Testament [Covenant]" — kept the testamentary metaphor (a will's bequests).
- **`sed quoad remissionem, ne ad retentionem` (288):** John 20:23 — Andrewes prays to share the apostolic power of the keys *only as to remitting*, not retaining (he wants mercy's half of the power, not judgement's). A pointed devotional turn; render clearly.
- **`Mane nobiscum, Domine… quia advesperascit` (290):** Luke 24:29, the Emmaus "Abide with us, for it is toward evening" — Andrewes applies it to the evening *of life* (*inclinata est jam dies vitæ nostræ*, "the day of our life is now far spent"). Use the AV "Abide with us… for it is toward evening, and the day is far spent."
- **Inline Greek (290):** `Paracletus, ἄλλος` = "the Paraclete, *another* [Comforter]" (John 14:16, ἄλλον παράκλητον). Keep the Greek; the four Spirit-titles *Paracletus / Unctio / Sigillum / Arrhabo* = Comforter / Unction (1 John 2:27) / Seal (Eph 4:30) / Earnest (2 Cor 1:22, 5:5) — a compact pneumatology; kept the AV terms.
- **The Trinity-works brace (290):** `Opera { Creatoris → Justitia / Redemptoris → Misericordia / Spiritus Sancti → Sancta Spiratio }` — the works of Creator/Redeemer/Spirit are Justice/Mercy/Holy Spiration. *Sancta Spiratio* = "Holy Spiration/Breathing" (the technical term for the Spirit's procession — the Spirit's proper "work" is the breathing-forth of love). Kept "Holy Spiration" with the note.
- Psalm cribs (§9): Ps 119:55 ("thought upon thy Name in the night-season, and have kept thy law"), Ps 65:8 ("outgoings of the morning and evening to praise thee"), Ps 127:3 ("he giveth his beloved sleep"), Ps 51:11 ("take not thy holy Spirit from me"), Ps 63:6 ("upon my bed… in the night-watches"), Ps 4:6 ("lift thou up the light of thy countenance"), Ps 36:9 ("in thy light shall we see light"), Ps 147:9 ("feedeth the young ravens that call upon him"), Ps 71:5 ("thou art… my hope from my youth"), Ps 102:11 ("my days are gone like a shadow"). Kept BCP wording.
