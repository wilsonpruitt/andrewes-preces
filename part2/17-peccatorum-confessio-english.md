## Translator's flags

- **Parity 2/2** against the two page-markers (323 mid, 324 top). *Peccatorum Confessio* — the Confession of Sins proper, opening with the bare *Peccavi*, "I have sinned." Two parts: **original** sin, then the **fourteen-fold** catalogue of **actual** sin in a downward spiral.
- **Original sin (323):** *Quis potest facere mundum de immundo semine?* (Job 14:4, AV "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean?"). *Peccator sum { ex immundo semine / ex immundo utero }* — kept the brace ("of unclean seed / of unclean womb"). *In peccatis concepit me mater* (Ps 51:5, the Miserere, Coverdale "in sin hath my mother conceived me"). The two graft-figures: **a root of bitterness** (Heb 12:15) and **a shoot of the wild olive** (*oleaster*, Rom 11:17) — the fallen nature as bitter root and uncultivated scion, the opposite of the good olive into which grace grafts.
- **The fourteen-fold catalogue** rendered as a mounting confession, each verb first-person perfect ("I have sinned… cast away… rejected… grieved… gone after… passed… feared not… returned not…"). The progression is deliberate: sin, then refusal of law and discipline, then grieving the Spirit, then willful wandering, then insensibility to every remedy — *not returned, not when called back, not when afflicted* — then hardening, then provoking. It descends from act to disposition to defiance.
- **The close (324):** *(14) Et hæc omnia vidisti* (Lam 3:59, "thou hast seen my wrong") — *Et tacuisti* (Ps 50:21, "these things hast thou done, and I kept silence"). The most fearful line of the confession: God has **seen all** and **held His peace** — the silence of forbearance, not of consent (Ps 50:21 continues, "thou thoughtest that I was altogether such an one as thyself; but I will reprove thee"). Kept the two lines as the hinge into the Aggravatio. §17 ends; the Aggravation of sin (§18) follows.
- **No Greek.** *1 Reg.* = 1 Kings in the Vulgate's 1–4 Reg. numbering (viii.47 = Solomon's temple-prayer; xvi.33 = Ahab). Psalm-lines Coverdale/BCP; the catalogue draws the prophets (Isaiah, Jeremiah, Lamentations) and Proverbs.
