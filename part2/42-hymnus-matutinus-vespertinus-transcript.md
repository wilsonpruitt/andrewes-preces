## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ THE FOUNT TRAP IS BACK, and it bites four times on these two pages.** Per the ruling settled at §34 (600 dpi, `raw/end/crop-upsilon-367-390.jpg`), this fount **draws capital upsilon with flat arms so that it reads as Τ**. On these leaves the affected words are the heading **ΥΜΝΟΣ** (twice, 388 and 390) and, in the italic, **Υἱὲ / Υἱὸς / Υἱὸν / Υἱὲ** — printed `Τἱὲ`, `Τἱὸς`, `Τἱὸν`, `Τἱὲ`. Re-verified here at 600 dpi (`crop-388-head-425.jpg`, `crop-388-huios-425.jpg`, `crop-390-huion-427.jpg`). **Transcribed Υ; this is a reading of the sort, NOT a silent emendation** — and `Τἱὸς` is not a Greek word, so there is nothing to weigh. A later pass working from the 200-dpi images must not "restore" the tau.

2. **⚠⚠ THE ASTERISK ROWS ARE CROSS-COLUMN ALIGNMENT MARKS, and this is the structural key to the whole section.** Eight asterisks appear **twice**, and each time in *the column that lacks matter the facing column has*:
   - printed **389 (Latin)**, after *Jesu Christe.* — because the Greek at 388 has **καὶ ἅγιον Πνεῦμα** there and the Western Latin Gloria has no such clause;
   - printed **390 (Greek)**, after *Ἰησοῦς Χριστός,* — because the Latin at 391 has **cum Sancto Spiritu,** there and the Alexandrine Greek has no such clause.
   Verified on the facing leaves, not inferred (`crop-390-oti-427.jpg` beside PDF 428). So the 1853 is doing something careful: **it marks, in each column, the exact line where the other column carries a clause it does not**, and the two marks are on opposite sides of the same doctrinal question — where in the Gloria the Holy Ghost is named. **Both rows must be preserved in both layers and in the print mirror.** They are not ornament and they are not a lacuna in the text; deleting them would silently level two recensions into one, which is the opposite of what this edition is for.

3. **⚠ CONSEQUENCE FOR THE ENGLISH: on the Gloria the two columns are two RECENSIONS, not text-and-translation.** The Latin at 389/391 is the **received Western Gloria**, not a rendering of the Greek at 388/390 — hence the asterisks, hence *bonæ voluntates* against εὐδοκία, hence the missing fifth verb. **The English therefore translates the Greek** (CONVENTIONS §6) and the Latin's divergences are recorded in flags rather than blended in. The Φῶς ἱλαρόν is the opposite case: *Lumen hilare* **is** a close Latin rendering of its Greek, tracking it line for line and indent for indent, and there the two columns are text and translation in the ordinary way.

4. **The Greek Gloria has FIVE verbs of praise where the Latin and the BCP have four.** Αἰνοῦμεν / εὐλογοῦμεν / προσκυνοῦμεν / **δοξολογοῦμεν** / εὐχαριστοῦμεν, against *Laudamus / benedicimus / adoramus / glorificamus / gratias agimus* — the Latin has five too, but the fifth Greek verb **δοξολογοῦμέν σε** is a distinct word from δοξάζω and the Latin *glorificamus* answers it only approximately. Kept and translated as printed; the English must not collapse two of the five to match the Prayer Book's four-verb habit.

5. **`ἐν ἀνθρώποις εὐδοκία` is NOMINATIVE, and so is the Latin `bonæ voluntates`.** The Greek says *good will among men* (peace on earth, good will among men), not the Vulgate's *hominibus bonæ voluntatis* ("to men of good will"). **The 1853's Latin agrees with the Greek against the Vulgate** — and prints the plural *voluntates*, which is odd but is what stands. Both kept as printed; this is a real textual agreement between the two columns and should not be flattened.

6. **`Κύριε βασιλεῦ ἐπουράνιε` has no `Θεός`** where the Latin has *Domine Deus Rex cœlestis* and the BCP "O Lord God, heavenly King." The English follows the Greek and drops "God," with the divergence recorded here.

7. **`Ἰησοῦς Χριστὸς` at printed 390 is NOMINATIVE, not vocative** — "thou only art the Lord, Jesus Christ" — where the Latin has the vocative *Jesu Christe* and the BCP "thou only, O Christ." Kept as printed; the case difference is visible and is not corrected.

8. **The Greek Gloria lacks `ὁ ὕψιστος`** — it ends *εἰς δόξαν Θεοῦ Πατρός*, "unto the glory of God the Father," with no "art most high." The Latin agrees (*in gloria Dei Patris*), so here it is the **BCP** that has the extra, not the Latin. Not supplied.

9. **⚠ The footnote's reference is WRONG and is kept as printed.** *C. Plinius in Epistola **xxvii.** lib. x.* — the letter about the Christians who met *stato die ante lucem* and sang *Carmen Christo quasi Deo* is **Pliny, Ep. x. 96** (numbered 97 in some editions), not 27. Almost certainly a misprint for **xcvii**. **Kept as printed and flagged**, joining `[Jer. l. 24.]` §23, `[Eph. i. 8.]` §27, `[Dan. vi. 4.]` §28, `[Psal. cxix. 114.]` §34, and *ante Intercessionum* §40. The note's claim itself — that the Gloria *may perhaps* (*forsitan*) be the very hymn Pliny heard of — is the 1853's conjecture and is translated as a conjecture, not a finding.

10. **`Ἄξιος εἶ ἐν πᾶσι καιροῖς` is second person, against the received Φῶς ἱλαρόν's impersonal `ἄξιόν ἐστιν`.** The common text reads *ἄξιόν ἐστιν ἐν πᾶσι καιροῖς ὑμνεῖσθαί σε*, "it is meet at all times that thou be hymned"; this text has **"Worthy art thou"**, and the Latin agrees (*Dignus es*). Both columns carry the second person, so it is the recension, not a slip. Kept.

11. **`ζωὴν ὁ διδούς` — the article makes it a title, not a clause.** "O Son of God, **the giver of life**" (Latin *vitæ dator*), the credal epithet (τὸ ζωοποιόν), rather than "who givest life." The Latin's flat noun-phrase confirms the reading.

12. **A blotted sort at printed 388: the delta of `δόξαν` (line *διὰ τὴν μεγάλην σου δόξαν*) is inked in solid** and reads as a blob at 200 dpi. Resolved at 600 dpi (`crop-388-doxan-425.jpg`) — the word is δόξαν, and the defect is ink, not a wrong sort. Transcribed normally, recorded here so a later pass does not raise it again.

13. **⚠ INDENTATION IS LOAD-BEARING IN THIS SECTION and is transcribed exactly.** The Latin recto reproduces the Greek verso's indent levels line for line — most visibly in the Φῶς ἱλαρόν, where the cascade *ὑμνοῦμεν Πατέρα / καὶ Υἱὸν / καὶ ἅγιον Πνεῦμα Θεοῦ* is answered by *laudamus Patrem / Filium / et Sanctum Spiritum Dei* at the same three depths. In the Prototype B mirror the two pages are read across, so **an indent changed on one side silently breaks the parallel on the other.** Note one exception kept as printed: the Latin at 391 opens *Quoniam Tu solus sanctus* one level in, where the Greek at 390 opens Ὅτι σὺ εἶ μόνος ἅγιος flush.

14. **No `[?]` in this section.** All four leaves are clean, and the Greek was read at 600 dpi on five crops (`crop-388-head`, `crop-388-doxan`, `crop-388-huios`, `crop-390-oti`, `crop-390-huion`).
