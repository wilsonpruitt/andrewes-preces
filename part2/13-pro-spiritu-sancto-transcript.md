## Translator's flags

- **Section = printed 316(mid)–318(top).** A second **CONFESSIO LAUDUM** heading (distinct from §10's) introduces the subsection **Pro Spiritu Sancto** — "For the Holy Spirit," a thanksgiving over the Spirit's whole work. Opens with the first stanza of the **Veni, Creator Spiritus** (Rabanus Maurus) — kept as the received Latin hymn; render in the traditional English ("Come, Creator Spirit, / visit the minds of Thine own, / fill with grace from on high / the hearts which Thou hast made"). It is the doxological counterpart to §11–12 (the Son's Passion & glory) now turned to the Spirit.
- **In veteri Testamento (316):** the Spirit's OT operations — the brooding on the waters (Gen 1:2 *incubatio*), the sending-forth into living things (Gen 1:20), the inbreathing of man (Gen 2:7), the filling of Bezaleel (Exod 31:3), the Seventy elders (Num 11:25), the descent on the prophets (1 Sam 10:10). *Incubatio aquarum* = "the brooding upon the waters."
- **Adventus visibilis / invisibilis (316–317):** the Spirit's **visible comings** — 1. as **shadow** (*Umbra*), the overshadowing at the Conception (Luke 1:35); 2. as **dove** (*Columba*) at the Baptism (Matt 3:16); 3. as **breath** (*Habitus* — the "manner/form" of Christ's breathing, John 20:22, "Receive ye the Holy Ghost"); 4. as **fiery tongues** (*Linguæ igneæ*) at Pentecost (Acts 2:3). The left-margin labels (Umbra / Columba / Habitus / Linguæ igneæ) name the four forms — keep them as a left column. Then the **invisible comings**: on the gathered at prayer (Acts 4:31), on Cornelius (Acts 10:44), on the twelve Ephesians (Acts 19:6).
- **The -κλησις wordplay (317) — the jewel of the section.** *Visitatio subinde per vices* ("His visitation from time to time, by turns") unfolds in three modes, each Latin term paralleled by a Greek noun in -κλησις:
  - **1. In vocatione** (in calling) = the trio **Ἔκκλησις** (calling-out), **Ἀνάκλησις** (calling-back), **Ἐπανάκλησις** (re-calling) — set beside the Latin *Avocatione a peccato / Evocatione e mundo / Revocatione a recidiva* (a calling-away from sin, a calling-forth from the world, a calling-back from relapse).
  - **2. Invocatione** (in invocation) = **Ἐπίκλησις** (the *epiclesis*, the calling-upon).
  - **3. Advocatione** (in advocacy/comforting) = **Παράκλησις** (the Paraclete's work, comfort/advocacy).
  Keep BOTH columns (Latin ` | ` Greek); the whole point is the vocatio/‑κλησις echo — the Spirit as the God who calls, is called upon, and comforts. Render the Greek transliterated-and-glossed in the English so the wordplay survives.
- **Divisio (317):** the threefold *distributio* of 1 Cor 12:4–6 — **Gratiarum** (of gifts/*charismata*), **Ministrationum** (of ministrations), **Operationum** (of operations) — then named again as *Charismata Spiritus, Opera, Fructus* (the Spirit's gifts, works, fruits). Keep the brace.
- **The works list (317–318):** thirteen operations of the Spirit, each with its text — conviction (*compunctio redarguentis*, John 16:8), the teaching unction (1 John 2:20), the bringing-to-remembrance (John 14:26), the shedding-abroad of love (Rom 5:5), the help of our weakness in prayer (Rom 8:26), the witness of adoption (Rom 8:16), the sealing in the mysteries (2 Cor 1:22), the earnest of experience (*arrhabo*, Eph 1:14). *Arrhabo* = "earnest/pledge" (ἀρραβών).
- **The seven-fold *Visitatio* with right-column glosses (318):** Visitation, Indwelling, Purging, Enlightening, Strengthening, Adorning, Perfecting — with three glosses in the right column: **Visitatio → Invisere cor** ("to visit the heart"), **Illustratio → Illuminatio** ("illumination"), **Perfectio → Perductio** ("a leading-through / bringing-home"). Keep the glosses apposed (` | `). This is the Spirit's inward itinerary from first visit to final perfecting.
- **Close (318):** *Dux veritatis* (the Guide into truth, John 16:13) and *Subministratio virtutis* (the supply of power/strength). §13 ends here; **Triumphus misericordiæ (§14)** follows.
- **Scripture cribs:** OT operations (AV); the Spirit-works are Gospel/Epistle (AV). *Veni Creator* in the traditional English hymn form, not a fresh translation.
