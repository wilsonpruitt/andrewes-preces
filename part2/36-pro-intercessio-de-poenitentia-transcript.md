## Translator's flags

1. **⚠ WATCH ITEM — this section has no head, and the defect is in the printing.** Printed 371 opens cold with *Pro spiritu vulneratis*, in the middle of an intercession: no title, no opening petition, no *Pro* clause for the Church, the King, the bishops or the household, which is where every intercession in Part I begins. Printed 370 ends a full third of the way up the page, so nothing was crowded out. **Confirmed not a scan artifact** — the leaves are consecutive (PDF 397, 398), there is no duplicate in this stretch, and the OCR text layer reads the two pages in the same order with nothing between. Restore from Brightman or Harley 6616 before print. **Neither layer supplies a heading**; the transcript begins where the plate begins.

2. **`præcipue non orantibus` is the last line of the litany and its point** — "chiefly for them that pray not." The whole roll of the afflicted (the wounded in spirit, the dying, the prisoner, the poor, the stranger, the woman in travail, the traveller) ends by turning to those who are not praying at all, which includes everyone the preceding lines have described as past praying. Do not soften it into "especially those who cannot pray": *non orantibus* is the ones who do not, not the ones who cannot.

3. **`animam agentibus` = the dying** (*animam agere*, to be giving up the ghost), not "the busy" or "the strong-souled." It sits between the wounded in spirit and the sick taking medicine, and it is the strongest word on the page.

4. **⚠ *De Pœnitentia* is a SCHEMA, not a prayer** — like §24's *Divisio* and its *Per.* table. It is an outline of the parts of penitence (Contrition → Confession) with its scripture warrants, then a **numbered argument in three steps**: 1. all have sinned (with three proofs) · 2. what then? is it for nothing? · 3. God gave repentance unto life (with its assurances, then the six gerunds of God's soliciting). **The English must not turn the outline into sentences**; it stays an outline, and the ellipses stay elliptical.

5. **`secunda tabula` is the technical term and must not be flattened to a metaphor.** *Quasi { civitas refugii / secunda tabula }* — penitence as **the second plank after shipwreck** (Jerome's *secunda post naufragium tabula*, the standard scholastic tag for penance after baptism), set beside the city of refuge. "A second chance" would destroy it.

6. **`Ἀγανάκτησις` (2 Cor. vii. 11) is the one Greek word on the page, and it is §19's word.** The anatomy of godly sorrow in §19 used the same term among its five affects — **indignation**, the sinner's anger at himself. Use the same English here as there; the repetition is Andrewes' cross-reference to his own earlier page.

7. **Elliptical lines kept elliptical, all of them.** *Locus relictus est remissioni, **si*** [Heb. xii. 17] / *cubat tantum peccatum* [Gen. iv. 7] — "there is place left for pardon, **if** … sin do but couch [at the door]"; *Quid tum?  **Nunquid in vanum.***; *Promittendo, **si***; *Minando, **nisi***. Andrewes writes the conjunction and lets the reference finish the sentence. **Nothing is completed** in either layer; the reference is the completion.

8. **⚠ LAYOUT: the two tables at the foot of 372 have braces spanning rows, and are encoded with ` | ` — the §32 exception, used again and disclosed.** As printed: three rows *Oratio/Jejunium/Eleemosyna* with their objects *animam/corpus/bonum*, brace-joined to a single **immolat**; then three rows *Dæmonem/Carnem/Mundum* brace-joined to a single **vincit**, with a third column *Deo./Nobis./Proximo.* standing to its right. Collapsing either to an inline brace would break the row-alignment, which is the whole content: **prayer offers the soul, conquers the devil, and is toward God; fasting offers the body, conquers the flesh, and is toward ourselves; almsgiving offers our goods, conquers the world, and is toward our neighbour.** The three Lenten works read across, not down.

9. **`Job. ix. [3.]` — the verse number is the 1853 editor's, in brackets, and is kept in brackets.** Likewise `[Ibid.]` under the *contritum* member (Ps. li. 17 again, "a broken and a contrite heart," which supplies two of the three brace-members from one verse).

10. **No `[?]` in this section.** Both plates are clean; the OCR agrees with the vision transcription throughout, including the two-column gerunds and the *Quasi* brace.
