# Part III — Preces Quotidianæ — §3 *Misericordia Dei* — raw transcript

Source: 1853 Parker ed., printed pp. 416 (foot)–424 (PDF 459–467), images `raw/end/p-NNN.jpg`. Offset **+43 throughout**, every leaf eye-verified; `3 H` at printed 417 and `3 I` at 425 bracket the section. **LATIN throughout**, no Greek and no Hebrew. The left-margin reference column of §2 continues here and runs to the end.

**Untitled in the 1853** — the slug is ours. What the plate gives is three small-caps sub-headings (MISERICORDIA EJUS · MISERICORDIA DEI · PATER MISERICORDIARUM) and then a numbered series **1.–10.** on the kinds of God's mercy. **A SCHEMA, not a prayer** for its first nine tenths — the §24 *Divisio* / §36 *De Pœnitentia* / §41 *Monita* discipline governs: keep it an outline, make no line a sentence. At head **10.** (printed 422) it turns, and from *Et Tu, Domine, remitte* to the end it **is** prayer.

**⚠ BOUNDARIES — both ends verified, and the §2/§3 cut now stands.** §2 ends mid-416 on *Beatus vir qui sperat in Eo*; this section ends on printed **424**, whose lower two-thirds are blank, and printed **425 opens GRATIARUM ACTIO** under a fresh centred all-caps heading. So the mercy-schema does **not** close by answering §2's nine pleas — it makes its own argument and ends in its own prayer. The provisional call recorded in §2's flag 1 is therefore confirmed, and the two files stay separate.

Transcription discipline: verbatim incl. 1853 accentuation and punctuation; `[?]` = unreadable; indentation = 4 spaces per printed level; book abbreviations italicized exactly as printed; `[ ]` = the 1853 editor's supplied references; **a reference at the START of a line was printed in the margin column, a reference trailing its line was printed inline** (the §2 convention); braces per CONVENTIONS §2–4. Running heads, catchwords and printer's signatures omitted.

## Transcription notes

1. **Offset +43 across all nine leaves**, eye-verified; no duplicates in this stretch. Printed 419 = PDF 462 and 425 = PDF 468 both match STRUCTURE's Phase 0 anchors exactly.

2. **⚠ BRACES INSIDE THE REFERENCE COLUMN — new, and the layout's one real novelty.** At printed 417 two entries are governed by a **brace joining two or three margin references** to a single word of text, which itself braces two members: `{ xxxvi. 5. / cviii. 4. }   Altæ { ad cœlos, / super cœlos. }` and `{ xxv. 6. / ciii. 17. / xiii. 6. }   Æternæ { Ante, / Post. }`. Printed 421 opens with the same figure at its largest — three references braced to three participles. Encoded inline per CONVENTIONS §2 rather than as ` | ` rows, because both sides are genuine brace groups and neither is a column of independent cells. **`transcript2tex.py` must be able to set a brace in the apparatus column**, which it has never had to do.

3. **`Tuâ.` at printed 421** — set with what appears to be a circumflex, where the sense wants a plain ablative *Tua*. Transcribed as printed with an inline unclear-print comment; **verify on a cleaner digitization before print** — at 200 dpi a fallen accent and a damaged sort look alike, and the OCR is no help on diacritics.

4. **`*Syr.* ii. 18.` (417)** — Ecclesiasticus ii. 18, abbreviated from *Syriacides*/Sirach. The volume's usual abbreviation is *Ecclus.* (§40, printed 383) and Part III §1 used *Eccl.* (398); this is a **third** form for the same book. Kept as printed; recorded so no pass normalises the three.

5. **⚠ A wrong reference — and the volume again proves its own:** `*Matt.* xi. 25.` (423) for *Venite ad Me, qui laboratis* is **Matt. xi. 28**. And the converse happens at printed 421: `*Psal.* cxix. 49.` is cited **correctly** for *Memor esto verbi Tui servo Tuo* — the very verse §34 misnumbered as `[Psal. cxix. 114.]` (printed 367). Both kept as printed; the pair is now the **third** case in Part III of the 1853 supplying, elsewhere in its own pages, the proof that a reference is wrong (after `Job. xiv. 14.` vs §17 and `Is. xxxiii. 15.` vs §37). Two further references are doubtful and left unadjudicated: `*Psal.* cvi. 47.` (419, the verse is cvi. 44) and `*Jer.* v. 11.` (419, which does not obviously carry *misericors et miserator*).

6. **⚠ The apostolic series at printed 423 numbers a witness it never announces.** *Nec frustra dixerat Apostolus Tuus* (Paul) · *Nec frustra **alter** Apostolus* (Peter) · *Nec frustra **quartus*** (John) — but under *alter* the plate sets **two** citations, 1 Pet. iii. 18 and *Jac.* ii. 13, so **James is the third and is never named as such**. Nothing supplied; the ordinal stands as printed and the silent third is flagged, not filled.

7. **No `[?]`, no Greek, no Hebrew.** Nine clean leaves.
