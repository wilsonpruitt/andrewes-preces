## Transcriber's notes

1. **Parity: 2 printed pages / 2 pages, line-exact.** Printed 431 carries 27 lines of text under the heading; printed 432 carries 31. Both counts verified against a per-line ink-band measurement of the 200-dpi images, not by eye alone.

2. **⚠ `[Te Deum.]` (431) is the first NON-SCRIPTURAL source-tag the reference column has carried** anywhere in the volume. It is bracketed exactly like the editor's supplied scripture refs, and it is correct: *Dignare, Domine, die isto sine peccato nos custodire* is the Te Deum's own line, which Andrewes has turned to *nos hodie*. **Kept as printed; do not expand it into a scripture reference.**

3. **⚠ Wrong references, kept as printed and never mended** (the running discipline; Part III has now added more bad refs than any other stretch of the volume):
   - **`lxxiv. 6.`** for Ps. lxxiv. **16** — *Tuus est dies, et Tua est nox* is Vulg. Ps. lxxiii. 16 / AV Ps. lxxiv. 16 / BCP lxxiv. 17. Under no numbering is it verse 6. Almost certainly a dropped initial `1`.
   - **`cxix. 81.`** for Ps. cxix. **91** — *Ordinatione Tua perseverat dies, nam omnia Tibi serviunt* is verse 91 verbatim (Vulg. cxviii. 91). Verse 81 is *Defecit in salutare Tuum anima mea*. A transposed pair of figures, the same mechanism as the garbled Hebrew at printed 386.
   - **`cxxxiv. 4.`** for Ps. **cxxxii**. 4 — *somnum ab oculis … dormitationem a palpebris* is Vulg. Ps. cxxxi. 4 / AV Ps. cxxxii. 4. Ps. cxxxiv has only three verses, so the reference cannot stand on any reckoning.
   - **The Ps. cxliii run (432) numbers one high from its second member on.** Printed `8. / 9. / 10. / 11. / 12.` against AV/BCP `8a / 8b / 9 / 10a / 10b`. Only the first is right. Recorded as a run, not five separate errors, because the fault is evidently one displacement carried down the column.

4. **⚠ Two departures from the Vulgate in the Latin itself** — not reference errors, and both kept:
   - `Libera nos a **manu** venantium` (432) where Vulg. Ps. xc. 3 reads *de **laqueo** venantium* (BCP "from the snare of the hunter"). *Manu* for *laqueo* is a substantive change of figure, from snare to hand.
   - `Respice in servos Tuos, et in opera **ipsorum**` (432) where Vulg. Ps. lxxxix. 16 reads *et in opera **Tua*** — and the plate further inserts `gratia ac` before *splendor*. The prayer asks God to look on the servants' works, not His own. Translated as printed.

5. **⚠ `a negotio perambulante in tenebris` is the Vulgate against the Prayer Book**, and CONVENTIONS §9 yields to it — a fourth recorded override, on the same principle as §28's *κατ᾽ ἐμοῦ*, §40's Ps. lxv. 2 and Part III §2's *calefactus*. Coverdale's "the pestilence that walketh in darkness" translates the Hebrew *deber*; the Vulgate's *negotium* renders the LXX's πρᾶγμα, "the thing," and that is what Andrewes prays. The English keeps the thing, not the pestilence. Same rule applied to `dæmone meridiano` at 432, where Coverdale has "the sickness that destroyeth in the noon-day."

6. **The morning office quarries Ps. xci. 5–6 and hands the rest to the evening office** — see §6's note 3. Printed 431 takes the night clause as a thanksgiving for deliverance already given (*Qui liberasti nos a terrore nocturno*) and printed 432 takes the day clause as a petition for the day beginning (*Serva nos a sagitta volante interdiu*). §6 reverses both. **This is the structural fact of the pair and must not be flattened by a later pass that "tidies" the duplicated verses.**

7. `Et opera manuum nostrarum dirige super nos; et opera manuum nostrarum dirige` (432) is Ps. xc. 17 given **without a reference of its own** and set at the deeper first-line indent, so the plate treats it as a new unit continuing the `xc. 16.` citation. Reproduced in that position.
