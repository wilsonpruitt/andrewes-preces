## Translator's flags

**Parity 9/9**, line-exact on every page.

1. **⚠ THE §2/§3 BOUNDARY IS CONFIRMED, and §2's flag 1 can be closed.** I marked that cut as ours and reversible, on the condition that §3's numbered mercy-kinds might turn out to answer §2's nine pleas. They do not. The schema makes its own argument (ten heads on the kinds of God's mercy), turns to prayer at head 10, gathers four apostolic warrants, and **ends on printed 424 with two-thirds of the page blank**; printed 425 opens GRATIARUM ACTIO under a fresh all-caps heading. The two files stay separate.

2. **The shape, and why the English must not tidy it.** Heads 1–9 are a **ladder of "not only … but"**: merciful in kind (1), gentle even in punishing (2), easily entreated (3), suffering with us (4), pardoning and pardoning lavishly (5), giving not pardon only but grace (6), rewarding even our poor service (7), merciful not by nature only but by practice (8), not by practice only but by promise (9) — and then (10) *all these things gathered up into one in Christ*. Every rung is a bare heading with its proofs stacked under it. **It is an outline and stays one**; the §24 *Divisio*, §36 *De Pœnitentia* and §41 *Monita* rulings all apply, and no line has been made into a sentence.

3. **⚠ Where the schema becomes prayer, it does so mid-page and without warning** — at printed 422, three lines after head 10's *Quem nominasse sat erat*, the apostrophe *Jesu, Fili David, miserere mei* begins and never stops. From *Et Tu, Domine, remitte* the English is in the vocative for the rest of the section. **The transition is Andrewes' and is not marked in the plate**; the English marks it no more than he does.

4. **The three titles.** *Misericordia Ejus* / *Misericordia Dei* / *Pater Misericordiarum* → **HIS MERCY · THE MERCY OF GOD · THE FATHER OF MERCIES**, small caps as set. The third is 2 Cor. i. 3, and *Naturalis Illi* under it — **"Natural unto Him"** — is the whole point of the heading: mercy is not something God does but something God is, which the next line says outright (*Misericordia ipsa dicitur Deus*, "Mercy itself is called God").

5. **⚠ Coverdale cannot carry the first three lines, and the flag says so rather than the page.** *Suavis · Dulcis · Melior vita* (416) are Ps. 109:21, 69:16 and 63:3 — and the BCP Psalter renders the first two by the **same** English word, "comfortable" ("because thy mercy is comfortable"; "for thy loving-kindness is comfortable"). Andrewes' Latin distinguishes them, so §9's crib yields here to the plain distinction and the English reads **Sweet / Pleasant**, with *Melior vita* keeping Coverdale's own "better than the life itself" compressed to **"Better than life."**

6. **Coverdale carries almost everything else, and four times it is decisive.** *He hath not dealt with us after our sins, nor rewarded us according to our wickednesses* (418) and *Yea, many a time turned He His wrath away, and would not suffer His whole displeasure to arise* (418) are Coverdale word for word for the Latin as Andrewes has it. *When I said, My foot hath slipt, Thy mercy, O Lord, shall hold me up* (419) is Coverdale for *Si dicebam motus est pes meus*. And **at 421 the crib settles a line the volume elsewhere misnumbers**: *Memor esto verbi Tui servo Tuo, in quo mihi spem dedisti* is Coverdale's *O think upon thy servant, as concerning thy word: wherein thou hast caused me to put my trust* — Ps. 119:49, correctly cited **here**, and the very verse §34 tagged `[Psal. cxix. 114.]` (printed 367). **Neither section is corrected; both stand.**

7. **⚠ `Quomodo percutiam te, Ephraim?` (418) is not the Vulgate, and the English follows Andrewes.** Hos. xi. 8 reads *quomodo dabo te* — "How shall I give thee up?", the AV's words and the famous ones. Andrewes writes *percutiam*, **"How shall I smite thee?"**, which is the question the head requires (gentleness *in the very chastising*). Followed, and flagged, and not conformed to the AV.

8. **`Placabilitas. Quia faciliter.` (418) → "Easiness to be entreated. Because readily."** *Placabilis* is the Prayer Book's word in the Comfortable Words tradition and the noun has no clean English; "placability" is a Latinism and "appeasableness" is unspeakable. The four-word head is left as the two fragments it is.

9. **`Compassio` (419) → "Fellow-suffering," not "Compassion."** English has worn *compassion* down to mere pity; the head's own gloss (*condolet calamitati nostræ*, He grieves *with* us) restores the etymology, and the following *compassus est eis* is rendered "had compassion upon them" where it is quoting the Psalter. **`viscera tenera`** (Luke i. 78) keeps the AV's **bowels** — the anatomy is the argument, and "tender mercy" would erase it two lines after a head built on suffering-with.

10. **`Venia non modo, sed et profusa` (420) → "Pardon, and not only pardon, but lavish pardon."** The head needs the word twice in English, which the Latin does not; the ellipsis in *non modo … sed et* will not carry in English without it. Disclosed here as the section's one added word.

11. **`Prævenientem, Subsequentem, Circumdantem` (421) → "Preventing, Following, Compassing about."** The Articles' own **prevenient and following** grace, as §39 ruled — but here they are participles governing *gratiam* from the line before, so the English keeps them participial. **"Preventing" is retained against the modern sense**: it is the word the English formularies use and the word §39 established, and *Ex omni parte*, "On every side," is what the third one adds.

12. **`opella` (421) → "poor service."** A diminutive of *opus* with a shrug in it — a scrap of work, hardly worth the name — and the parenthesis (*that grace enabling us*) takes even that away from us. "Little work" is exact and flat; "poor service" carries the diminutive's disparagement, which is what makes *remunerat abunde* land.

13. **⚠ The `Si non … Certe Deus nunquam` triad (422) is an argument from the lesser, and its force is in the order.** If Isaac would not change his word (having blessed the wrong son), if the Persian would not — **a heathen**, and the appositive is the sting — if profane Pilate would not (*What I have written I have written*), **surely God never will.** The English keeps *Ethnicus* as a bare appositive rather than smoothing it into "a heathen king," and keeps all three protases in the same shape so the conclusion falls on the fourth line.

14. **Two self-quotations kept audible.** *Fili David, qui remisit Shemeo blasphemanti* (422) is **§2's own plea** at printed 412 (*David pepercit Shemei maledicto*), now turned round: there David's clemency was urged on God, here it is urged on David's Son. And *quorum ego primus* (423, 1 Tim. i. 15) is the text behind **§2's `Peccatorum primis`** at printed 414 — the two "chief of sinners" passages are eleven pages apart and answer each other. Cross-referenced in both sections; **neither imported into the other.**

15. **⚠ The apostolic series numbers a witness it never announces (423).** *Thine Apostle* (Paul) · *the second Apostle* (Peter) · *the fourth* (John) — with **James** set under Peter's head and never called the third. The English reproduces the gap exactly: "the second," "the fourth," and no third. It reads as an error and may be one, but supplying "the third" before James would be to write the line Andrewes did not.

16. **`Non hæc frustra dicta sunt, / non possunt.` (424) — the section's last argument, and it turns on a gap.** *They cannot be* — the second line borrows *dicta sunt* across the comma, exactly as §38's *Sicut enim pœnitentia destruit vetera peccata, / Sic nova peccata pœnitentiam* borrows its verb. The English keeps the borrowing rather than completing it, because the clipped second line is the whole rhetorical point after four pages of *Nec frustra*.

17. **`Venite ad Me … et Ego reficiam vos` (423) → "and I will refresh you," not the AV's "give you rest."** *Reficere* is the Vulgate's verb and means to restore or feed again; Andrewes' whole preceding argument has been about what God *gives*, not what He grants leave from. The reference is printed `Matt. xi. 25` where the verse is xi. 28 — kept as printed (transcription note 5).
