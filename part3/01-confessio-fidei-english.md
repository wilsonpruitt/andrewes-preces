## Translator's flags

**Parity 8/8.** Eight printed pages, eight English pages; one movement break (the blank line before *Credo, Domine; supple defectus fidei meæ* at printed 402) and one before *Domine, adauge mihi fidem* (405), both marked by blank lines in the plate, neither a section break — the section's only hard boundary is the all-caps CONFESSIO PECCATORUM at printed 406, confirmed by reading one page past.

1. **⚠ THE FIND OF THE SESSION — Part III §1 is a SECOND RECENSION of §23, and of §38's closing lines.** Printed 402–405 run the same matter as §23 (printed 340–342, *In magnum pietatis sacramentum*): the same six agent-nouns of the economy of grace (*mundator · liberator · triumphator · præcursor · advocatus · instaurator fidei*, each in the same mystery), the same *Ut Ipse Christus formetur in nobis*, the same cold-in-prayer / hot-in-affection pair, the same *Ut propter Christum Tuum … accipiamus unctionem*, and the same *Domine, adauge mihi fidem, ut granum sinapis*. Printed 398 likewise runs §38's *Ponam peccata mea coram me* and *Domine, paratum est cor meum … metuo ne non sit … Utinam esset paratum! hei mihi, quod non est! … dispono me et præparo. Adjuva dispositionem…* — in a **different order** (here the *Ponam* couplet comes first). **The two recensions must not be conformed to each other.** Where the Latin is identical the English is worded identically (that is why the six titles read cleanser / deliverer / conqueror / Forerunner / Advocate / finisher here, exactly as at §23, and why *Cathedræ* is again "Throne"); where the Latin differs the English differs, and the difference is listed below. This is the §34/§37 discipline applied across a Part boundary.

2. **The recension is PLURAL where §23 is singular.** §23: *Cum frigid**us** sum in oratione … indige**o** … auribus **meis***. Here: *Cum frigid**i** sumus … indige**mus** … auribus **nostris***. The Harley text prays in the first person plural throughout this stretch (*nos operantem, ne nos pateremur, fiamus, simus vocati, simus participes*) and drops into the singular only at the two personal hinges — *Credo … adjuva incredulitatem **meam*** and *Domine, adauge **mihi** fidem*. That is a real difference of use, not a scribal slip, and the English keeps it exactly (we/our against §23's I/mine).

3. **Five substantive divergences from §23, all kept as printed, none reconciled:**
   - **`Cum fœcundi simus in affectu` (404) against §23's `Cum fervidus sum in affectu`.** *Fervidus* answers *frigidus* in a clean antithesis (cold in prayer / hot in affection) and is much the better reading; *fœcundi*, "teeming, fruitful," is what this plate sets and what the OCR confirms. Rendered "when we teem with affection" — deliberately not smoothed into "hot," which would silently import §23. **On the pre-print verification list.**
   - **`a Te / Patre unigenitum` (404) against §23's `a Te, Pater ungens`.** §23 has a vocative — *from Thee, O Father that anointest* — which is what the following *unctionem* wants; here the phrase is an accusative epithet of Christ, *only-begotten of Thee the Father*. Both construe; they are not the same sentence. Followed the plate.
   - **`Illuminatione justitiæ` (401) against §23's `illuminatione scientiæ`** — the enlightening of *righteousness*, not of *knowledge*. Kept.
   - **The Spirit's operations are five here, six at §23** — *Reprehensione, Doctrina, Tolerantia, Subventione, Contestatione*; §23 adds *Consolatione*. Nothing supplied. The five English words are §23's five, unchanged.
   - **The faith-catalogue (405) differs at three points** from §23's: it adds *destruentem Legem* and *viventem, mundam*, drops *vincentem mundum*, reads *subministrator**em*** for *subministratricem*, and carries **no scripture references at all** where §23 tags every line. §23's refs are not imported.

4. **⚠ `subministratorem virtutis` (405) is a grammatical fault kept as printed** — a masculine accusative in a column of feminines agreeing with *fidem* (*operantem, cooperantem, viventem, mundam, sanctissimam*); §23 has the correct *subministratricem*. Confirmed in the plate and in the OCR, so it is not a scan artifact. English cannot show it (our nouns have no gender) and says so here instead.

5. **⚠ `Ponam peccata meam coram me` (398) — a wrong sort, kept.** See transcription note 2. The English gives the sense the sentence must have, *my sins*, and the flag carries the defect; **the argument of the couplet is the repeated preposition** (§38's ruling), so *before me* / *before Thee* stand in the same words here as there.

6. **`Oratio cum Deo collocutio est` (398) → "Prayer is a speaking together with God."** The commonplace is Gregory of Nyssa's ὁμιλία μετὰ Θεοῦ / Clement's ὁμιλία πρὸς τὸν Θεόν. "Conversation with God" is the usual English and is here avoided: *conversatio* is a live word elsewhere in this very section (*sanctitatem conversationis*, 401, = manner of life), and using it in both places would make two different things look like one.

7. **`Magnum pietatis sacramentum … manifestum in carne Deum, / Hominem Deum` (399).** 1 Tim. iii. 16 in the AV's words ("great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifest in the flesh") stitched to the Nicene *qui propter nos homines et propter nostram salutem*, which the English gives in the Creed's own English ("who for us men and for our salvation"). **`Hominem Deum` is rendered "Man that is God," not "the God-man"** — the schoolmen's compound would flatten a phrase that is deliberately two nouns in apposition, and the Latin puts *Man* first.

8. **`Christum Spiritus` (400) → "The Christ of the Spirit."** The triad is *Son of the Father · Christ of the Spirit · our Lord*: each Person named by his relation, the middle one by the anointing (χριστός = anointed) which the Spirit gives. Not emended to *Christum Spiritu*, and not glossed on the page.

9. **The article-numbers.** The inner series `1. 2. 3.` (under *In God*) and `1.`–`12.` (under *In Jesus Christ*) are the articles of the Creed expanded; the outer `3.` and `4.` at printed 401 are the third and fourth articles of the Creed itself, whose 1 and 2 the plate never numbers. English reproduces both series exactly as set, with the indentation doing the work of distinguishing them (see transcription note 4). **A later pass must not supply the missing `1.` and `2.`**

10. **`quæ nos oportuit` / `quo nos oportuit` (400) — the same figure twice, and it is the argument of the whole creed.** He suffered *what we must have suffered, that we should not suffer it*; he descended *whither we must have gone, that we should never go down thither*. The English holds the two lines to the same shape, because the parallel is Andrewes'.

11. **`ubi nobis nihil juris` (400) → "where we had no right at all."** A legal genitive (*nihil juris*, no title, no claim) continuing the court-language of §7, §32 and §41: heaven is a place we have no title to, and he goes to prepare it. "Where we have no business" or "no place" would lose the law.

12. **The turned lines at 403–404 are not reproduced.** English cannot break a word at a line-end without looking like an error (§43's ruling), so *infer-/num*, *novita-/tem*, *quæ-/rentes*, *dijudi-/cantes* are rendered whole and the line-keying is kept by re-ordering within the two lines (e.g. *By frequent meditation into hell / going down*). The one place this crosses a page-turn is *dijudi-/cantes* (403→404): the English puts "judging ourselves" at the foot of 403 and opens 404 with "that with the world / we be not condemned," which keeps both page-line counts intact.

13. **`Meminisse / Cathedræ, / Apparitionis, / Intercessionis / Tuæ.` (404)** — a trailing genitive governing all three nouns, as at §1's *{ … } Tuæ*. English keeps the four lines and lets the last carry the possessive ("that are Thine") rather than repeating "Thy" three times and leaving a dangling fourth line. *Cathedra* = the seat at the right hand, and is "Throne" here because it is "Throne" at §23.

14. **`sapientes … viciniora saluti` (403).** *Sapientes* is Col. iii. 2's *quæ sursum sunt sapite* — the AV's "set your affection on things above," here a participle, so "being wise"; *viciniora saluti*, "nearer to salvation," is Rom. xiii. 11. Both left in their own words rather than assimilated to the AV's phrasing, since Andrewes has recast the grammar.

15. **`voto et voluntate` (405) → "by vow and by will."** *Votum* in this pairing is the classic *votum* of desire/intention (to be of the Church *in voto* where one cannot be *in re*); "by desire and will" would be the smoother English and would lose the technical word. Kept "vow," flagged here.

## Collation findings — Brightman 1903 + recension parallels (2026-08-04)

> Applied to the ENGLISH layer only. **No transcript is altered**; the 1853 plate stands as printed. Method and full ledger: `apparatus/BRIGHTMAN-collation.md`.

- **✅ THE 398 EPIGRAPH LEAF IS SOURCED AT LAST** — it carried no sources at all.
  - `ORATIONIS fundamentum et basis est fides. *Theophylact.*` → **Theophylact, *in S. Lucam* xviii** (ed. i. 433): `πάσης προσευχῆς βάθρον καὶ κρηπὶς ἡ πίστις`. ⚠ ***βάθρον καὶ κρηπίς* is exactly *fundamentum et basis*** — the Latin is a close rendering of that sentence, not a loose echo.
  - `Sursum corda.` → first in the **Hippolytean canons 3** (2nd–3rd cent.) and **Cyprian, *de Orat. dom.* 31**.
  - Brightman also notes a wordplay lost in this neighbourhood, *fundit / fundatur*, closest parallel **Augustine, *serm.* CXV 1**: *fides fundit orationem, fusa oratio fidei impetrat firmitatem*.
- **✅ TWO OF THE FIVE DIVERGENCES FROM §23 INDEPENDENTLY CONFIRMED.** (1) **`mundam`** — Brightman's note reads `P. 189. 6. **H reads mundam 'pure'; O, vincentem mundum.**` H = Harleian, O = Oxford 1675: so this is a **manuscript variant, not a slip**, and it corroborates the structural claim that **Part III is the Harley recension**, from a witness who held the Harleian MS. (2) **Five Spirit-operations, no *Consolatione*** — Brightman's Part III text likewise gives *reproof, teaching, bearing with, help, witnessing with*. Both kept exactly as they stand; neither recension conformed to the other.
- **✅ This section repaired §23.** `In Spiritum Sanctum … Extrinsecus et invisibiliter, sed / Efficaciter et manifeste in / Nos operantem` supplies what the plate lost at the 339/340 join — see `part2/23-…-english.md`.
