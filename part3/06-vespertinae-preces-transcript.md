## Transcriber's notes

1. **Parity: 4 printed pages / 4 pages, line-exact** — 26 lines on 433 under the heading, 31 on 434, 30 on 435, 11 on 436. All four counts taken from a per-line ink-band measurement of the images, not by eye.

2. **⚠ THE VOLUME ENDS HERE.** Printed 436 carries eleven lines and then two-thirds of a blank page; PDF 482 opens `VARIÆ LECTIONES ET ADDENDA QUÆDAM, ex apographo Samuelis Wright`. Read one page past, as the standing lesson requires: **there is nothing further.** The primary text of the *Preces Privatæ* is printed 1–263 · 267–395 · 398–436, and it is now complete in both layers.

3. **⚠ THE FIND — the two offices divide Psalm xci. 5–6 between them and then exchange the halves.** This is the structural fact of the §5/§6 pair and it is exact:

   | | the day clause (*sagitta volante interdiu*) | the night clause (*terrore nocturno / negotio perambulante*) |
   |---|---|---|
   | **§5, morning** | 432, **petition** — *Serva nos* | 431, **thanksgiving** — *Qui liberasti nos* |
   | **§6, evening** | 433, **thanksgiving** — *Qui liberasti nos* | 434, **petition** — *Libera nos* |

   Each office gives thanks for the danger it has come through and prays against the danger it is going into, and the verse supplies both halves. **The repetitions are the design, not redundancy** — a later pass that notices the same lines twice and "tidies" them destroys the chiasm. Note also that on 434 the night clause is set **without any reference at all**, three pages after 431 cited it: the plate assumes the reader has the morning office in hand.

4. **⚠ `Pro.` (434) is a NEW abbreviation for Proverbs**, against §4's `*Prov.*` at printed 425. Part III has now used three abbreviations for Ecclesiasticus (`Eccl.` 398, `Ecclus.` in Part II, `Syr.` 417) and two for Proverbs. **Do not normalise any of them.**

5. **⚠ Two editorial supplements stand INSIDE the text on printed 434** — `suum [malum] habuit` and `suum merebi[tur.]` — the first supplying a noun, the second a verb-ending. Everywhere else in the volume the square brackets have belonged to the reference column. These are reproduced in the transcript; **the English can carry the first and cannot carry the second**, and says so.

6. **⚠ Wrong references, kept as printed:**
   - **`cxxxiv. 3.`** (433) for Ps. cxxxiv. **2** — *In noctibus extollite manus vestras in sanctuario* is Vulg. Ps. cxxxiii. 2 / AV and BCP cxxxiv. 2. Verse 3 is *Benedicat te Dominus ex Sion*. Every witness makes it 2.
   - **`Is. xxxviii. 14.`** (433) for Isa. xxxviii. **12–13** — *præcisa est velut a texente vita mea* is verse 12 and *de mane usque ad vesperam finies me* is verse 13. Verse 14 is *sicut pullus hirundinis sic clamabo*.
   - **`Job. xxxv.`** (433) is given **without a verse number**, alone in this section; the text is Job xxxv. 10, *qui dedit carmina in nocte*.

7. **⚠ Two references are RIGHT, and right on the Prayer Book's numbering — which is evidence for CONVENTIONS §9.** `xlii. 10.` (433) and `lxv. 8.` (433) are wrong on the Vulgate's reckoning and wrong on the AV's, and exactly right on Coverdale's: BCP Ps. xlii. 10 is *The Lord hath granted his loving-kindness in the day-time*, and BCP Ps. lxv. 8 is *thou that makest the outgoings of the morning and evening to praise thee*. **Andrewes — or his compositor — was counting verses out of the Prayer Book Psalter here.** That does not make the BCP his source (the Latin is still Vulgate), but it is the first hard evidence in the volume that the Prayer Book Psalter was the book physically to hand, and it strengthens §9's Coverdale-first crib rule rather than overriding it. Set this beside the section's four bad references: where the plate is right it is right *by the Prayer Book*.

8. **⚠ Isaiah xxxviii — Hezekiah's psalm — for the THIRD time in the volume**: §37 at printed 372, Part III §2 at printed 409 (as the misprinted `Is. xxxiii. 15.`), and now *quasi textor* at printed 433. Three appearances in three different Parts is a text Andrewes returned to, not a coincidence. Cross-referenced in all three files; **do not import one rendering into another** — the recension rule holds here as everywhere.

9. **⚠ Printed 436's closing funnel is sub-grid.** Measured left edges, in order: `493, 494, 551, 522, 552, 580, 546, 563, 605, 618, 645`. The last five lines narrow by uneven steps — the text is being drawn to a point on the page, and the effect is the last thing the reader sees in the book. **`transcript2tex.py` must not round these onto the 28.5-px ladder**; the half-level indents in the transcript above are the closest a text file can come, and the measured numbers above are the authority.

10. **⚠ `Qui das dilectis Tuis somnum suavitatis` (434) is the only line in the section set LEFT of the free verse's home margin** (L−1, x≈380). It is the longest line on the page and the plate has hung it out to fit; it is not a new indent level in the sense the others are. Reproduced in position, flagged as probably compositorial rather than authorial.

11. **The 435 ladder widens instead of narrowing** at *Quid recte, quid rectius / Quomodo Tibi magis acceptus / Quomodo hominibus magis gratus* — L3, L1, L0, stepping out to the left as the question opens from God to men. Everywhere else in the volume the cascades close inward. Kept exactly; it is the page's one rhetorical gesture and it is legible only in the indentation.
