# *Notæ Marginales ex eodem MS.* — raw transcript

Source: 1853 Parker ed., **PDF 492–493** (apparatus pages **[xi]–xii**), images `raw/end/p-NNN.jpg`.

⚠ **THE BRIEF'S PAGE MAP WAS TWO LEAVES LONG.** It gave the *Notæ Marginales* as PDF 492–495. They are **PDF 492–493 only**: PDF 493 closes with **`FINIS.`** and the printer's imprint `EXCUDEBAT R. CLAY, LOND.`, PDF 494 and 495 are blank (146 and 31 dark pixels respectively, measured), and PDF 496 is the library/Google end matter. **The whole apparatus is twelve pages, PDF 482–493.** Same class of error as the map's "~397" for Part II and "~430" for Part III — an estimated range that was never walked to its end. Corrected 2026-08-03.

**⚠ WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS, AND WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN ITS LENGTH SUGGESTS.** These are **the manuscript's own marginal notes** — Andrewes (or his copyist) recording, prayer by prayer, **which ancient liturgy the prayer was taken from**. It is the author's own source apparatus. It is the reason the *Preces* can be called a cento rather than a composition, and it is direct evidence for CONVENTIONS §6's account of what the book is.

**⚠ THE BRACKET RULE, AND IT IS LOAD-BEARING.** The head-note reads: *`Quæ nosmetipsi addidimus, uncinis sunt inclusa.`* — **what is in square brackets is the 1853 editor's, what is unbracketed is the MANUSCRIPT's.** So `S. Chrysost. Liturg.` is Andrewes' note; `[Goar, Rit. Græc. p. 74. Paris. 1647.]` is the editor giving chapter and verse in Goar's *Euchologion*. **Never merge the two.** Any future presentation of these notes must keep the brackets or it will attribute a 1647 Paris edition to a man who died in 1626.

Transcription discipline as in `variae-lectiones-transcript.md`: verbatim, Greek by vision only, brackets exactly as printed.

## Transcriber's notes

1. **Twenty-four notes in all**, covering printed pp. 16–246 — i.e. **entirely within Part I**, and entirely within the *usque ad pag. 250* range of the apograph. There is **no source-note anywhere in Parts II or III**, which is consistent with the apograph not extending there and is not evidence that those Parts are un-sourced.

2. **The liturgies named are three, and only three:**
   - **S. Chrysostom** — pp. 16, 18, 32, 54, 56, 118, 122, 138 (×2), 140, 238, 240
   - **S. James** — pp. 66, 116, 118, 124, 126, 140, 162
   - **S. Basil** — pp. 118, 240, 242, 244 (×2), 246

   ⚠ **The distribution is not random and should not be flattened into "the Eastern liturgies."** Chrysostom carries the morning and intercessory matter; **Basil is concentrated almost entirely in the eucharistic sequence at pp. 238–246** and appears nowhere before p. 118; James is spread thin across both. Three notes name **two** liturgies for one passage (pp. 118. 7; 140. 7; 240. 4) — Andrewes knew a phrase stood in more than one rite and said so.

3. ⚠ **The gap from p. 140 to p. 162, and again from p. 162 to p. 238, is in the manuscript, not in our transcription.** The two leaves are consecutive (PDF 492, 493) and nothing is missing between them. Do not read the gap as loss.

4. ⚠ **`p. 246. lin. 19. Ἥνωται`** carries the only note in which the editor supplies a **Greek rubric** rather than a page reference — `[Εὐχὴ ἐν τῷ συστεῖλαι τὰ ἅγια.]`, "the prayer at the veiling of the holy things." Bracketed, therefore the editor's, therefore **not evidence that Andrewes' MS named the rubric.**

5. **Editions cited by the 1853 editor** (all bracketed, all his): Goar, *Euchologion sive Rituale Græcorum*, Paris 1647; *Auctarium Bibliothecæ Patrum*, tom. ii, Paris 1624 (for St James). Both post-date Andrewes. **A pre-print check should confirm the Goar page numbers**, which are the only externally verifiable claims in this document — but that is a courtesy to the reader, not a correction to Andrewes.
