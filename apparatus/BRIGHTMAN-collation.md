# Brightman 1903 as a collation witness — pilot, method, and the anchor table

**Pilot run 2026-08-04. Five probes, four hits, one crux solved. Nothing applied.**

## 1. What Brightman is, and is not, as a witness

**He is NOT a witness to the Greek or Latin text.** He prints none of it — *"no attempt has been made to mark the distinction between the Greek and the Latin of the original"* — confirmed mechanically: **zero Greek runs and zero Hebrew runs in 755 KB of OCR across all 472 pages.** He cannot correct a transcription.

**He IS a strong witness to four things we actually need:**

1. **Construal.** An independent English of the same book. Where his reading and ours diverge, one of us has misread.
2. **Scripture references.** Marginal refs throughout, with `heb.` / `sept.` / `vulg.` added where the original departs from the English Bible. ⚠ This bears directly on our worst problem — the volume's dense bad-reference stretches, and the three places where it proves its own misprints.
3. **Non-scriptural sources.** He identifies them, and his index names the **edition Andrewes used**. This is what the pre-print attribution questions need.
4. **The manuscripts.** He collates **O** (Oxford 1675) against **H** (Harleian), **L** (Laudian), **W/Wy**, and **S** (Stokes). Our Part III is the Harley recension, so **H-vs-O variants in his notes speak directly to Part III against Part II.**

## 2. The pilot — four findings

### ✅ 2.1 §35 Bradwardine — confirmed, and sourced exactly
Our §35 (printed 370) identified Bradwardine. Brightman's note: *"P. 192. Thomas Bradwardine was archbishop of Canterbury for five weeks in 1349. **Gain:** the work, the exact corollary, and the edition Andrewes had.

### ✅ 2.2 §39's Fulgentius attribution — CORROBORATED; our Augustine doubt answered
Our notes flagged the 1853's `ex Fulgentii l. i. ad Mon.` as **UNVERIFIED and "looks like Augustine"** (*De Doctrina Christiana* IV), and kept it as printed. Brightman, working independently from the sources, **also gives Fulgentius** — index: **`Fulgentius of Ruspe, S. (Migne Patr. lat. lxv.), 257`**, and a note at his p. 257. 9 identifying him as bishop of Ruspe, 508–533. His introduction paraphrases the passage as ours reads it — *"it is rather by the piety of his prayers for himself and his flock than by fluency of speech…"*
**Gain:** a pre-print item moves from *doubted* to *independently corroborated*. (PL 65 is Fulgentius's volume and contains *Ad Monimum*.) The transcript is unchanged either way; what changes is the note.

### ✅✅ 2.3 Part III §1's `mundam` — confirmed as a HARLEIAN-vs-OXFORD variant
Our Part III §1 finding recorded, among five substantive divergences from §23, that the faith-catalogue **adds `mundam` and drops `vincentem mundum`**. Brightman's note:

> **`P. 189. 6. H reads mundam 'pure'; O, vincentem mundum.`**

**This is an independent confirmation of one of our own five divergences — and it names the mechanism.** H = Harleian, O = Oxford 1675. ⚠ **It corroborates the structural claim that Part III is the Harley recension**, from a witness who had the Harleian manuscript itself.

### ✅✅ 2.4 THE `Isaac` CRUX AT PRINTED 425 IS SOLVED — the word is **Mosis**
Our §4 note (printed 425): *"`Cantemus Domino Isaac et Israelitarum` (Exod. xv. 2) — Isaac has no part in the song at the Red Sea and no construal makes him fit. Translated straight, difficulty left visible, on the pre-print list."*

Brightman's text, in the same Benedictus-catena and in the same position:

> *"Blessed be the Lord **(of Jethro)** Ex. xviii 10"*
> *"I will sing unto the Lord **(of Moses and the Israelites)** Ex. xv 1"*

**The catena tags each text with whose blessing it is, and the reading is `Mosis et Israelitarum`** — Exod. xv. 1, *"Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the LORD."* ⚠ **`Isaac` is an error in the printed line**, and the reference is off by one as well (**xv. 1**, not xv. 2).

**Kept as printed in the transcript, per standing discipline.** What changes is that the English's note can now say what the reading must be instead of leaving the difficulty merely visible. **The crux comes off the pre-print list.**

## 3. Method — why a linear pass is the wrong shape

**Brightman rearranged the book on principle** (preface §3), and **his notes and index are keyed to his own pagination**. So there is no mechanical crosswalk from his page to our printed page. The anchors below make the point:

| Brightman p. | our printed p. | section |
|---|---|---|
| 106 | 386 | Part II §41 (*Monita* / evening examination) |
| 189 | ~403 | **Part III §1** (*Confessio Fidei*) |
| 192 | 370 | Part II §35 (Bradwardine) |
| 257 | 380 | Part II §39 (*Ante Concionem*, Fulgentius) |

His 189 < 192 < 257 maps to our 403 > 370 < 380. **The order is genuinely scrambled; a page-by-page collation would mean building a 472-page crosswalk first.**

**The right shape is a QUESTION-DRIVEN pass.** Every hit above came from grepping a proper name or a distinctive phrase — cheap, and aimed at items that already matter. Run our existing lists through him:

1. the **pre-print verification list**
2. the running list of **wrong and doubtful references** (Part III §2 alone has 4 demonstrably wrong + 6 doubtful — the densest in the volume)
3. the **attributions** (§39 done; §35 done)
4. the **cruces** (425 done; `p. 207`; `Tuâ.` at 421; the unnumbered witness at 423)
5. the **five plate defects** in the watch list — ⚠ especially **printed 370/371, where a heading and an opening are MISSING from the 1853**. Brightman had the manuscripts; he may simply have the missing text.
6. **class A's H/L variants** — his notes are the second opinion on the Wright apograph's readings, from different manuscripts.

**Accumulate the anchor table as a by-product.** Each lookup fixes one (his page ↔ our printed page) pair at zero marginal cost. If enough structure emerges, a fuller pass becomes cheap later. If it never does, we lost nothing.

⚠ **Two standing limits:**
- **Anything depending on italics — i.e. ALL of the Hebrew — needs his page images, not the OCR.** This folds into the 600-dpi digitization job that already gates both books.
- Brightman is a **witness, not an authority**. He is emending from manuscripts we have not seen, and he says himself that where he adds words to complete a sense he brackets them. **The transcripts still record the 1853 plate; nothing here writes back to them.**

## 3b. ✅ THE RETRY PASS — 2026-08-09. **Part III §2's reference list is now fully adjudicated: 8 of 8.**

The 2026-08-04 pass closed on two no-hits and left six references "doubtful and unadjudicated." **All eight are now settled or improved**, from a single method change.

### ⚠ THE METHOD FINDING — search him by OUR ENGLISH, not by the Latin
Both 2026-08-04 no-hits were failures of the *query*, not of the witness. Brightman prints no Latin, so a Latin phrase can never match him; and his English is his own, so it will not match ours word for word either. **What does match is the SCRIPTURE WORDING**, because both of us are quoting the same English Bible — grep the AV/BCP phrase the line is built on (*"carest thou not that we perish"*, *"known in the dark"*, *"for mine own sake"*) and he surfaces immediately, margin and all. Once one line of a section is located, **his whole rendering of that section is contiguous** and every neighbouring reference comes free. Our printed 409–416 is his pp. 146–149, a dense line-by-line parallel. **This is much cheaper than the 2026-08-04 pass and should be the default shape for any further lookup.**

### ⚠⚠ The one real correction: we were wrong at printed 409
`*Psal.* xl. 9.` heads *Defecerunt in vanitate dies mei, et anni in anxietate cordis*, and our note proposed **Ps. xxxi. 10** as "closer." It is **Ps. lxxviii. 33** — Brightman cites it twice in unrelated parts of his book, and the Latin proves it without him: *Defecerunt in vanitate dies* is **verbatim** Vulgate Ps. lxxvii. 33, and *anni in anxietate* renders that verse's own second half. Ps. xxxi. 10 supplies neither half. ⚠ **A conjecture that reads plausibly is not a collation; this one survived two passes before a witness was actually asked.**

### ⚠ The one that resisted: `*Job.* xxv. 3.` at printed 414
Brightman carries our line verbatim but tags its first clause to **Job xiv (vulg.)**, not Job xxv — and marks it `vulg.` precisely because the Latin departs from the AV there. That is what actually explains *nec infans unius diei*: the LXX of Job xiv. 4–5 has "though his life be but a day," which Job xxv. 4 does not say. So the cento is probably **Job xiv. 4–5 (vulg.) + xxv. 5 + iv. 18** — three sources where our note claimed two. **The margin's figures are OCR-noisy and were not decoded further; this needs his page image and goes on the pre-print list** beside printed 386's Hebrew, which is blocked on the same thing.

### A by-product: a third witness to the BCP Psalter
Brightman numbers *"He considered that we are but flesh… a wind that passeth away"* as **Ps. lxxviii. 40** — in two margins and in his own printed psalm text — where the AV has **39**. That is the **Prayer Book Psalter's numbering**, one ahead through this psalm. It joins printed 433's `xlii. 10` and `lxv. 8` as evidence that the BCP was the book physically to hand, and it strengthens CONVENTIONS §9's Coverdale-first rule. It does **not** unsettle our own note at 411, which reads the plate's `lxviii. 39` on AV numbering with a dropped `x`.

⚠ **And a plate pattern worth recording:** Part III §2 misnumbers **Psalm lxxviii twice in seven pages** — at 409 (`xl. 9`) and 411 (`lxviii. 39`). Two corruptions of one psalm reads as a compositor's hand rather than an author's numbering, which is a small independent support for the standing "kept as printed, flagged, never mended" discipline.
