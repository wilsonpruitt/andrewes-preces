# Class A — the ledger

Every class-A reading from `variae-lectiones-transcript.md` (apparatus pages [i]–vi, printed pp. 6–242), resolved against our own transcripts.

## ⚠ The rule this ledger exists to enforce

**NOTHING HERE HAS BEEN APPLIED, AND NOTHING HERE MAY BE APPLIED SILENTLY.**

Our transcripts record **the 1853 plate**. Class A records **the manuscript behind it**. They are two witnesses and the edition needs both. The 1853 says class A is *pro veris habenda* — "to be held as the true ones" — and it may well be right, but "the editor of our base text thinks his own text is wrong here" is an argument to be made in an apparatus, not a licence to overwrite a transcript. **Any change to a transcript is a separate, argued commit, and it is Wilson's decision, not a session's.**

Status values: `applied` is never used and should stay never used. Everything is `recorded` (located in our files, difference confirmed), `recorded — not locatable` (the apparatus's line number does not resolve), or `structural` (not a word-change but a layout or content claim).

## 2. ⚠⚠ THE HEADLINE: THE 1853 DROPPED HEBREW THAT STOOD IN ANDREWES' TEXT

**This overturns a standing correction in our own map, and the correction has now been reversed in `STRUCTURE.md`.**

`STRUCTURE.md` carried, and the project memory repeated, the line: *"CORRECTION: NO Hebrew in the main text at 198/200/212 (confirmed by eye) — the Hebrew is Variae Lectiones §A apparatus only."*

**Half right, and the wrong half is the important half.** It is true that no Hebrew is printed at those places in the 1853 — our eyes were not deceived. But class A is *"variæ lectiones quæ in textu ipso occurrunt"* — **readings that occur in the text itself**, held to be the true ones. So what the note actually records is that **the Hebrew stands in the manuscript's text and the 1853 edition left it out.**

The Hebrew class A restores, in full:

| printed | what the MS has | gloss |
|---|---|---|
| 38. 3 | `אדאג` in the margin opposite | Heb. for `μεριμνῶ` — "I am anxious" |
| 76. 4 | *adde* `יצר טוב` after `Φαντασίαν καλὴν` | **the rabbinic *yetzer ha-tov***, the good inclination |
| 86 (ult., penult.), 88 | *Voces Hebrææ Græcis respondentes in altero στοίχῳ subjiciuntur* | a Hebrew column set under the Greek |
| 92. 10, 13, 16 | `תהום` · `תוהו` · `בוהו` before `αἱ ἄβυσσοι` · `γῆ` · `τὰ χλοερὰ` | **Genesis i. 2** — the deep, and *tohu wa-bohu* |
| 96. 25–31 | a full Greek–Hebrew bilingual column (below) | the vocabulary of sin |
| 152. 8 | `ואתה תעל משחת חיי` **in place of the Greek** | **Jonah ii. 7**, under the MS's own heading `Τοῦ Ἰωνᾶ` |
| 192. 17, 18 | *Hebraice leguntur*; the Greek is at the foot of the page | the plate has inverted the MS's priority |
| 198 (before line 1) | `אל תשחית` / `הצילני` ‖ `תעל משחת` / `חיי` | glossed by the 1853 itself: *ne perdas / libera me* ‖ *subleva de corruptione / vitam meam* |
| 200 (after line 8) | `אל תשחית` / `הצילני` | |
| 202. 15 | `הצילני` **in place of** `ῥῦσαί με` | |
| 210 (before last line) | *eadem Hebraice* — Ps. lxxxvi. 11 | |
| 212. 1 | `יראתי כסלתי` after `ὁ φόβος μου` | |

**The bilingual column at printed 96. 25–31** — seven Greek names for sin, each with its Hebrew:

`φαντασίαν` יצר · `σφάλμα` שגגה · `ἐνοχὴν πταῖσμα` אשם · `ἁμαρτίαν` חטאה · `παράβασιν` פשע · `ἀδικίαν` עון · `βδέλυγμα` תועבה

That is the standard Hebrew vocabulary of sin — *yetzer, shegagah, asham, chata'ah, pesha, avon, to'evah* — set out as a lexicon. **It is not in the printed book at all.**

⚠ **Consequences, all of them for Wilson, none of them for a session:**
1. The edition's claim to carry four scripts is **understated, not overstated**. Andrewes' Hebrew was systematic where the 1853 shows it as occasional.
2. The `יצר` at 96. 25 and the `יצר טוב` at 76. 4 mean Andrewes was **reading his own *φαντασία* through the rabbinic doctrine of the two inclinations.** That is a substantive theological finding about the *Preces*, not a textual footnote.
3. **Whether to restore any of it is a decision about what edition this is** — a faithful reprint of the 1853, or a critical text of Andrewes. The project has never had to answer that question before, because until now the two coincided.
4. ⚠ **All Hebrew here is read at 200 dpi and the look-alike sorts (ד/ר, ב/כ, ה/ח) are not always separable.** `אדאג` (38. 3) is the least certain — the reading is secure on sense (it must render *μεριμνῶ*) but the third sort is not crisp. **Every Hebrew form above needs re-reading at 600 dpi before it is printed anywhere.**

## 3. ⚠ The 1853 prints its own conjectures, and the manuscript overrules them

Three places where class A is not correcting a misreading but **withdrawing an editorial invention**:

- **100. 10** — the plate has `[στρατιώτας]`, marked *ex conjectura nostra*; the MS reads `βιαστάς`. Soldiers for violent men.
- **104. 6** — the plate has `[οὐκ εἰς τέλος]`, *ex conjectura nostra*; the MS reads `οὐκ ἀεί`.
- **242. 6** — *dele uncinas quibus has voces e conjectura nostra additas notavimus*, and read `τῆς` before `διανοίας`.
- **202. 3 &c., 9, 13** — ⚠ the strongest of the set: *`Notæ numerales non leguntur; ex Vers. Angl. a nobis desumpta sunt.`* **The numerals in our transcript of printed 202 are not in the manuscript at all** — the 1853 editor took them from an English version. Anything our files infer from that numbering is inference from an editor's scaffolding.
- **204. 17–23** — and the MS's own numbers, where it has them, run **`2, 1, 4, 3, 5, 6, 7`**, not in sequence.

**To the project's credit, the discipline already in force handles all of this correctly**: the transcripts record brackets as printed and never silently mend. But `[στρατιώτας]` and `[οὐκ εἰς τέλος]` were transcribed as the 1853's supplied readings, and are now known to be **wrong** supplied readings. That is worth a line in the introduction.

## 4. ⚠ Class A and class C collide — and the collision is NOT resolved here

At eight lines a class-A reading and a Drake marginal address the same words:

| printed | class A (the MS's text) | class C (Drake, *in ora libri*) |
|---|---|---|
| 34. 27 | `τὰ ἔργα` → `τὸ πλάσμα` | `τὰ ἔργα] τὸ πλάσμα (τὰ ἔργα superscriptum est)` |
| 34. 28 | `ὁμοίωμα` + point | `ὁμοίωμα] ὁμοίωσιν` |
| 50. 18 | `προσδοκίας` → `ἐλπίδος` | `προσδοκίας.` |
| 52. ult. | *dele* `ἃ μέλλει ἀποθανεῖν` | `μέλλει ἀποθανεῖν.` |
| 104. 3 | *dele* `οὐχ ἑκὼν` | `οὐχ ἑκὼν.` |
| 162. 16 | `ὑπερέχουσα` → `ὑπερβάλλουσα` | `ὑπερέχουσα in marg. legitur` |
| 170. 16 | `ἀνομίας` → `ἁμαρτίας` | `ἁμαρτίας] ἀνομίας` |
| 190. 13 | `ἀνόμων` → `ἀνομιῶν` | `ἀνόμων] in marg. tantum` |
| 224. 8 | `ἑσπερινὴ` → `δειλινή` | `δειλινή] ἑσπερινή` |

**The tempting theory** — that the printed 1853 text has in places adopted **Drake's marginal reading** rather than the manuscript's, so that our transcript at those lines is a later hand, not Andrewes — is **attractive, partly supported, and not established.** It cannot be settled from the printed apparatus alone, because **the direction of class C's `]` is ambiguous**: Drake wrote in the margin *of the apograph*, so his lemma should be the manuscript's reading, yet at 34. 27 and 224. 8 the lemma is what the *printed* text has, which points the other way.

⚠ **Do not build an argument on these collisions until the manuscript itself has been seen.** They are recorded here as a question, in the same spirit in which the volume's cruces are left as cruces. **The brief's standing instruction — "class C is evidence about Drake, not about Andrewes; never apply it to the text" — is unaffected and still governs.**

Worth noting for whoever does see the MS: **Drake was not a mere normaliser.** He collates against the **Wechel** edition (pp. 4. 20; 170. 28), cites **Aquila, Theodotion and Symmachus** at p. 134. 23–24, and traces the Prayer of Manasses to the *Apostolic Constitutions* II. 22 *apud Zonaram* (Paris, 1618). The brief's "later hand with a normalising agenda" is accurate about his aim and understates his competence.

## 5. Structural claims — no words change, but the page does

- **10. 3 – 14. 11** — *indicem quendam precum constituere videntur*: this stretch is **an index of prayers**, marked with the MS's dot-symbols, pointing to pp. 186, 190, 198, 208, 214, 220, and *fortasse* 234 or 236. **Our transcripts render it as text.** If it is an index it is apparatus, and Prototype B would set it differently.
- **32. 27** — after `ἐποίησα` **a ruled line**, *quasi spatium ad enumeranda peccata reservatum esset* — a blank left for the reader to enumerate his own sins. **A deliberate silence in the book, and our transcript does not show it.**
- **30. 19, 22** — `α΄`, `β΄` in the margin: the MS numbers parts with **Greek letters**, per the preface's principle iii. Our files use arabic throughout.
- **62. 21–22 · 70. 12 · 74. 6–20 / 76. 5 · 82. 27 · 116. 1–4 · 156. 12 · 158. 4 · 174. 5–6 · 182. 11–15 · 186. 11–12 · 204. 1–2** — column and line re-arrangements; several say two facing sets are meant to answer one another *invicem*, which bears directly on the mirror's page-fit.
- **82. 27 – 84. 7 · 186. 14–16, 21 sqq. · 188. 1–3 · 190. 26** — ⚠ ***manu posteriori addita sunt***: the intercessions naming `τῶν Παναγίων Βερκίγγ` and `Οὐϊντονίας` (**Winchester** — Andrewes' own see) are **a later hand in the manuscript.** Whether they are Andrewes adding to his own book or someone else adding to it is exactly the sort of question the introduction should raise and not answer.
- **70. 19 · 72. 5, 13, 17 · 94 · 96. 9, 11 · 106–110 · 130–134 · 150–152 · 164. ult. · 170. 2 · 218. 6, 12, 13** — words to be set *litteris grandioribus* / *majusculis*: the MS's own display hierarchy (`Τοῦ Μωϋσέως`, `Τοῦ Ἰώβ`, `Τῆς Χαναναίας`, `Τοῦ Μυριοταλάντου`, `Τοῦ Τελωνοῦ`, `Τοῦ Ἀσώτου`, the prophets, `Τοῦ Δαβὶδ`, `Τοῦ Σαλομῶν`, `Τοῦ Μανασσῆ`). **These are speakers' names — the Publican, the Prodigal, the Canaanite woman, the Debtor of Ten Thousand Talents — and the manuscript displays them.** The 1853 flattens them into the text. For a mirror edition this is a live typographic decision.
- **128. 10 · 136. 4, 18** — cross-references to an **English version in Andrewes' *Works*, vol. vii, pp. 290, 293**. A witness the project has not consulted.
