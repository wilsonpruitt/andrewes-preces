# Part I — The Meditations (ΜΕΛΕΤΑΙ / MEDITATIONES) — English (Wroot Press translation)

Translated from the Greek of the 1853 text (`meditations-transcript.md`), line-keyed page by page to the Greek leaves (even printed pages 252–262); the Latin recto consulted as first witness. Register per `CONVENTIONS.md` §8–10, the Psalter lines crib-checked against the **Coverdale/BCP Psalter** (§9). Two meditations close Part I: **Meditation I, Of the Day of Judgement** (252–259), and **Meditation II, Of the Shortness of Life** (260–263). Both are penitential centos of Scripture; the LXX-vs-AV departures, the BCP agreements, and Andrewes' own paraphrases are flagged at the end.

MEDITATION I.

OF THE DAY OF JUDGEMENT.

O FATHER without beginning, O only-begotten Son,
        O quickening Spirit,
    pitiful, merciful, longsuffering,
        of great mercy, of many tender compassions,
    who lovest the righteous, and hast mercy on sinners,
    who passest over sins, and grantest petitions,
        God of them that repent,
        Saviour of them that sin;
I have sinned before thee, O Lord,
    and thus and thus have I done.
        Ah, ah! alas, alas!
How was I enticed by mine own lust?
    How did I hate instruction?
And I neither reverenced, nor stood in awe of,
        the incomprehensibleness of thy glory,
        the awfulness of thy presence,
        the terribleness of thy power,
        the exactness of thy justice,
        the loveliness of thy goodness.
I will call, if there be any that will answer me;
    to which of the holy angels shall I look?
        O wretched man that I am!
Who shall deliver me from the body of this death?

MEDITATION II.

OF THE SHORTNESS OF LIFE.

HAVE mercy upon me, O Lord, for I am weak;
    Remember what my substance is,
        remember that I am but flesh,
    a wind that passeth away, and cometh not again.
My days are as grass, as a flower of the field,
for a wind hath gone over me, and I shall not be,
    and I shall know my place no more.
        For I am earth and ashes,
        dust and grass,
            flesh and breath,
            corruption and the worm.
As a sojourner upon the earth,
    dwelling in a house of clay,
    of few days and evil,
    to-day and not to-morrow,
    in the morning, and no more until night,
    in a body of sin,
    in an age of corruption,
    short-lived and full of wrath,
    as a flower that flourished and fled away,
    and as a shadow, that in no wise abideth.
Remember this, O Lord, and forbear, forgive;
    for what profit is there in my destruction,

or in my going down into corruption?
For the multitude of thy compassions,
    for the riches, and the exceeding
        overflowing abundance of thy mercies,


For whatsoever is dear to thee, or meet for us to remember,

## Translator's flags

- **The trinitarian opening (printed 252).** *ΠΑΤΕΡ ἄναρχε, Υἱὲ μονογενὴς, Πνεῦμα ζωοποιοῦν* parallels the post-days deprecation's *ΠΑΤΕΡ ὁ κτίσας…* (see `deprecation-english.md`) — the same three-fold address that opens a penitential act. The following titles are Exod. 34:6 / Ps. 103:8 (*οἰκτίρμων, ἐλεήμων, μακρόθυμος, πολυέλεος* — "merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and of great goodness"), rendered in that AV/BCP key. The wordplay *ὁ παράγων ἁμαρτήματα, καὶ παρέχων αἰτήματα* (passing-by sins / granting requests) is kept as an English assonance, "passest over sins … grantest petitions."
- **LXX-vs-AV departures (follow Andrewes, per §9).**
  - **printed 252, Job 5:1.** *Εἰς τίνα ἀγγέλων ἁγίων ὄψομαι;* = "to which of the holy **angels** shall I look?" — LXX *ἀγγέλων ἁγίων*, where AV reads "to which of the **saints** wilt thou turn?" (Heb. *qĕdōšîm*, "holy ones"). Latin *Angelorum sanctorum* confirms.
  - **printed 260, Ps 89:47 (LXX 88:48).** *Μνήσθητι τίς μου ἡ ὑπόστασις* = "Remember what my **substance** is" — LXX/Vulg (*quæ mea substantia*), where AV and BCP both read "remember how **short my time** is."
  - **printed 260, Job 14:1.** *ὀλιγόβιος καὶ πλήρης ὀργῆς* = "short-lived and full of **wrath**" — LXX *ὀργῆς*, where AV reads "full of **trouble**."
- **BCP agreements (Coverdale with the Greek).** **printed 258, Ps 143:2** — *μὴ εἰσέλθῃς εἰς κρίσιν μετὰ τοῦ δούλου σου* = BCP "enter not into judgement with thy servant," verbatim. **printed 256, Ps 44:16** — *ἡ αἰσχύνη τοῦ προσώπου μου ἐκάλυψέ με* = BCP "the shame of my face hath covered me." **printed 260, Ps 103:15–16** — the grass/flower/wind of mortality follows the BCP cadence ("the days of man are but as grass … as soon as the wind goeth over it, it is gone").
- **Andrewes' own paraphrase (printed 260, Ps 30:9).** Andrewes writes *τίς … ὠφέλεια ἐν τῇ ἀπωλείᾳ μου* = "what profit is there in my **destruction**," where both the LXX (*ἐν τῷ αἵματί μου*) and his own Latin recto (*in sanguine meo*) read "in my **blood**." This is Andrewes altering the citation, not a text-critical divergence; the Greek "destruction" is followed, with the "blood" noted here. The next line, *ἐν τῷ καταβαίνειν με εἰς διαφθοράν* ("going down into corruption," LXX *διαφθοράν*), keeps the LXX word behind Acts 2:27.
- **The Judgement catalogue (printed 254–256).** The great asyndetic list of terrors (unbribable Judge, unquenchable fire, the sleepless worm of Mark 9:44, the gulf fixed of Luke 16:26, Hades enlarging his mouth of Isa. 5:14, the fiery stream of Dan. 7:10) is rendered as a chain of bare noun-phrases to preserve the drum-beat; the Latin expands several into relative clauses (*Ignis qui non extinguitur*, *Murus quem transgredi non possumus*) — the terser Greek is followed.
- **The passion / the desire (printed 256).** *τοῦ πάθους* is rendered "of the passion"; the Latin recto glosses it *desiderium carnis meæ*, "the desire of my flesh." The paired brace-lists (*ἀνόητον / ἀνόνητον…* and *ἄτοπον / ἄσχημον / κίβδηλον*) are turned into parallel abstract nouns to keep the two braces visibly matched, with the trailing *ἐπαίσχυντον / ἐπονείδιστον* ("a thing of shame, and of reproach") set below the second brace as in the print (cf. Latin *confusione, opprobrio … dignam*).
- **The close of Meditation II (printed 262).** *Εἰσάκουσον … ἱλάσθητι … πρόσχες … Κύριε, πρόσχες καὶ ποίησον … μὴ χρονίσῃς … ἕνεκά σου* is **Daniel 9:19 verbatim** ("O Lord, hear; O Lord, forgive; O Lord, hearken and do; defer not, for thine own sake, O my God"), and is rendered in the AV wording throughout. *ἱλάσθητί μοι τῷ ἁμαρτωλῶν πρώτῳ* fuses Luke 18:13 with 1 Tim. 1:15 ("chief of sinners"); *κατακαυχάσθω τὸ ἔλεος τῆς κρίσεως* is James 2:13 ("mercy rejoiceth against judgment").
- **Greek-vs-Latin, section titles.** Meditation II's Greek heading *ΠΕΡΙ ΤΗΣ ΤΟΥ ΒΙΟΥ ΒΡΑΧΥΤΗΤΟΣ* ("Of the Shortness of Life") is followed; the Latin recto titles it differently, *FRAGILITATIS HUMANÆ* ("Of Human Frailty"). Likewise Meditation I's *ΠΕΡΙ ΗΜΕΡΑΣ ΤΗΣ ΚΡΙΣΕΩΣ* ("Of the Day of Judgement") vs Latin *JUDICII EXTREMI* ("Of the Last Judgement").
- **Source-scan notes (transcription level, do not affect the English).** Three glyph calls flagged in the transcript recur here only as construal: the two articles at Rom. 7:24 (printed 252, read *τοῦ … τοῦ*), the ink-smudged *αὐτοκατάκριτον* (printed 258, "self-condemned," Lat. *proprio judicio condemnatum*, Tit. 3:11), and the one-word *Διοτιοῦν* (printed 262, = *δι' ὅ τι οὖν*, "for whatsoever cause"). Recheck on a cleaner scan before print.
- **General.** Only the Greek leaves (even printed pages) carry an English block; the Latin recto is the construal-witness throughout. Section-openers set in Greek capitals keep their emphasis in caps (O FATHER, HAVE mercy). Braces and the ` } ` right-brace joins reproduce the 1853 typography.
