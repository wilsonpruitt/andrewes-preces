    For thou hast been my help.

            Summer,
            Autumn,

    BEHOLD, thou wast wroth, for we too have sinned.

    and so the wind carries us away.
And now, O Lord, thou art our Father,

    Be not wroth very sore, and

    And now, behold, look upon us, O Lord,
        we are all thy people.

        forget us not.

Be merciful, O Lord, unto our unrighteousnesses,
    and our sins remember thou no more.
O Lord, I am carnal,
    sold under sin.
In me (that is, in my flesh) dwelleth
    no good thing.
For the good that I would, I do not,
    but the evil which I would not, that I do.
I consent unto the law, that it is good;

    and I delight in it after the inward man.

    warring against the law of my mind,

    O wretched man that I am!
who shall deliver me from the body of this death?
I thank God through JESUS CHRIST.
For where sin abounded, grace did much more

O Lord, thy goodness leadeth me to repentance.

        the snare of the devil, who am taken captive by
        him.
The time past of my life may suffice me
    to have wrought the will of my lusts,
    walking in lasciviousness, revellings, drinkings,
    and in the rest of the excess of riot.
        O Lamb, without blemish and without spot,
who hast redeemed me with thy precious blood,
    by that same blood have mercy on me and save me;
        both by the blood,
        and by that thy name,
    beside which there is none other under heaven
given among men,
        whereby we must be saved.
O God, thou knowest my foolishness,
    and my sins are not hid from thee.
O Lord, all my desire is before thee,
    and my groaning is not hid from thee.
Let not them that wait on thee be ashamed for my sake,

O Lord, O Lord of hosts;
Neither let those that seek thee be confounded for my sake,
    O God of Israel.
Save me out of the mire, that I sink not;
    let me be delivered from them that hate me, and
        out of the deep waters.
Let not the waterflood overflow me,
        neither let the deep swallow me up,
        and let not the pit shut her mouth upon me.

Vainglory, | the Amorite,
Envy, | the Hittite,
Wrath, | the Perizzite,
Surfeiting, | the Girgashite,
Lasciviousness, | the Hivite,
the cares of this life, | the Canaanite,

    Mercy,
    Patience,
    Temperance,
    Chastity,
    contentment,
    the readiness of diligence.

        I BELIEVE:
In the Father, a natural affection kindly-disposed,
    the Almighty, a saving power,

the Creator, a providence unto the { keeping / governing / perfecting } of all.

In JESUS, salvation,
    in Christ, an anointing,
    the only-begotten Son, adoption,

In his conceiving and birth { a cleansing / of the unclean }

in death, the sting of death

But I have trusted in thy mercy,
    from everlasting to everlasting.
        how hast thou multiplied thy mercy, O God!

    let me not be ashamed of this

    FURTHERMORE we beseech thee,
Remember, O Lord, all men for good,
    have mercy upon all, O Master,
    be reconciled unto us all.
Give peace to the multitudes of thy people,
    scatter the offences,
    make the wars to cease,

    Thine own peace and love
grant thou unto us, O God, our Saviour,

Remember the crown of the year

Thou openest thine hand,

## Translator's flags

- **p.106 "in the morning watches"** (Ps 63:6–7) — Andrewes' Greek ἐν τοῖς ὄρθροις = "at dawn / in the morning watches"; the AV reads "in the night watches." ὄρθρος is unambiguously daybreak; followed the Greek (this is a morning office).
- **p.106 "as a menstruous rag"** (Isa 64:6) — Andrewes' LXX ὡς ῥάκος ἀποκαθημένης = "as the rag of a woman in her separation"; the AV euphemises "as filthy rags." Followed the explicit Greek per §9.
- **p.108 Isaiah 64 cento** — "faded as a leaf … the wind carries us away" (ἐξερρύημεν ὡς φύλλα), "we are the clay, the work of thy hands," "we are all thy people": the confession runs on the LXX of Isa 64:6–9.
- **p.108 "O thou our hope … as one native-born that turneth aside to a lodging"** (Jer 14:8–9) — three Greek departures from the AV, all followed: ὑπομονὴ ἡμῶν "our hope/endurance" for LXX ὑπομονὴ Ἰσραήλ; αὐτόχθων "one native to the soil" where the AV renders the Hebrew "wayfaring man"; ἐκκλίνων εἰς κατάλυμα "turning aside to a lodging."
- **p.108–110 Romans 7 cento** — "carnal, sold under sin" (7:14), "in my flesh dwelleth no good thing" (7:18), "the good that I would, I do not" (7:19), "I consent unto the law" (7:16), "O wretched man that I am" (7:24); AV wording echoed where the Greek matches. "grace did much more abound" (Rom 5:20).
- **p.112 the two columns** — LEFT = the passions to be driven out (Τύφος "vainglory," Ἀσέλγεια "lasciviousness," τὸ χλιαρὸν τῆς ἀκηδίας "the lukewarmness of sloth/accidie"); RIGHT = the seven nations of Canaan (Deut 7:1: Amorite … Jebusite). The passions are set against the peoples to be dispossessed. Ἐθαῖος = the Hittite (LXX more usually Χετταῖος).
- **p.114 the ΠΙΣΤΕΥΩ / CREDO catalogue** — the Apostles' Creed re-read as a list of benefits: each article named for what it gives (salvation, anointing, adoption; a cleansing of our unclean conceiving and birth; the curse, the sting, and corruption "done away," ἀνῃρημένην, per Gal 3:13 / 1 Cor 15:55–56; a power from on high "transforming unto holiness"). **"the Lord, a tending"** — θεραπείαν (Lat. *curam herilem*, "a master's/household care"); θεραπεία carries both "attendance/service" and "healing," rendered "a tending" to hold the pair.
- **p.116 "fillest every living thing with thy good pleasure"** (Ps 145:16) — Andrewes' εὐδοκίας σῆς = "of thy good pleasure / favour"; the AV reads "satisfiest the desire of every living thing." Followed the Greek.
- **p.122–124 the great intercession** — mostly bare imperatives, rendered as a litany: "with them that sail, sail thou; with them that journey, journey thou" keeps the compact Greek compounds (σύμπλευσον / συνόδευσον). "storehouses" = τὰ ταμεῖα (Ps 144:13, AV "garners").
- **p.126 the "Be thou, O Lord" prayer** (Ἴσθι Κύριε) — Andrewes' celebrated brace-prayer, each preposition (within / without / above / beneath / before / behind / round about) paired with its purpose (strengthen / keep / shelter / support / direct / bring back / fortify) me. Rendered as the printed brace-table; the shared "me, that thou mayest … me" runs once through the centre.
- **p.128 the closing doxology** (1 Chron 29:10–13) — echoes the AV "thine is the greatness, and the power, and the glory, and the victory"; ἐξομολόγησις rendered "praise" (Lat. *laus*), ἰσχὺς "might" (Lat. *potestas*); "praise thy glorious name" = τὸ ὄνομα τῆς καυχήσεώς σου.

### Transcription flags carried from `day4-transcript.md`

- **p.114 οὗ / οὐ** (Ἐν καταβάσει, οὗ ἡμᾶς χρῆ) — the relative is unclear in the scan; read as οὗ "whither," on the parallel with the preceding οἷς ἡμᾶς ἐχρῆ and the Latin *quo nos oportuit*.
- **p.124 καταποντισμοῦ** — the final glyph is ambiguous (‑οῦ / ‑οῖ); read as the genitive καταποντισμοῦ to match the genitive series (λοιμοῦ, λιμοῦ, σεισμοῦ) and the Latin *diluvio*.
- **Latin p.111 "Rom. v. 2."** — for *superabundavit gratia* (scripturally Rom 5:20) the 1853 prints "Rom. v. 2."; left verbatim.
