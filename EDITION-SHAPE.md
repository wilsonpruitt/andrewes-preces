# What kind of edition is this? — the decision memo

**Status: WILSON'S CALL, NOT YET MADE.** Written 2026-08-04, after the *Variæ Lectiones* apparatus pass. Nothing in this file has been applied. It exists so the ruling is recorded once and a later, cheaper session can execute against it without re-deriving the argument.

---

## 1. The question, and why it only just arose

The project has never had to say whether it is producing **a faithful reprint of the 1853 Parker edition** or **a critical text of Andrewes**, because until the apparatus pass the two coincided. Two findings ended that:

1. **The 1853 dropped Hebrew that stands in Andrewes' own text.** Class A is by definition *variæ lectiones quæ in textu ipso occurrunt*, held *pro veris*. Hebrew stands at printed 38 · 76 · 86–88 · 92 · 96 · 152 · 192 · 198 · 200 · 202 · 210 · 212 — including the rabbinic **`יצר טוב`** at 76, **Genesis i. 2's *tohu wa-bohu*** at 92, a **seven-word Greek–Hebrew lexicon of sin** at 96, and **Jonah ii. 7 replacing the Greek outright** at 152. Full table: `apparatus/CLASS-A-ledger.md` §2.
2. **Three blank leaves after printed 184** — six blank pages, the largest silence in the manuscript — fall **mid-`day7`**, immediately after Rev. xxi. 4 and immediately before the great personal intercession at 186 **which our own transcript note already cross-references out of Day 7**. The manuscript agrees with the note and disagrees with the file. Detail: `apparatus/CLASS-B-unit-boundaries.md` §2.

## 2. The reframing that dissolves the either/or

**The transcripts are not the edition. They are the record of the 1853 plate.**

Nothing obliges one book to be derived from them. The real question is *how many books we derive, and what each one claims*. This is the shape of the answer, and it is what makes a two-book plan coherent rather than indecisive.

**Invariant under every option below:** `part1/`–`part3/` stay a faithful record of the plate, and **class A is never applied to them**. The discipline that has held for 436 printed pages — wrong references and printing defects kept as printed, flagged, never silently mended — is not up for revision here.

## 3. The fact that shrinks the question

The apograph runs *usque ad pag. 250*. **Class A cites printed 6–242; class B cites printed 2–250.**

Therefore **everything in dispute lies in printed 2–250** — a bit over half of Part I, and **none of Part II (267–395) or Part III (398–436)**. Those 170 printed pages are reproduced identically under every option. This is a decision about roughly 250 of the volume's 436 printed pages.

Class B is also silent about the seven-day boundaries, and that silence is **not** evidence against them: preface principle **iv** marks each day by its own **astronomical sign**, a different device. The day cuts at printed 30 · 70 · 92 · 106 · 130 · 148 · 168 stand. The deprecation/evening cut at 218–220 is confirmed outright.

## 5. The options

### Option 1 — One book: the mirror, faithfully
Prototype B as chosen: Greek verso / Latin recto page-for-page, English as italic register at the spread foot, apparatus at the back. Class A's Hebrew, the three blank leaves, the withdrawn conjectures at 100.10 / 104.6 / 242.6, the MS's display hierarchy of speakers' names — all argued in the apparatus, none applied.

*Cheapest, most defensible, one finishing effort.*
*Cost:* the Hebrew lexicon of sin at 96 and the *yetzer tov* at 76 — a substantive theological finding about the *Preces* — appear only as a back-matter table in a book few will buy to read.

### Option 2 — Two books *(Wilson's proposal; recommended)*
**Book A** is exactly option 1, unchanged. **Book B** is an **English devotional** derived from the same corpus: cut to the *manuscript's* divisions, Hebrew restored where class A puts it, laid out on class B's spacing record, every editorial move visibly noted.

The cost objection to a critical text evaporates here. Re-cutting is expensive *because it re-cuts line-keyed English and breaks the mirror's page-fit* — **and Book B has neither constraint.** The transcripts never move.

*Risk:* Book B is the easier book. If it ships first the mirror may never ship.

### Option 3 — An actual critical text
**Out of reach, and for a substantive reason, not a scheduling one.** Class A is the 1853 editor's *report* of Wright's apograph — a copy of Andrewes' book, read by a Victorian, printed. We would be emending from a report of a copy. Class A and class C also collide at nine lines and the direction of Drake's `]` cannot be settled from the printed apparatus (`CLASS-A-ledger.md` §4). A critical text needs the **Pembroke College manuscript** in hand.

**Option 2 does not foreclose option 3.** That is the strongest argument for option 2.

**One shared blocker gates both books: a non-Google digitization.** Worth going to get regardless of the ruling.

Book A's real risk is **page-fit**, and it is not small. Book B's real risk is **editorial drift** — see §8.

## 7. Recommendation

**Option 2, mirror first.**

1. **The mirror's lane is empty**, so it cannot be scooped. No urgency; do it properly.
2. **The devotional's lane is contested and moved in 2023**, so it must differentiate hard — and **all four of its differentiators are products of doing the scholarly work first**: a fresh translation not descended from Newman; completeness (Parts II–III); the manuscript's divisions; class B's layout. **Ship the devotional first and you ship it without them.**
3. Every editorial call and the whole introduction get settled once, by Book A, and Book B inherits them. Backwards, you make every call twice.

**Escape hatch:** if page-fit proves intractable, flip the order rather than stall. Do not let the mirror's hardest problem hold the catalogue hostage.

## 8. If option 2 is chosen — the discipline Book B must not lose

Book B is where the project is most likely to betray itself, because **a devotional reader is exactly the reader who cannot check you.**

- Every division moved from the plate's order and every Hebrew word restored gets a **visible note**. No silent improvement, ever. Same rule as the transcripts, applied to a book whose readers won't have the plate.
- The Hebrew restoration **cannot proceed before the 600-dpi read** (`CLASS-A-ledger.md` §2 note 4). Printing a misread Hebrew word in a script the reader cannot check is the worst failure available to this edition — cf. the garbled `חזתלת` for `תוחלת` at printed 386, which the plate itself commits.
- **Class A is never applied to `part1/`–`part3/`.** Book B is *derived*; it does not write back.
- The MS's own numbering at 204.17–23 runs `2, 1, 4, 3, 5, 6, 7` — not in sequence. And the numerals at printed 202 **are not in the manuscript at all**; the 1853 editor took them from an English version. Anything Book B infers from that numbering is inference from an editor's scaffolding.
- Say plainly on the copyright page what Book B is: not a translation of the 1853's text-as-printed, but a reading text on the manuscript's divisions, with the departures listed.
